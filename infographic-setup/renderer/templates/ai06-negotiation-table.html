You don't beat an increase by refusing it. You answer the reason underneath it — and "raw cost" and "our margin" need completely different answers.
Their argument
Your counter + the test question
"Raw material costs went up."
↔
Index it. Most commodities are public, so show the actual move, not the claim.Ask:Which index, and over exactly what period?
"Our labour and wage costs rose."
↔
Labour is a fraction of unit cost. Size it before you accept it.Ask:What share of this part's cost is labour?
"Margin pressure across the board."
↔
Hardest to counterTheir margin isn't yours to fund. Anchor to a should-cost, not their P&L.Ask:What's the should-cost build for this part?
"Low volume makes it uneconomic."
↔
Then price the volume tier, or commit volume for a held price.Ask:What volume holds today's price?
