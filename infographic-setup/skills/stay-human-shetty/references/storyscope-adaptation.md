# StoryScope Adaptation Boundary

## Source

Charlie Hills' `stay-human` skill applies findings from:

- Jenna Russell, Rishanth Rajendhran, Chau Minh Pham, Mohit Iyyer, and John Wieting, `StoryScope: Investigating idiosyncrasies in AI fiction`, arXiv:2604.03136, version 4, 2026.

The study analyzes 61,608 long-form stories generated from 10,272 prompts. Discourse-level narrative features achieved 93.2% macro-F1 for human-versus-AI detection. The paper reports that AI stories tend toward over-explained themes, tidy single-track plots, less moral ambiguity, lower temporal complexity, and model-specific narrative signatures.

## Correct Use Here

The evidence concerns fiction of roughly 5,000 words, not LinkedIn supply-chain captions. Do not claim that the same detector accuracy applies to professional social writing.

Use the findings as a structural editing lens:

- remove redundant moral statements;
- resist perfectly tidy argument shapes;
- vary entry points and temporal order when clarity permits;
- preserve unresolved operational trade-offs;
- judge structure separately from vocabulary.

Add Shetty's Desk-specific checks the paper does not test:

- repetition across a creator's recent captions;
- captions that narrate the visual instead of adding value;
- hooks that vary cosmetically but not structurally;
- invented personal authority;
- repetitive `problem -> list -> moral -> question` architecture.

The target is authentic professional reasoning, not evasion of an AI detector.
