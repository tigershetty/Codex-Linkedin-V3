# Stay Human - Shetty's Desk

Use structural editing, not synonym replacement. Preserve the author's argument and evidence while changing the shape that makes the draft feel generated.

## Required Context

Read these before editing:

1. `tiger-voice.md`
2. the post's research brief and approved visual
3. `references/published-voice.md` when available
4. [references/storyscope-adaptation.md](references/storyscope-adaptation.md) for the research boundary

Never invent a lived anecdote, operating result, quotation, or statistic to make writing feel human.

## Workflow

### 1. Extract The Human Spine

Write four private notes before rewriting:

- the useful professional truth;
- the concrete scene or consequence that makes it matter;
- Tiger's actual judgment;
- the tension that should remain open rather than resolve neatly.

If any note is missing, improve the thinking before polishing the prose.

### 2. Quote Structural Flags

Inspect the draft and quote the exact lines that trigger each applicable flag:

| Flag | What to catch |
|---|---|
| Explained moral | A final sentence restates what the whole post already proved. |
| Single-track neatness | Every paragraph advances one tidy argument and all uncertainty disappears. |
| Linear explainer | Definition, five bullets, summary, question with no scene, jump, or complication. |
| Visual echo | The caption reads the image back to the reader instead of adding context or consequence. |
| Template footprint | The same opener, `This is where`, `For me`, bullet count, or closing question used in recent posts. |
| Cosmetic hook variety | Ten hooks share the same thesis and sentence architecture under different labels. |
| Unearned authority | A personal claim or certainty is not supported by experience, evidence, or careful framing. |
| Flattened rhythm | Paragraphs have uniform length, repeated transitions, or stacked short fragments. |
| Too-complete resolution | The method appears to solve every case and hides the remaining trade-off. |

Score each `clear`, `mild`, or `none`.

### 3. Change The Shape

Apply only the changes the draft needs:

- Enter through a meeting moment, downstream consequence, contradiction, or artifact instead of always stating the thesis first.
- Move one beat out of strict explanatory order when clarity survives. A downstream scene can appear before the upstream cause.
- Let one complication remain: ownership varies, evidence can conflict, or a decision can still be genuinely hard.
- Delete the sentence that explains the moral after the reader already understands it.
- Make the caption add what the visual cannot: operating context, judgment, failure mode, or practical use.
- Vary paragraph length naturally. Keep Tiger's connected reasoning and avoid dramatic fragment stacks.
- Use bullets only when they create a reusable operating reference.
- End with a question a practitioner can answer from experience, not agreement bait.

### 4. Engineer Ten Structurally Different Hooks

Use ten different entry architectures. Do not publish category labels with the hooks.

1. recognizable scene;
2. downstream consequence;
3. diagnostic question;
4. counterfactual or reversal;
5. artifact or operating rule;
6. unresolved tension;
7. meeting language someone could actually hear;
8. precise distinction;
9. personal reflection grounded in the topic;
10. concise contrarian claim earned by the caption.

Reject a set when more than three hooks could be produced by swapping only the topic noun.

### 5. Final Checks

- Verify every load-bearing claim against the research file.
- Check the caption does not merely enumerate the visual.
- Compare its opening, transition pattern, bullet structure, and close with the last three approved captions.
- Remove repeated creator phrases unless they are genuinely the most natural wording.
- Prefer precise nouns and consequences over adjectives about importance.
- Keep the final caption concise enough that every paragraph earns its place.

## Output Format

```md
STRUCTURAL FLAGS
- [flag] `[clear|mild|none]`: "quoted line" -> reason

REWRITE STRATEGY
[two or three sentences]

HOOK OPTIONS
1. ...
...
10. ...

FINAL CAPTION
[caption]

WHAT CHANGED
- [one line on structure]
- [one line on precision/value]
```

Do not replace Tiger's voice with the editor's voice. The goal is recognizably human thinking with less template residue, not artificial roughness.
