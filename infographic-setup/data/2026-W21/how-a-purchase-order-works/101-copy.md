## LinkedIn Caption

A PO is not a document. It is a legal commitment, a finance record, and a supplier instruction all at once.

Treating it as a form to fill in is how approvals get missed, invoices get disputed, and payments run 60 days late.

This is Episode 26 of Supply Chain 101. In Episode 25 we started the Procurement month with the supplier scorecard. Today we follow the purchase order from the moment a need is identified to the moment the invoice is paid, because most of the friction in procurement-finance relationships lives somewhere in these seven steps.

And it moves through seven hands before it closes:

• Requisition: someone in the business identifies a need and raises a formal request. This is where the process starts and where most ad-hoc buying bypasses the system entirely.

• Approval: the requisition goes through an authorisation workflow. The delay here is almost always process, not urgency. Most approvals take longer than the actual purchase.

• PO creation: procurement converts the approved requisition into a legal purchase order and sends it to the supplier. At this point the company has made a financial commitment.

• Supplier confirmation: the supplier acknowledges receipt and confirms they can fulfil the order. Without this, you have a commitment with no confirmation it will be met.

• Goods receipt: the warehouse records what was actually received. This step is the one most often done late, informally, or not at all, and it is what makes the next step possible.

• Invoice matching: finance matches the supplier invoice against the PO and the goods receipt note. This is the three-way match, and it is where most payment delays originate. If goods receipt was never recorded, the match cannot close.

• Payment: the invoice is approved and payment is released to the supplier.

The gap between goods receipt and invoice matching is where most SC-finance friction lives. Not because the process is wrong, but because two teams own adjacent steps and rarely communicate when one step is late.

How many of these seven steps does your team own end-to-end?
