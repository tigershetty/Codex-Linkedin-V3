## LinkedIn Caption

You don't need 50 metrics, you need 10 that map to SCOR's 5 performance attributes. Two per attribute - one for the boardroom, one for the floor.

Most SC dashboards have the inverse: hundreds of operational numbers and almost nothing the CEO can actually use.

This is Episode 23 of Supply Chain 101. In Episode 8 we covered SCOR as the universal supply chain language. Today we're using its five performance attributes as the structure for cutting a bloated KPI list down to the ten that actually matter.

The five SCOR attributes are: Reliability, Responsiveness, Agility, Cost, and Asset Management. For each attribute, you need two KPIs: one strategic (the boardroom number that tells leadership if you're healthy), and one operational (the floor number that tells the team what to act on today).

- Reliability:
Strategic: Perfect Order Rate (% of orders delivered complete, on time, undamaged, with correct documentation)
Operational: On-Time Delivery (% of orders shipped by promised date)

- Responsiveness:
Strategic: Order Fulfilment Cycle Time (average time from order to delivery)
Operational: Manufacturing Lead Time (production cycle time per unit)

- Agility:
Strategic: Upside Supply Chain Flexibility (% volume increase deliverable in 30 days)
Operational: Forecast Accuracy (forecast vs. actual at SKU level)

- Cost:
Strategic: Total Supply Chain Cost as % of Revenue
Operational: Cost per Order Shipped (or per unit)

- Asset Management:
Strategic: Cash-to-Cash Cycle Time (your CCC from Episode 22)
Operational: Inventory Turns (revenue / average inventory)

The mistake isn't picking the wrong metrics. It's reporting strategic metrics to the warehouse and operational metrics to the board. Both audiences need different numbers because they're solving different problems. Two scorecards, not one bigger one.

Which of these ten does your organisation track today, and how many of them actually shape decisions?
