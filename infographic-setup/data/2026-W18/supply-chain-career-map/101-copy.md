## LinkedIn Caption

You can be ten years deep into supply chain and still not know which career you're actually building?

A lot of paths share the same three letters at the entry level, they diverge fast after that.

This is Episode 24 of Supply Chain 101. Over the past 23 episodes we've covered the language, the mechanics, and the strategy of supply chain. Today we're closing with the question almost everyone in the field eventually asks: where can I actually go from here?

Supply chain is not a single career path. It's at least six, branching from a common entry point into completely different executive destinations. Each path requires a different mix of skills, attracts a different temperament, and leads somewhere recognisably distinct.

• Procurement: deep in supplier relationships, negotiation, total cost analysis, category strategy. Path leads to CPO or VP of Sourcing.

• Planning and S&OP: forecasting, demand-supply balancing, capacity allocation, integrated business planning. Path leads to VP of Supply Chain or Chief Supply Chain Officer.

• Logistics: warehousing, transportation, distribution, network design, 3PL/4PL management. Path leads to Head of Operations or VP of Logistics.

• Manufacturing: production, quality, lean, capacity, plant management. Path leads to COO or VP of Operations.

• Analytics and digital: forecasting models, optimisation, control towers, data infrastructure, AI applications. Path leads to Chief Data Officer or Chief Digital Officer.

• Strategy and consulting: network design, transformation, M&A integration, advisory. Path leads to Chief Strategy Officer, partner-track consulting, or independent advisory.

What you need to consider is that the highest-impact supply chain leaders are T-shaped, not I-shaped. They go deep in one path, but they're literate across all six. They can speak procurement to a CPO, planning to a CSCO, and analytics to a CDO without translation. That breadth is what gets them invited into rooms specialists never reach.

If you've followed the series from the start, you've now seen the language, the mechanics, and the strategy. The career map is what ties them together. Each of the 23 concepts we covered maps more naturally to one or two of these six paths than the others.

Which paths spoke to you?

Which path are you on, and which adjacent path would make you the most valuable version of yourself in five years?
