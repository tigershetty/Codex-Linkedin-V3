## Narrative Summary — Variant A (Paradox-Led)

Global supply chains spent 30 years eliminating every buffer in the system. Safety stock. Excess capacity. Redundant suppliers. The result: 39 kilometres of water between Oman and Iran, and the insurance market's 48-hour decision to stop pricing it, now controls whether manufacturing inputs, fertilizer, and energy costs cascade into Q2. The chokepoint was always there. The supply chain with no buffer to absorb it is new.

### The 39km That Controls 20% of Everything
- 20 million barrels per day flow through Hormuz — equal to 20% of global petroleum liquids — a single geographic node built into the cost base of every downstream manufacturer without ever appearing on a risk register.
- One-fifth of global LNG moves through the same 39km; Qatar alone ships 9.3 Bcf per day from inside the Gulf, with one inland pipeline offering 2.6 Bcf of bypass — the rest goes through Hormuz or not at all.
- The escape route covers only 13%: Saudi Arabia and UAE combined can reroute 2.6 million b/d via inland pipelines, leaving 14 million barrels per day with no physical alternative to the strait.

### How Insurance Closed the Strait Before the Military Did
- 70% of Hormuz traffic stopped within 48 hours of the February 28 strikes, before any blockade was declared — driven entirely by insurance market withdrawal, not military action.
- War-risk premiums doubled in a single session: a $150 million containership now carries a single-transit premium of $750,000, up from $375,000, pushing annualised insurance costs to $150–200 million per vessel.
- 170 containerships carrying 450,000 TEUs remain stranded inside the strait; CMA CGM issued an Emergency Conflict Surcharge of $3,000 per 40-ft container on all Gulf-touching routes within 24 hours.

### The 90-Day Cascade Your Risk Model Has Not Mapped
- **39km**: the width of the waterway simultaneously disrupting four supply chain layers at once — oil pricing, LNG supply, petrochemical feedstocks, and one-third of global fertilizer trade.
- Iran's 100-million-tonne annual petrochemical capacity is feeding a 15–25% feedstock cost spike in plastics, adhesives, and specialty chemicals — costs arriving in Q2 with no Hormuz line in most manufacturing risk models.
- One-third of global fertilizer trade is stranded with spring planting beginning in six weeks; McKinsey's 2025 survey found most supply chain leaders see risk only to tier-1 vendors — the petrochemical and fertilizer cascades live entirely in tier 2 and tier 3.
