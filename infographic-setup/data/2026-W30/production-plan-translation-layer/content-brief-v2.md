## 4. Research Thesis

**Non-obvious thesis:** production planning is not just "making a schedule"; it is the constraint translation step that determines whether demand can become a build commitment.  
**Counterargument / caveat:** the production plan is not the same as detailed scheduling; a good post needs to separate the planning commitment from the line-level schedule.  
**What people get wrong:** they treat the demand plan, production plan, and factory schedule as one continuous number, when each step changes the level of commitment and operational detail.  
**Meeting-room use:** use before a production planning review, S&OP handoff, or commercial change request to ask which constraint changes the plan first.

## 5. Content Shape

**Hook direction:** Comparison-Gap / Meeting Moment  
**Opening line candidate:** The demand plan says what the business wants; the production plan says what the factory can actually build.  
**Core explanation:** demand enters as volume, mix, timing, and promise pressure; production planning checks capacity, materials, labor, line rules, and changeover impact; the result becomes a build commitment that later turns into a detailed factory schedule.  
**Reusable takeaway:** a demand-to-build translation layer that separates demand input, constraint check, production commitment, detailed schedule, and feedback.  
**CTA question:** When demand changes, which constraint changes the plan first?
