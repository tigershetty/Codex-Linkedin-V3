## 1. Image Intelligence

**Power format:** PF8 anatomy + PF6 decision test/tree + AI workflow card  
**Eye path:** start with a bold two-tone title, read the variance dashboard on the left, cross through a constraint bridge in the center, compare sequence options on the right, then land on the human verification strip  
**Hero element:** a premium demand-to-sequence operating board on a luminous planning table, supported by small factory/warehouse props and a restrained Claude workflow chip  
**Density level:** dense-saveable  
**Repeated cell schema:** avoid generic equal cards; each zone needs a distinct visual logic: variance chart, root-cause notes, constraint bridge, changeover risk matrix, sequence lanes, verification checklist  
**Proof device:** role-specific workflow detail and human verification; no external statistic needed  
**Save mechanism:** a planner or S&OP analyst can save it as a template for demand review preparation

## 2. Caption Intelligence

**Opening move:** role pain / meeting-room limitation / artifact promise  
**Audience promise:** show how to move from variance explanation to a sequence decision the factory can actually review  
**Authority signal:** practical planning language: forecast vs actual, product family, changeover, constraints, ERP/MES, material availability, frozen window, customer promise  
**Value artifact:** dashboard / decision board / verification checklist  
**CTA/save trigger:** ask whether the review shows which production decision changes first

## 3. Adaptation To Shetty's Desk

**What to borrow:** Ref 13's forecast-to-decision boundary, Ref 39's S&OP alignment logic, Ref 51's AI workflow usefulness, Ref 70's Claude-as-planning-tool cue, and Ref 89's demand-planning depth  
**What to avoid copying:** one more generic dashboard, fake optimizer UI, exaggerated AI tool branding, or an image where the sequence lanes look decorative but do not connect to the demand miss  
**Supply-chain translation:** the demand miss becomes useful when it is translated into family impact, constraint risk, and sequence choices that a planner can validate  
**Tiger-specific angle:** AI should reduce planning confusion by structuring the decision, not by pretending the messy factory schedule can be solved from one prompt  
**Best-fit calendar topics:** Demand review dashboard; sequence to cut changeover; S&OP demand review narrative; production plan translation

## 4. Prompt Inputs

**Reference lesson for GPT Image 2:** create a high-end operating artifact where the dashboard and sequence board are physically connected, so the reader can see the handoff from demand review to factory decision  
**Visual mechanics to borrow:** two-tone title, left-to-right workflow spine, mixed dashboard anatomy, decision-tree/sequence lanes, callout rails, verification checklist, and meaningful labeled icons instead of decorative logos  
**Reference lesson for caption:** lead with the meeting-room gap, explain what Claude can structure, then state exactly where human planner validation is required  
**Reference lesson for topic selection:** AI-for-SC posts are strongest when they show a concrete task transformation rather than a broad claim that AI saves time
