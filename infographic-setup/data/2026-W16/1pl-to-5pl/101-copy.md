## LinkedIn Caption

A 1PL owns every truck and every warehouse whereas a 5PL owns nothing and manages everything. The further up the ladder you go, the less you physically control and the more you strategically coordinate.

This is Episode 16 of Supply Chain 101. We've covered how supply chains buy, hold inventory, manage cost, and classify complexity. Today we're closing with how goods actually move, and specifically how companies decide who moves them.

There are five levels of logistics outsourcing, and each one represents a different trade-off between control and capability:
- 1PL: you do everything yourself. Own trucks, own warehouses, own drivers. Think of a local brewery delivering its own beer. Maximum control, but it only scales if logistics is your core business.
- 2PL: you outsource transport to a carrier or freight forwarder. They move your goods, but you manage the rest. This is the most basic form of outsourcing.
- 3PL: you outsource warehousing, transport, and fulfilment to a logistics provider. They handle operations on your behalf. This is where most mid-to-large companies sit today, and it's the level most people mean when they say "we use a logistics partner."
- 4PL: you bring in a partner that doesn't just operate. They manage your other logistics providers, optimise routes, and coordinate across the network. A 4PL is a strategist, not a truck operator.
- 5PL: the entire logistics function is managed as a platform or network service. Think large e-commerce platforms that orchestrate thousands of carriers, warehouses, and fulfilment centres through technology. This is logistics as a managed ecosystem.

The decision point isn't which level sounds most impressive. It's when your complexity outgrows your team's ability to coordinate, and that's the signal to move up.

What level does your logistics operation actually require?
