## LinkedIn Caption

Offshoring saves money on labour. Nearshoring saves money on speed. Reshoring saves money on control. All three are true and all three are incomplete. The real comparison isn't unit cost, it is the total landed cost, and most companies miss the whole truth

This is Episode 18 of Supply Chain 101. In Episode 17 we covered demand planning, sensing, and shaping. Today we're looking at one of the most debated strategic decisions in modern supply chain: where should you manufacture?

There are three options, and each one optimises for something different:
- Offshoring means manufacturing far from your end market, typically in low-cost countries. The advantage is lower unit cost. The trade-offs are longer lead times, higher inventory requirements, shipping costs, customs complexity, and less direct oversight of quality and operations.

- Nearshoring means manufacturing closer to your end market, though not necessarily in the same country. Think Mexico for US companies, or Turkey and Eastern Europe for Western European companies. The advantage is shorter lead times and easier coordination. The trade-off is higher unit cost than offshoring, though not always by as much as people assume.

- Reshoring means bringing manufacturing back to your home market. The advantage is maximum control, minimal lead time, and the simplest supply chain. The trade-off is typically the highest unit cost, though automation is closing that gap in some industries.

Here's the thing most people miss: the comparison should never be unit cost alone.

Total landed cost includes freight, duties, inventory carrying costs, quality failure rates, lead time buffers, management overhead, and the opportunity cost of slow response. When you add those factors, offshoring's advantage often shrinks significantly, and in some cases flips entirely.

Has your org run a total landed cost comparison, or is the decision still based on the number on the supplier quote?
