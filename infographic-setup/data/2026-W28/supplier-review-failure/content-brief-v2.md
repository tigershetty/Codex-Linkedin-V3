## 4. Research Thesis

**Non-obvious thesis:** A supplier review that produces no behavioral change is not a review. It is a reporting ceremony.  
**Counterargument / caveat:** Scorecards still matter, but only when tied to an improvement plan, owner, due date, and commercial consequence.  
**What people get wrong:** They think the score is the output. The real output is the agreed action.  
**Meeting-room use:** A manager can use this to redesign QBRs around decisions instead of presentations.

## 5. Content Shape

**Hook direction:** Contrarian / Failure-Mode  
**Opening line candidate:** If your supplier review ends with a score and no changed behavior, it was not a review. It was a presentation.  
**Core explanation:** Performance review should be a loop: measure, discuss, agree action, assign owner, track consequence.  
**Reusable takeaway:** The 5-part review loop: metric, cause, owner, action, next checkpoint.  
**CTA question:** In your last supplier review, what actually changed after the meeting ended?
