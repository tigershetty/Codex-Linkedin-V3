## Caption

Supplier concentration risk is not just about how much you spend. It is about how hard a supplier is to replace.

That distinction matters because a supplier can look small in the spend file and still be operationally critical. Maybe they make a component that only fits one production line. Maybe the drawing, tooling, qualification, or approval process sits with them. Or maybe there is an alternative supplier on paper, but nobody has tested whether that supplier can actually deliver when demand moves.

This is where supplier count can give a false sense of comfort. You may have a large vendor master and still have a few relationships carrying most of the real dependency.

The practical test is simple:

• Does this supplier support a critical part, process, or customer promise?

• Do we have a qualified alternative that can take volume without months of onboarding?

• If the supplier failed, how long would it take before the business feels it?

My view on this is that procurement teams should not only ask, "Who are our biggest suppliers?" They should also ask, "Who are the suppliers we would struggle to replace in the next 30 days?"

Because those are not always the same names.

And once you can name the three suppliers that could stop the business, the conversation becomes much more useful. You can decide where to dual-source, where to hold inventory, where to build technical alternatives, and where leadership needs visibility before the problem becomes urgent.

Which supplier in your current base would be hardest to replace in the next 30 days?
