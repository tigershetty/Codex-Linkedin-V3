**Topic type**: Company Strategic Pivot

**Hook candidate**:
"IKEA makes 70% of Europe's products in Europe and 80% of Asia's products in Asia. For the U.S., that number is 15% — and tariffs just made the gap a €26B problem."

**Why this is the recommended topic (user-directed + Company Strategic Pivot criteria)**:
IKEA is the single most instructive supply chain story available this week: a company whose ENTIRE competitive moat is supply chain design (flat-pack = 10x shipping efficiency, global scale = unit cost leadership) is now being forced by tariffs to abandon the global model that created the moat in the first place. The mechanism is counter-intuitive and universally transferable: flat-pack economics require massive production runs — the scale that makes KALLAX cheap in China cannot be replicated in a North Carolina factory at 2M pieces/year. Every VP Supply Chain managing import-dependent categories faces a version of this exact trade-off.

**The Strategic Narrative (5-zone arc)**:

Zone A — PRE-PIVOT STATE:
"IKEA's Global Scale Moat"
IKEA designed products around the supply chain, not the reverse. Flat-pack reduces product volume by 50-75%, enabling 10x more products per shipping container. This requires massive production runs at a handful of global factories to hit unit cost targets. Result: 15% of US products made in US (vs 70% in Europe, 80% in Asia). Revenue: €41.5B (Ingka Group FY25). Affordability as competitive identity.

Zone B — TRIGGER MECHANISM (CENTER/PRIMARY):
"The Tariff Math That Broke the Global Model"
Trump tariffs (2025): 25-50% on upholstered furniture, kitchen cabinets, mattresses from China/Vietnam. Red Sea disruptions: 49% reduction in Suez vessel transits = extended lead times + freight spikes. Section 122 (Feb 24, 2026): 10% flat non-discriminatory rate — India, Vietnam, Mexico, China all taxed equally. The China+1 arbitrage that was supposed to solve the problem is now also taxed. IKEA's response at Davos 2025: warning of "looming price increases."

Zone C — STRUCTURAL MOVE:
"The Re-Americanization Strategy"
Declared "Re-Americanization": accelerate US manufacturing sourcing. $70M factory in Mocksville, NC — KALLAX shelves, 2M pieces/year (vs estimated 20M+ at Chinese scale factories). Partnerships expanded with Sauder Woodworking (Ohio). Target: mattresses and bulky items sourced primarily in US. Acquired Locus for last-mile delivery control. Long-run target: approach Europe's 70% regional sourcing model.

Zone D — OUTCOME / TENSION:
"The Flat-Pack Paradox"
Inter IKEA Group FY25: Revenue €26.3B (flat). Operating profit €1.7B despite "higher sourcing costs including tariff costs partly absorbed." US regional sourcing: 15% — down from 19% in 2014 (it's been going in the WRONG direction for a decade). The paradox: US factory at 2M KALLAX/year has higher per-unit cost than Chinese factory at scale. Tariffs must stay above ~25% for local production to win on total cost. The $70M NC factory = first step of a 5-7 year journey to 70% regional.

Zone E — DECISION IMPLICATION:
"The Break-Even Question for Every VP Supply Chain"
IKEA's case reveals the hidden cost of decades of global optimization: de-localization is NOT reversible in 12-24 months. It requires product design review + supplier development + factory capital at scale. The transferable framework: What is YOUR regional sourcing % today? At what tariff rate does local production become cheaper than imported + tariffed? That break-even calculation is the decision every COO needs to run this week.
