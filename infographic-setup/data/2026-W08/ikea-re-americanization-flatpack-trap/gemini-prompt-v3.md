## LinkedIn Caption

IKEA ships 10x more product per container than assembled rivals. That efficiency is why they cannot build a factory in America.

Most import-dependent supply chains are running the same strategy IKEA ran for 12 months: absorb tariff costs, optimise import sourcing, wait for the tariff to expire. IKEA, at EUR 44.6 billion in annual sales, hit the absorption ceiling. Sofas up USD 50. Bedroom sets up USD 100. Operating profit compressed with tariff costs "partly absorbed" disclosed in the annual results.

This infographic maps the Flat-Pack Paradox: the mechanism that built IKEA's global dominance is the same one blocking their re-Americanization.

Three things worth saving:
- 85% of IKEA US products are imported vs. 70% domestically sourced in Europe. US is IKEA's most tariff-exposed market globally. [IKEA/AMB Logistic, 2025]
- USD 70M NC factory produces 2M KALLAX units/yr. Comparable Chinese plant: 20M+. The cost gap does not close at 40% effective tariffs. [Reuters/BNN Bloomberg, 2025]
- Section 122 surcharge (15%) stacked on 25% furniture tariff = 40% effective rate on imported sofas. Expires July 24, 2026. [WilmerHale, 2026]

My read: Every supply chain team still optimising import costs is making the mistake IKEA made in 2024. The question stopped being "how do we source cheaper" when the effective tariff rate crossed 25%. It became: which categories in our portfolio can cross domestic break-even, and does our product design allow it?

In your organisation's supply chain, which SKU categories are your "kitchen cabinets" where domestic sourcing already crosses the break-even threshold, and which are your "KALLAX" where the product design makes domestic manufacturing structurally uneconomic regardless of tariff rate?

The tariff break-even benchmarks for import-dependent operations (save for your next sourcing review):
1. Below 20% effective rate: optimise imports — domestic premium not justified for most categories
2. 20–35%: run break-even by category — bulky, low-efficiency items likely cross the threshold
3. Above 35%: domestic sourcing competitive for most categories except high flat-pack-efficiency SKUs
Save this for your next tariff scenario planning session.
