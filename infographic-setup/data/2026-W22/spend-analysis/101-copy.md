## LinkedIn Caption

Why does every cost reduction programme start with a conversation and end with a spreadsheet nobody agrees on?

We started with a need to PO journey on Supply Chain 101, earlier this week. Now we jump into Episode 28.

A spend analysis maps all organisational spend by supplier, category, and
business unit to show where the money actually goes. It is not a cost
reduction programme. It is the picture that makes one possible.

The Pareto principle holds across almost every organisation that has run a
proper spend analysis. Twenty percent of spend categories account for eighty percent of total spend.

The strategic categories are usually visible because someone manages them. The problem is the tail: fragmented spend across dozens of smaller categories where no one has clear ownership and the per-unit cost is consistently higher than it needs to be.

• Direct spend is usually managed: Someone negotiates the materials that go
into the product, tracks the suppliers, and monitors performance. The data is
imperfect but the visibility exists.

• Indirect spend is a different story: Facilities, IT, professional services,
travel, MRO. In most organisations this runs between 15 and 30% of revenue.
Almost none of it is managed strategically because no one has mapped what is actually being spent and with whom.

My view on this is that cost reduction programmes stall not because the
savings are not there, but because the spend is not visible enough to know
where to start. A spend analysis does not find the savings, it finds the
categories where savings are most likely to exist.

Does your team know which category holds the most unmanaged spend in your organisation right now?
