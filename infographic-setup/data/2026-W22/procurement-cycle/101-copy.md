## LinkedIn Caption

Every procurement team has a process, although many never wrote it down. The ones that did outperform the ones that didn't.

This week on Supply Chain 101, Episode 29 we dive deep into the journey of a need to a PO.

There are seven steps between a sourcing need and a signed purchase order.
In a reactive team they run out of sequence, some get skipped entirely, and
the process rebuilds from scratch each time a new category comes up. In a
planned team the same seven steps run in order, and each one feeds the next.

• The foundation (Steps 1 to 3): Identify the real requirement, define
specifications clearly, and survey the supplier market before making contact
with anyone. These three steps take the most time, generate the most leverage downstream, and get cut most often when the team is under pressure.

• The market cycle (Steps 4 and 5): Issue the RFQ with the foundation complete and you get comparable, evaluable responses. Evaluate on total cost, lead time, and quality record, not price alone. A team that jumps to price at Step 5 undoes every hour invested in Steps 1 to 3.

• The close (Steps 6 and 7): Negotiate with a benchmark. Award and issue
the PO with a written record of why this supplier was selected.

One of the key things that people miss out on: reactive purchasing and planned procurement use the same seven steps. The difference is whether your team reaches Step 3 before or after they have already committed to a supplier in their mind.

At which step does your procurement process most often stall?
