## 10 Hook Options

1. **Contrarian** — The cheapest quote is almost never the cheapest supplier. Price is one column on a scorecard that has at least five.
2. **Comparison-Gap** — Two quotes land. One is 12% cheaper. The other delivers two weeks faster. Choosing on price alone is how you find out, three months later, which number actually mattered.
3. **Stat-Lead** — Unit price decides maybe 40% of what a supplier truly costs you. The other 60% is sitting in the columns nobody scored.
4. **Question-Why** — Why does the lowest bid so often turn into the most expensive relationship you have?
5. **Decision-Pressure** — You have five quotes open in a spreadsheet and a decision due Friday. Sorting by price is fast. It is also where most sourcing regrets begin.
6. **Paradox** — The lowest quote wins the order and quietly loses you the money. Both things are true, and that is the whole problem.
7. **Personal-Reflection** — I get asked this one constantly: "we have all the quotes, so how do we actually choose?" The honest answer is that the quote is the easy part.
8. **Question-How** — How do you compare five supplier quotes fairly when only one number on each page is easy to read?

10. **Timeline-Shock** — A quote takes a week to evaluate and a year to live with. Spend the week on the right five questions.

## LinkedIn Caption

The cheapest quote is almost never the cheapest supplier. Price is one column on a scorecard that has at least five.

This month on Shetty's Desk we're walking through procurement, and last week we covered the funnel that gets you from a long list down to a shortlist. Now you have the quotes in hand. This is where good teams slow down and weak ones just sort the spreadsheet by price.

So how do you compare them properly? You score the same handful of criteria for every supplier, and you weight them before you look at a single number.

• Price gets the attention, but it is only what you pay on day one. The real cost shows up in everything around it.

• Lead time and reliability decide whether the saving survives contact with reality. A supplier who is 10% cheaper but twice as slow forces you to hold more stock, and that extra inventory often eats the discount whole.

• Quality, payment terms, and references are the quiet columns. Longer payment terms free up cash. A bad reference is the cheapest warning you will ever get.

Here's the thing most people miss. The criterion that matters most is not on any quote: what does this supplier actually do when something goes wrong? You only learn that from their existing customers, which is exactly why the reference call is not a formality.

Score it, weight it, then decide. The weighting is where your real priorities show.
