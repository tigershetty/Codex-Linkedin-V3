**Topic**: The Kraljic Matrix — why you can't manage every supplier the same way

## 1. Framing
A 2×2 that sorts everything you buy into four types, so you know which suppliers to
fight on price, which to protect, and which to ignore. It reframes procurement from
"buy cheap" to "buy appropriately." Everyone already does this in real life — you
haggle on the big easy purchase and lose sleep over the one irreplaceable thing.

## 2. Verified facts & data
- Introduced by **Peter Kraljic**, *"Purchasing Must Become Supply Management,"* **Harvard Business Review, 1983** — the article that argued procurement is strategic, not clerical. **[High]** CARD-READY.
- Segments on **two axes — profit impact (importance of the purchase) × supply-market risk/complexity** — giving four categories. **[High]** CARD-READY (the axes).
- Four quadrants: **Non-critical/Routine** (low impact, low risk — e.g. office stationery), **Leverage** (high impact, low risk — costly but easy to source), **Bottleneck** (low impact, high risk — cheap but hard to get), **Strategic** (high impact, high risk). **[High]** CARD-READY.
- Each quadrant maps to a posture: **Leverage → exploit/squeeze on price; Strategic → partner & protect; Bottleneck → secure supply / diversify; Routine → automate & forget.** Power-based strategies: **Exploit / Diversify / Balance.** **[Med]** (consistent across secondary refs; original HBR text paywalled).

## 3. The "so what"
Same wallet, completely different behaviour — and that difference *is* the matrix.
Treating every supplier the same wastes attention on the trivial and under-protects
the critical.

## 4. Visual-data candidates
- A genuinely-3D **2×2 as four raised tiles**: X "Supply risk →", Y "Profit impact ↑".
- Each tile: name + buying posture + one everyday example. **Tile height encodes management attention** (Strategic tallest → Routine flattest).
