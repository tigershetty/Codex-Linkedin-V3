# Research Brief — make-vs-buy
**Pipeline**: 101 · **Week**: 2026-W26 (RW01) · **Generated**: 2026-06-21
**Standard**: consultant-grade, verified, reliability-tagged
**Topic**: Make vs. Buy — the decision that quietly shapes the whole business

## 1. Framing
How a company decides whether to build something itself or buy it from a supplier —
and why "it's cheaper to buy" is only half the question. The decision literally
defines the boundary of the company. Everyone feels it weekly: cook dinner or order
in, wash the car or pay someone.

## 2. Verified facts & data
- Two academic foundations: **Transaction Cost Economics** (Coase; **Oliver Williamson**) — *make* internally when transaction costs are high, *buy* from the market when low — and the **core-competency** view (**Prahalad & Hamel, HBR 1990**) — keep what differentiates you in-house, outsource the rest. **[High]** CARD-READY (the two lenses).
- A rigorous make-vs-buy compares on **total cost of ownership, not unit price** — Williamson's claim is that it turns on the purchase price *and* the transaction costs (negotiating, monitoring, the risk of being held up by a supplier). **[High]** CARD-READY.
- Standard practice evaluates on **five dimensions: cost, capability, risk, quality, speed/strategic fit** — not cost alone. **[Med]** CARD-READY (the decision criteria).
- The gating question practitioners use: **"Is this core to what makes us *us*?"** Yes → lean Make (protect control/IP); No → lean Buy (supplier expertise, flexibility). **[Med]** (practitioner framing of core-competency).

## 3. The "so what"
The right answer isn't about price; it's about what you're good at and what you'd
lose control of. Outsource your core and you give away the thing that made you
special; make everything and you carry cost and complexity you don't need.
