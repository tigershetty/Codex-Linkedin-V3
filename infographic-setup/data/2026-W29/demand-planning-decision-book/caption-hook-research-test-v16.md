## Hook Engineering Findings

Use these as hypotheses, not permanent rules yet:

- **Best opener family for Tiger:** consequence-first or meeting-moment tension. It sounds more like Tiger than a viral creator-style stat hook.
- **Avoid:** ultra-short stacked lines, fake contrarianism, "this changes everything", and engagement bait.
- **Use:** a practical gap between what teams discuss in the meeting and what they actually need to decide.
- **Strongest save trigger:** "save this for your next demand review" because the image is a meeting artifact, not just a concept.
- **Best comment trigger:** ask which operating decision changes first, because it invites practitioner specificity.

## Test Caption

A demand review should not end with everyone agreeing why the number was wrong.

That sounds obvious, but it happens more often than people admit.

The team explains the variance. The baseline was too high. A promotion did not lift the way it was expected to. Orders moved between weeks. A customer pulled back. Service issues distorted the signal.

All of that is useful, but it is still only the diagnostic layer.

The real value starts when the team asks what the miss changes.

Does inventory need to move?

Does capacity need to shift?

Does supply need to be expedited, delayed, or rebalanced?

Does the customer promise still hold?

This is where demand planning becomes more than a forecast review. The forecast is an input, but the demand plan is the operating commitment the business chooses to make after looking at the signal, the assumption, the risk, and the trade-off.

My view is that a stronger demand review separates four things very clearly:

• what signal changed
• where the forecast missed
• which assumption needs to be tested
• which decision changes next

That separation matters because otherwise the meeting becomes a debate about the number, when the business actually needs a decision.

And if the same miss keeps coming back, the answer is not always to tune the model harder. Sometimes the team needs to update the assumption the model is built around.

That is the idea behind this decision lens: read the miss, test the assumption, and then change the decision with discipline.

If actual demand moved away from the forecast tomorrow, which decision would your team change first?
