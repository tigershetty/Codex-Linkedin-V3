## 1. Image Intelligence

**Power format:** PF6 decision test/tree + process flow + PF2 boundary comparison  
**Eye path:** start with a bold artifact title, move into the forecast-to-decision spine, then scan side modules for signal, decomposition, commitment, and learning  
**Hero element:** open premium decision book on a demand planning desk, with page tabs, annotated charts, SKU cards, and small operational props around it  
**Density level:** dense-saveable  
**Repeated cell schema:** do not use identical repeated cards; each page module should have a different visual logic: signal stack, decomposition layers, boundary split, SKU segmentation tiles, FVA gauge, leading-indicator radar, decision tree, learning loop  
**Proof device:** role-specific detail and meeting-use logic; no external statistic needed  
**Save mechanism:** a planner can save it as a one-page reminder for demand review and S&OP prep

## 2. Caption Intelligence

**Opening move:** misconception / contrarian / plain premise  
**Audience promise:** stop treating the forecast as the answer; use it to decide what inventory, capacity, supply, and customer commitments need to change  
**Authority signal:** practical planning-room language: variance, bias, assumptions, demand review, S&OP, inventory, capacity, service  
**Value artifact:** decision rule / map / meeting narrative  
**CTA/save trigger:** ask which decision changes first when the forecast misses

## 3. Adaptation To Shetty's Desk

**What to borrow:** the directness of "Forecasts don't buy inventory," the structured-decision framing of ref 96, and the next-level practitioner framing of ref 89  
**What to avoid copying:** generic forecast-method education, clean-but-flat comparison tables, or a fake dashboard that looks precise but does not help the meeting  
**Supply-chain translation:** forecast output becomes a demand plan only when assumptions are reviewed and downstream decisions are made  
**Tiger-specific angle:** the valuable planning conversation is not whether the model was wrong; it is what the miss teaches and what the team changes next  
**Best-fit calendar topics:** W29 demand forecasting; demand plan vs. forecast; demand decomposition; demand review dashboard; FVA

## 4. Prompt Inputs

**Reference lesson for GPT Image 2:** create an artifact that feels like a working field manual for demand review, not a decorative AI infographic; make the decision book the hero and let the surrounding 3D planning scene prove context  
**Visual mechanics to borrow:** bold premise, structured decision map, annotated chart layers, boundary between forecast and plan, and save-worthy page tabs  
**Reference lesson for caption:** lead with the operating gap, then explain why the distinction matters in a real forecast review  
**Reference lesson for topic selection:** demand planning topics win when they give practitioners a repeatable decision test, not just definitions
