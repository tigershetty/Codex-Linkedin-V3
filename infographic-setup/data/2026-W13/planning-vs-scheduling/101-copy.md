## LinkedIn Caption

Planning decides what to make next month. Scheduling decides what to make at 2pm on Tuesday. The gap between those two is where most factories lose money.

This is Episode 3 of Supply Chain 101, a series that breaks down the building blocks of supply chain so they're clear whether you work in one or not. Last week we covered the difference between supply chain, logistics, and operations. Today we're going one level deeper into how production actually gets organised.

Planning is about allocation and priorities across time. Which product families get capacity this quarter? How much raw material do we commit to? These are decisions made in weeks and months, usually by a planner working with demand forecasts and inventory targets.

Scheduling is about sequencing and timing on the floor. Which order runs first on Line 3 this afternoon? How long is the changeover between Product A and Product B? These are decisions made in hours and days, usually by a production supervisor or scheduler working with real-time constraints.

Here's the thing most people miss: you can plan brilliantly and still deliver late if the schedule doesn't translate the plan into a realistic production sequence. The plan says "make 5,000 units this week." The schedule has to figure out when, on which machine, in what order, around maintenance windows and shift changes. That translation is where things break down.

Does your planning team talk to your scheduling team? And if they do, are they speaking the same language?
