## LinkedIn Caption

Sales says we'll sell 10,000 units. Operations says we can make 7,000. Finance has budgeted for 8,500. Somebody has to be wrong. S&OP is the process that figures out who, and what to do about it.

This is Episode 5 of Supply Chain 101. In Episode 4 we covered the three levels of planning. S&OP lives in the tactical layer, and it's probably the most talked-about process in supply chain that is also the most inconsistently executed.

S&OP stands for Sales and Operations Planning. It's a monthly cycle with five steps, and each one has a specific purpose:

- Demand Review is where the commercial side presents its forecast. What do we expect to sell, where, and when?
- Supply Review is where operations responds with what's actually possible given current capacity, materials, and constraints.
- Pre-S&OP is where the gaps between those two get surfaced and options are prepared.
- Executive S&OP is where leadership makes the trade-off decisions (do we add a shift, delay a launch, or accept the shortfall?).
- Implementation is where those decisions get translated into the operational and financial plans that everyone works from.

Here's the thing most people miss: the meeting itself is maybe 30% of the value. The other 70% comes from the preparation between meetings and the accountability that follows. Companies that treat S&OP as a monthly slide deck get monthly slide deck results. Companies that treat it as a decision-making rhythm get aligned execution.

Does your S&OP change behaviour, or is it a monthly PowerPoint ritual?
