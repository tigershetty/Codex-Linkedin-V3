## LinkedIn Caption

How do you buy a car?

- Procurement is the months of research: which brands, which features, which dealerships have the best reputation and financing terms.

- Purchasing is the day you walk in, negotiate the final price, and sign the papers.

Your company does both. The question is whether anyone has separated them.

This is Episode 9 of Supply Chain 101. So far we've covered the language of supply chain: planning layers, production strategies, and frameworks. Now we're moving into the mechanics. How supply chains buy, hold inventory, manage cost, and move goods. And it starts here, with the most commonly confused pair of words in the profession.

- Purchasing is operational and executional. It's the daily work of converting a need into a transaction: sending purchase orders, confirming delivery dates, managing invoices.
- Procurement is strategic. It's the work of deciding who to buy from, where to source, at what terms, and under what quality standards. Procurement designs the supplier relationship before purchasing executes it.

Here's the confusing part: when companies conflate the two, strategic decisions get treated like operational tasks. A supplier that should be evaluated on total cost, reliability, and alignment gets chosen because they responded to the RFQ fastest. And vice versa: operational orders that just need to go out get held up by strategic review they don't require.

Does your company have a procurement strategy, or just a purchasing department?

## Hook Options — All 10 (reference)
1. [Question-Why]: "Why do some companies have a procurement department and a purchasing department, while others use one name for both? Because they're not the same function, and treating them as one is how strategic decisions end up getting made by the person placing orders."
2. [Question-How]: "How do you buy a car? You research the market, compare options, decide on features, and negotiate terms. That's procurement. Then you sign the papers and drive it home. That's purchasing. Your company does both. The question is whether anyone has separated them."
3. [Stat-Lead]: "Purchasing is a transaction. Procurement is a strategy. One is a transaction. The other decides whether the transaction should happen at all, from whom, and under what terms. Most companies use the words interchangeably and wonder why their spend is unmanaged."
4. [Contrarian]: "Calling your procurement team a purchasing department isn't just a naming issue. It tells you how the company thinks about spend. If the people deciding which suppliers to partner with are the same people sending POs, the strategic work never gets done."
5. [Paradox]: "Purchasing buys what someone asked for. Procurement decides whether they should be buying it at all, from whom, and under what terms. One is a transaction. The other is a strategy. Most companies use the same word for both."
6. [Personal-Reflection]: "I have eight people sending out purchase orders every day and two people scanning the market for components we haven't bought in two years. Both roles sit in the same team, but they think about completely different problems on completely different time horizons."
7. [Result-First]: "When a company treats procurement as just a fancier word for purchasing, strategic supplier decisions get made with operational urgency. You end up choosing suppliers the way you choose lunch: whatever's fastest and cheapest today."
8. [Timeline-Shock]: "Monday morning: a procurement specialist is evaluating three potential suppliers for a component the company has never sourced before. She's comparing quality standards, lead times, financial stability, and contract terms. Down the hall, a purchasing specialist is sending out 40 POs for materials the factory needs by Thursday. Same department. Completely different jobs."
9. [Comparison-Gap]: "Purchasing asks: can we get this by Friday? Procurement asks: should we be buying this from this supplier in the first place? One operates in days. The other operates in quarters. Most companies expect the same person to do both."
10. [Decision-Pressure]: "Does your company have a procurement strategy, or just a purchasing department? If the answer takes more than five seconds, it's probably the second one."
