## LinkedIn Caption

Why does your supplier say 6 weeks but the customer waits 10? Because supplier lead time is one of at least five lead times in your supply chain, and it's the only a portion most companies measure.

This is Episode 13 of Supply Chain 101. In Episode 12 we covered safety stock and how lead time variability drives how much buffer you need. Today we're unpacking lead time itself, because most companies are only measuring a fraction of it.

When someone says "lead time," they usually mean supplier lead time: how long it takes from placing a PO to receiving the goods. But that's one segment of a much longer chain.

The full cumulative lead time breaks down into at least five components:
- Information lead time: how long it takes to identify the need, get approval, and enter the order into the system. This is pure decision speed, and it's often invisible.
- Supplier lead time: how long the supplier takes to produce or ship the goods after receiving the order.
- Manufacturing lead time: if you're transforming what you receive, how long does your own production take?
- Transportation lead time: time in transit from supplier to your facility, or from your facility to the customer.
- Receiving and QC lead time: time to unload, inspect, check quality, and make goods available in the system.

Add those up and you get the real lead time. In most cases I've seen, supplier lead time is less than half the total.

While cutting 3 days of information lead time (faster decisions, fewer approval loops) often costs nothing. Cutting 3 days of transportation lead time might mean airfreight or a completely different logistics network.

Have you mapped the full cumulative lead time for your top 10 SKUs?
