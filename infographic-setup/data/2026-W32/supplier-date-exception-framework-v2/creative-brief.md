# Working Infographic Brief — Supplier-date promise-control card V5

**Status:** selected internal prototype; not approved for publication.

**Reader and exact work moment:** A planner, buyer, KAM, or supply-chain manager has received a
changed supplier date and needs to decide whether it has become a customer-facing exception.

**One outcome / tension at feed size:** When a supplier date moves, start with customer need-by.

**Spatial thesis:** One incoming date-change ticket first crosses the customer need-by, current
promise and allocation context it could affect. It then tests credible supplier exposure and
recovery. The customer date remains visibly held until a credible miss remains after recovery and
KAM + Purchasing make a joint exception decision.

**Useful deep detail:**

1. Customer need-by: the date protected, current promise and priority/allocation.
2. Credible exposure: supplier source/reason, last confirmation and best/base/worst scenario.
3. Recovery: stock/buffer, alternative source, split delivery/freight.
4. Decision: hold the customer promise when no credible miss remains; otherwise KAM + Purchasing
   jointly own the customer exception, message, owner, and next update.

**Visual carries:** the date-change ticket, customer need-by as the governing first field, its
three sequential status fields, and the exact joint-exception boundary.

**Caption uniquely carries:** a lived meeting moment, why teams over-escalate or under-escalate,
scope, and an invitation to discuss the weakest gate.

**Claim boundary:** Tiger-proposed internal decision structure; no performance, cost, delivery, or
deployment outcome claim.

**Rendering route:** Figma-native exact social visual; optional semantic-state motion only after a
static review. V5 uses an original promise-control card rather than the V2/V3/V4 process-card,
route, or worksheet comparison.
