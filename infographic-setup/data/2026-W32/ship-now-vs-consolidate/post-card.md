# Standard Post Card — Ship Now vs Consolidate

**Reader and real work moment:** A planner or buyer has a ready shipment, a supplier says the next
PO will be ready tomorrow, and a combined freight quote makes waiting look attractive.

**Opening tension:** The freight quote says wait. The last safe departure says ship.

**The visible proof:** A physical freight-release gate between a ready pallet and the last safe
departure; the second PO is still outside the gate while the customer need-by sits beyond it.

**The useful keep:** Start at the customer need-by, work backwards to the last safe departure, then
test the second PO against that gate across credible ready-date scenarios. If one does not clear and
the ready PO still protects need-by, release it; if neither option protects need-by, replan or
expedite.

**Saved references used:** Pressure-gate pattern from the V3 MPS commitment fence, current
customer-safe timing corridor exploration, and Top-100 reference 33. Caption route uses the
saved-post half-truth to missing-dimension to decision-rule pattern.

**Three rough routes:** A: the release gate asks whether the ready truck can wait; B: the visible
freight saving collides with the invisible customer date; C: KAM and Purchasing stand on opposite
sides of the same outbound order.

**Selected route and why:** A, the pressure gate. The selected native render makes the lower-freight
temptation a physical coral pull against a ready shipment, then gives the reader one irreversible
route through the last safe departure to the customer need-by. The image carries the collision; the
caption carries the decision method and exception ownership.
