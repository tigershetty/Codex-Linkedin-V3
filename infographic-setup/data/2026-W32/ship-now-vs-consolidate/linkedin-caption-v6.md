**Status:** Draft — final Tiger approval and publication pending

The freight quote says wait. The last safe departure says ship.

When one PO is ready and another is expected tomorrow, the combined freight rate can look better.
That saving may be real, but the decision starts with the customer need-by date.

Work backwards to the last departure that still protects it, then test the second PO's ready date
across credible scenarios rather than relying on the one supplier date we hope will be true. If all
credible scenarios clear the departure, consolidate. If one misses it and releasing the ready PO
still protects need-by, ship what is ready.

And if neither option protects the date, this is no longer simply a consolidation choice. Replan or
expedite, and make the exception visible rather than letting it quietly become a planner's judgement
call.

The KAM and Purchasing Manager should own that exception together: the KAM protects the customer
commitment, while Purchasing owns the commercial trade-off. Their approval records the exception;
it does not make the service risk safe.

The freight saving counts only when the customer promise is still protected.
