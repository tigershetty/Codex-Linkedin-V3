## Recommended Caption

A freight saving can be perfectly real and still be the wrong shipping decision.

Picture the situation: shipment A is ready, shipment B may be ready in a few days, and combining them lowers the freight bill. The quote says wait, but the customer need-by date may say leave today.

My view on this is that the decision order matters more than the apparent saving:

1. First, confirm that the two orders can actually travel together and that the supplier-ready dates, transit ranges, customer date and freight quotes are current. If one hard input is stale, stop and refresh it.

2. Then test service across credible best, base and worst sensitivity cases. This gives you the last defensible departure under the inputs you have today.

3. Only after that should you calculate the economic break-even hold, using the costs that genuinely change between shipping now and waiting.

4. The smaller of the service and economic limits governs.

If any credible consolidation case misses the customer need-by date while shipping now protects it, ship now. If neither option protects the date, replan or expedite instead of choosing the cheaper miss.

An exception can still be valid, but it should be a conscious one. The KAM and Purchasing Manager need to accept it together because one protects the customer commitment and the other owns the purchasing and freight trade-off. And if the same exception keeps returning, the supplier-date reliability, customer-date process, freight setup or governing rule needs correction.

This is a transparent decision aid, not an optimiser or an arrival prediction. Its job is to make the assumptions, limits and ownership visible before an attractive freight saving is treated as certainty.

Before holding a ready order, ask for its last defensible departure.
