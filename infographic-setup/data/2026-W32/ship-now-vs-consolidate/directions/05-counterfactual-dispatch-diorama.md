# Direction 05 — Counterfactual Dispatch Diorama

**Status:** selected C3 candidate; Tiger’s final visual and caption approval still required.

## The visual wager

Can one physical dispatch world make the tempting decision and its consequence visible at the same time? The reader needs to see why holding a ready load is economically attractive, and why it can still become the wrong release decision once it spends the customer-safe departure.

## Reader promise

`A lower combined rate can be worth waiting for, but only if the customer date is not being used as the buffer.`

## Causal story in the image

- **A — ready now:** the green service-safe path carries the ready load towards the customer need-by clock.
- **B — ready range:** the coral, translucent arrival trace is a credible readiness range, not a promised date or a measured probability.
- **Last safe departure:** one engineered shutter is the shared decision boundary.
- **Held A:** the lower-right A is intentionally the counterfactual version of the same ready load: it is held in a two-load bay while waiting for B, then visibly locked behind the closed boundary.

The visual makes no claim about a real shipment, exact saving, supplier performance, or a universal optimiser. It is a decision illustration for two compatible loads with current, credible inputs.

## Render learning

`visual-v13-counterfactual-dispatch-diorama.png` established the new world and material language, but it failed the ten-second proof: both loads read as A, the consolidation motive was absent, and the calendar trace had no decision consequence.

`visual-v14-counterfactual-dispatch-diorama-retest.png` was the one bounded correction. It removes the decorative dates and footer, distinguishes A from B, makes B a range, and turns the held A into the spatial consequence. The LinkedIn crop is `visual-v14-counterfactual-dispatch-diorama-linkedin-4x5.png`.

## Acceptance result

- **3 seconds:** green customer-safe path, coral uncertainty, hard departure boundary, and a held load create a recognisable operating conflict.
- **10 seconds:** A is ready, B is a range, and holding A can consume the last safe departure.
- **30 seconds:** the reader can infer the customer-first rule; the caption adds research context, the test sequence, exception ownership, and the recurring-exception lesson.

The Operating Trace is doing causal work: remove B’s coral readiness band and the image no longer explains why the held load is trapped. No further image render is authorised for C3. If Tiger rejects this candidate, park the topic rather than starting another silent visual loop.
