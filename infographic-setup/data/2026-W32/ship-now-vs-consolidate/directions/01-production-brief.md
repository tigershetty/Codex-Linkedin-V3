**Status:** selected candidate after the rapid direction sprint; final Tiger approval and publication remain pending.

**Brand frame:** `references/brand-kits/shettys-desk-operating-studio/FRAME.md`

## Reader promise

A purchaser, planner, KAM, or logistics lead sees a familiar moment first: one order is ready, a
second compatible order might make freight cheaper, and the customer need-by could make waiting
indefensible. The reader leaves with the customer-safe-window distinction, not a generic argument
for or against consolidation.

## Creative decision

Direction 01 won because the conflict can be understood without domain jargon: a physical route
switch makes the question **"Which order gets to wait?"** immediate. Direction 02 remains the
better leadership/KAM sequel and Direction 03 remains the stronger compact reference post; neither
is a colour variation of this one.

## Public claim boundary

This is a proposed professional decision method, not a customer result, universal optimisation
claim, or calculated business outcome.

- Start with compatibility and current inputs.
- Test the customer need-by against credible supplier-ready and transit scenarios before treating a
  freight saving as actionable.
- When a credible wait scenario misses need-by while ship-now protects it, ship now is the standard
  choice.
- A KAM + Purchasing Manager exception records the risk; it does not make the customer miss safe.
