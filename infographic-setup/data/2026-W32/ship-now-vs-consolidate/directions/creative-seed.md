# C3 Rapid Direction Sprint — Creative Seed

**Status:** active direction test; the existing `The Last Safe Departure` package is retained as a **non-selected negative control**. It is not a visual or caption standard for this sprint.

## The real decision

A compatible inbound PO is ready. A second PO may be ready soon. The purchasing practitioner has a plausible freight-saving reason to wait, but the customer need-by date may not permit the hold.

The post must make the reader feel the decision before it teaches the method:

> **Who, or what, is allowed to wait when a customer commitment is exposed?**

## Reader and job

- **Primary reader:** working purchasing practitioner or Purchasing Manager.
- **Secondary reader:** KAM, planning manager, logistics lead, or transformation leader who recognises the hand-off.
- **Reader moment:** a ready PO, an incomplete consolidation, an approaching customer need-by, and no obvious owner for the trade-off.
- **Reader promise:** leave with one useful decision distinction, rather than a generic freight-saving reminder.

## Creative rules for the three tiles

1. Start in the operating moment, not with the formula, tool, policy, or a consultancy-style framework title.
2. Give the visual one physical decision object and one reading route. A reader should understand the tension in three seconds.
3. Keep the output useful enough to save: one governing question, a small decision rule, or a compact check sequence.
4. Do not use invented customer stories, timings, costs, results, probabilities, or internal calculator fixtures.
5. Do not represent a service-risk exception as safe. Customer need-by is the proposed first gate; compatibility and input freshness still apply.
6. Treat unreliable supplier-ready dates, unclear customer dates, and changing freight quotes as a boundary, not a footnote.

## Shared claim boundary

The following is a **proposed decision method / professional judgment**, not a published customer result or universal optimisation claim:

- Test customer need-by across credible scenarios before treating a consolidation saving as actionable.
- When a credible consolidation scenario misses need-by while ship-now protects it, the standard choice is ship now.
- An exception requires joint KAM and Purchasing Manager escalation and remains a recorded risk, not a safe result.

## The test

These are not three colour variants. They are three different ways into the same work moment:

| Direction | Primary tension | Visual family | Saveable payload |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 — Which order gets to wait? | Customer promise vs a tempting hold | Physical loading-dock decision scene | The customer-time window decides whether waiting is allowed |
| 02 — One shipment, two valid scorecards | Functions have partial but legitimate views | Split-perspective / shared gate | Both scorecards must meet at one decision gate |
| 03 — Three dates, one shipment decision | A delay is only safe inside a real timing window | Date corridor / deadline logic | The three dates to inspect before consolidation |

## Direction-selection question

At mobile size, which tile would make a practitioner stop because it resembles a real decision they have had to make — and then save because it gives them a clearer way to handle it?

## Sprint result — 2026-08-03

**Selected:** Direction 01 — *Which order gets to wait?*

It wins the reach test because the release decision is immediately legible before any framework is
introduced: a ready pallet, a tempting wait path, and a customer need-by gate. Direction 02 is
retained as a future KAM/Purchasing ownership post; Direction 03 is retained as a future
high-save timing-reference post. Neither needs to be forced into the first post.
