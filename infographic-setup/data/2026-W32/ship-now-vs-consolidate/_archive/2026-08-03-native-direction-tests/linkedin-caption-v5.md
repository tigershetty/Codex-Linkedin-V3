**Status:** Draft — final Tiger approval and publication pending

The quote says wait, but the customer date says ship — and that is where a perfectly real freight
saving can become the wrong shipping decision.

When one PO is packed, another is promised for tomorrow, and the combined rate looks better, it is
very easy to frame this as a freight question. It is actually a customer-promise question, because
“tomorrow” only matters if the supplier date, the transit basis, and the compatibility check are
good enough to clear the last departure that still protects need-by.

That is the order I would use: confirm the inputs, work back from the customer date to the last
safe departure, and then compare the supplier's latest credible ready date with that gate. If it
clears, consolidate; if it does not, release what is ready.

And if the date is still vague, it should not quietly become a planner's decision just because the
quote is attractive. The KAM and Purchasing Manager need to own the exception together: one is
protecting the customer promise and one owns the commercial trade-off.

The saving is still real, but it cannot be allowed to spend the customer date by accident.
