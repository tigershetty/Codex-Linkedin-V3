“It'll be ready tomorrow.”

That sounds like a date. It is not a release date.

One PO is ready. The next one may be ready tomorrow. Waiting could make the freight cheaper.

But the customer still has one need-by date.

Start there. Work backwards to the last departure that still protects it. Then put the supplier's
latest credible ready date against that gate — not the date we hope will be true.

If it cannot clear the gate, ship what is ready.

If the dates are too loose to call, do not make it quietly become the planner's judgement. The KAM
and Purchasing Manager decide the exception together: one owns the customer promise; the other owns
the commercial trade-off.

The freight quote stays in the decision. It just comes after the customer date is protected.
