## Three Developed Directions

Use three genuinely different families. Utility, visual idea, and narrative are a useful default,

### Direction A

- Concept: The Shipment Hold Permit — a ready shipment may wait only after it earns permission through four ordered gates.
- Reader promise: A reusable release rule for deciding whether a ready shipment may wait.
- Opening: “A shipment is ready. Another PO is coming. Before you wait, you need four answers in the right order.”
- Visual object and eye path: One oversized warehouse release tag clipped to a pallet. Read vertically through compatibility/current data -> customer need-by across credible scenarios -> differential economics -> exception governance, ending on `SHIP / HOLD / ESCALATE`.
- Useful object: A saveable four-gate checklist with exact decision outputs.
- Caption arc: Familiar shipment scene -> why freight saving is incomplete -> correct sequence -> unreliable dates and expiring quotes -> governed exceptions -> optional calculator.
- Bridge: The website calculator accepts current inputs and produces an auditable decision record; LinkedIn still contains the complete rule.
- Claim modes and support route: Framework/workflow; state scope and failure boundaries, cite the research brief, and use no synthetic result. Tiger-specific customer-first and owner wording stays pending public-use approval.
- Why it is original: It transforms completeness and staged utility into a physical operating document rather than another poster, dashboard, or card grid.
- **Trade-off:** Highest practitioner save value, but its procedural appearance may narrow initial reach.

### Direction B

- Concept: The Last Safe Departure — waiting can be economically attractive and still fail because the customer date does not grant enough time.
- Reader promise: See exactly how to calculate the last defensible time a ready shipment may wait.
- Opening: “The shipment is ready and the freight quote rewards waiting. But the customer date may still say leave today.”
- Visual object and eye path: One large cobalt route switch. A ready pallet splits into `SHIP NOW` and `WAIT + CONSOLIDATE`; both paths run toward one customer need-by gate. Best/base/worst timing bands sit on the waiting route, with the limiting path reaching or crossing the deadline.
- Useful object: `permitted hold = min(service-safe hold, economic break-even hold)`, applied across all credible scenarios, plus a visible data-stop boundary.
- Caption arc: Put the reader at the loading decision -> explain why a likely-ready date creates false precision -> test service before economics -> explain the two limits -> define the exception boundary -> invite readers to test current inputs.
- Bridge: The calculator computes the last defensible departure from actual supplier ranges, transit ranges, customer dates, and current quotes.
- Claim modes and support route: Formula/method with explicit inputs, assumptions, limitations, and traceable research. Internal fixtures remain test-only. Named KAM/Purchasing ownership is approved in meaning under `TS-20260802-02`.
- Why it is original: It recombines contradiction, ordered reasoning, and formula utility into a spatial deadline race instead of a text-heavy comparison.
- **Trade-off:** Best balance of broad comprehension and practitioner value; exact role-governance detail moves to the caption or calculator.

### Direction C

- Concept: Two Keys to Hold a Shipment — an economically attractive hold still needs governed ownership when the customer date is exposed.
- Reader promise: A clear ownership model that prevents consolidation exceptions from becoming an uncontrolled habit.
- Opening: “Who is allowed to delay a ready shipment when the freight saving is real but the customer date is at risk?”
- Visual object and eye path: A dual-key control mechanism. The need-by clock dominates the top; supplier timing and freight quote feed the centre; KAM and Purchasing Manager controls sit at the bottom while the calculated status remains visible.
- Useful object: Rule -> joint exception -> recurrence -> system correction.
- Caption arc: Each function sees a partial truth -> unilateral ownership fails -> distinguish an allowed exception from a safe result -> show why repeated exceptions trigger correction.
- Bridge: The calculator's audit export records owners, decision time, reason, and recurrence.
- Claim modes and support route: Tiger interpretation and proposed governance model; do not present it as an industry-wide rule. Public use is approved in meaning under `TS-20260802-02`.
- Why it is original: It turns cross-functional governance into a memorable mechanical metaphor rather than another RACI or responsibility table.
- **Trade-off:** Strong leadership and comment potential, but it teaches less of the underlying economics and depends most heavily on Tiger approval.

## Selection

**Selected direction:** Direction B — The Last Safe Departure
**Why it wins:** It makes the contradiction legible before teaching the method, combines reach potential with save utility, and mirrors the successful tension in Tiger's supplier-versus-customer lead-time precedent without copying its subject or execution.
**3-second problem / promise:** A ready shipment is waiting for a second PO—but the customer date may not permit the hold.
**10-second insight:** The maximum defensible hold is the smaller of the service-safe limit and the economic break-even limit across every credible scenario.
**30-second useful action:** Check compatibility and freshness, calculate both limits, then ship, consolidate, stop, review, or escalate with the reason recorded.
**Coherence rationale:** Every atom serves one release decision: the contradiction earns attention, the deadline race explains it, the two-limit formula makes it usable, and the calculator continues the same job with live inputs.
**What the caption adds:** Input definitions, why likely-ready dates create false precision, failure boundaries, governance, and the distinction between a standard decision and an exception.
**What the visual provides:** The deadline tension, scenario path, two-limit formula, and decision sequence at a glance.
