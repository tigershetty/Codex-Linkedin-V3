“It should be ready tomorrow” is not a date you can release freight against.

When a PO is packed, the next one is expected shortly, and the combined freight quote looks better,
it becomes very easy to start with the saving because it is the one number everyone can see.

But the actual decision starts somewhere else. Work backwards from the customer need-by and identify
the last departure that still protects it. Then put the second PO's latest credible ready date
against that point, rather than the date we would like to believe.

If it crosses the gate, PO A leaves. The saving can still look attractive, but it is no longer a
clean consolidation decision; it is a decision to let the customer absorb an uncertainty created
upstream.

If the dates are not clear enough to make that call, the answer is not to tighten the range in the
spreadsheet. The KAM and Purchasing Manager need to make the exception together and be clear about
what risk is accepted. And if that is happening every week, it tells us to fix the foundation, not
normalise the exception.

Essentially, the freight quote tells us what we could save. The customer-safe window tells us
whether we have permission to try.
