# Hook Bank v2 — Which Order Gets to Wait?

**Use:** choose one opening that matches the selected visual and caption. These are story openings,
not standalone claims of customer results. `H-01` is selected for the first visual.

| ID | Hook | Mechanism | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|
| H-01 | **Which order gets to wait?** | Puts the reader inside the release decision | Selected visual headline |
| H-02 | A freight saving can be real while the decision to wait is still indefensible. | Cost/service collision | Caption opening |
| H-03 | A ready PO is not automatically spare time. | Reframes the readiness signal | Short text post or Note |
| H-04 | The consolidation decision has already started to drift if cost is the first question asked. | Changes the decision order | Practitioner commentary |
| H-05 | One order is ready. The next may be ready soon. The customer date gets the deciding vote. | Three-way tension | Alternate visual cover |
| H-06 | “It should be ready in a few days” is not enough information to hold a ready shipment. | Challenges false precision | Date-corridor sequel |
| H-07 | A cheaper combined shipment is only cheaper if the customer can afford the wait. | Plain-language boundary | Broad-reach opener |
| H-08 | The difficult part of consolidation is not the freight quote; it is deciding who is allowed to carry the service risk. | Ownership tension | Leadership/KAM sequel |
| H-09 | When a customer date is exposed, a saved freight cost is not the only cost in the decision. | Makes the hidden consequence visible | Caption opening variation |
| H-10 | The question is not whether consolidation saves money. It is whether the customer date gives you permission to wait. | Governing question | Selected caption alternative |

## Selection note

Avoid leading with `EOQ`, a formula, a calculator, or an AI tool. They are depth assets after the
reader recognises the operating moment; they are not the reason a buyer stops scrolling.
