## Final Approved Caption

If executive S&OP is where your team starts reconciling the numbers, the process is already late.

That sounds severe, but it is the clearest way I know to separate the meeting from the process.

A lot of teams describe S&OP as one monthly meeting. So the calendar fills with slides, sales arrives with one version of demand, supply brings a different view of what can be delivered, and finance points to the gap against the business plan.

Then the first real reconciliation begins when the executives are already in the room.

The meeting becomes a workshop. Assumptions are challenged, scenarios are rebuilt live, people disappear into spreadsheets to verify a number, and the decisions that actually need executive authority get squeezed into the final part of the conversation.

For me, a stronger monthly rhythm separates five pieces of pre-work:

• Data and performance readiness makes sure the actuals, gaps, and assumptions are trusted enough to discuss.
• Portfolio review settles which launches, phase-outs, and lifecycle changes should alter the plan.
• Demand review converts the baseline, events, and commercial intelligence into one consensus demand view with scenarios.
• Supply review tests that demand against capacity, materials, inventory, and the feasible response options.
• Integrated reconciliation brings those plans together, financializes the remaining gaps, and prepares the recommendation and decision asks.

The exact names and ownership will vary between businesses, but the operating contract should be similar: every review must finish something and hand something useful to the next one.

By the time Executive S&OP starts, the team should not be discovering the demand assumption, rebuilding the capacity view, or asking Finance what the scenario means for revenue, margin, or working capital.

The executive team should be choosing between prepared scenarios, allocating resources where authority is required, and committing the business to one plan.

The readiness test I keep coming back to is simple:

• Evidence - what changed, and what do we trust?
• Owner - who is accountable for moving the issue?
• Options - what can the business realistically choose?
• Ask - what specific decision needs executive authority?

If one of those is missing, the issue may still be important, but it is not ready for executive S&OP.

That is the difference between using the meeting to figure things out and using it to confirm, decide, and commit.
