## Hook Options

1. Ten minutes into executive S&OP, someone asks, "Which forecast version are we using?" The question is fair. Where it is being answered is the problem.
2. Every minute executive S&OP spends reconstructing the plan is a minute it cannot spend choosing one.
3. When your executive meeting opens, can every unresolved issue be stated as a decision? If not, the cycle is still doing pre-work.
4. A quiet executive S&OP can be a sign that the process is working because the difficult reconciliation happened before the executives entered the room.
5. My readiness test for an executive S&OP issue is simple: evidence, an owner, real options, and a decision ask.
6. Pre-work should remove avoidable uncertainty without pretending the genuine trade-off has disappeared.
7. "Can someone pull up the latest capacity file?" is not harmless meeting noise when the executive team is waiting to choose a plan.
8. An updated review and a completed hand-off are different things. One records what changed; the other prepares what happens next.
9. I would judge an S&OP cycle less by the executive deck and more by how few basic questions the room still needs to answer.

## Human Spine

- **Useful professional truth:** completing five review meetings does not create readiness unless each review hands forward something the next decision can use.
- **Concrete consequence:** executives end up reconciling forecast versions, assumptions, capacity, and financial impact instead of choosing a scenario.
- **Tiger's judgment:** judge the cycle by the quality of its hand-offs and by what the executive meeting no longer needs to reconstruct.
- **Tension left open:** some service, capacity, portfolio, and financial trade-offs genuinely require executive authority; good pre-work sharpens them instead of hiding them.

## Final Approved Caption

The executive meeting does not rescue a weak S&OP cycle. It exposes where the hand-offs stopped.

Once Operations reopens the capacity assumptions, Finance starts rebuilding the gap to plan, and Commercial explains what moved, the meeting is no longer choosing between prepared options. It is reconstructing the pre-work with the executive team already in the room.

Where I think the process often breaks is in the hand-off between reviews. A review can happen on time, use the right template, and still leave the next team with work it cannot use.

The data can be accurate, but the exceptions may have no owner. Demand can agree a number that has not been tested against supply. Supply can produce feasible options without showing the service or financial consequence. Integrated reconciliation can quantify the gap and still arrive without a recommendation.

In each case, the review happened but the actual hand-off did not.

The exact review names and ownership will vary between businesses, but the operating test is useful: each review should remove avoidable uncertainty from the next decision.

Before an unresolved issue enters the meeting, I would want four things visible:

* Evidence: what changed, and what do we trust?
* Owner: who is accountable for moving it?
* Options: what can the business genuinely choose?
* Ask: what decision requires executive authority?

Think about your last executive S&OP: which discussion arrived with a well-described problem, but no choice the room could actually make?
