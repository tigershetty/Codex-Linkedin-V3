## Draft Caption For Final Review

Ten minutes into executive S&OP, someone asks which forecast version is on the slide. At that point, the meeting is already doing the wrong job.

The question is reasonable; the problem is that it is being answered with the executive team already in the room.

Then Operations reopens the capacity assumptions, Finance rebuilds the gap to plan, Commercial explains which promotion moved, and a decision meeting becomes a live reconciliation exercise.

Go back a week and the failure is rarely one missing slide. More often, the five reviews happened, but each produced an update instead of a completed hand-off.

That distinction changes what useful pre-work should leave behind:

* Data and performance leaves a trusted exception pack, so the room is not debating basic actuals.
* Portfolio leaves approved lifecycle changes, not a list of launches and phase-outs that everyone is seeing for the first time.
* Demand leaves one consensus view with scenarios, rather than three functional versions of the number.
* Supply leaves feasible responses and their consequences, not one constraint chart with no choice attached.
* Integrated reconciliation leaves a recommendation and explicit decision asks, with the financial impact already visible.

None of those outputs is simply another status deck. Each one should remove a question from the executive meeting and replace it with a choice the business is ready to make.

Of course, not every issue should be resolved beforehand. Some trade-offs exist precisely because no function can close them alone. A service promise may protect revenue and overload capacity; a portfolio choice may be strategically right and financially painful. The point of the pre-work is not to make those tensions disappear.

It is to make them ready for a decision.

Before an issue enters the room, I would want four things visible:

* Evidence: what changed, and what do we trust?
* Owner: who is accountable for moving it?
* Options: what can the business genuinely choose?
* Ask: what decision requires executive authority?

Think about your last S&OP cycle: which issue reached the executive room before it had all four?
