# Market / reference-shelf scan — EOQ, consolidation, dispatch

**Scope:** a light creative scan for one proposed LinkedIn resource graphic. It is not a market-size
study, a demand estimate, or a claim about what LinkedIn will distribute.

## What was checked

1. The supplied saved/Top-100 collection for formula-led, planning-method, freight, and
   cross-functional-decision packaging.
2. Tiger's existing historical content corpus for how batch-size/EOQ material has already been
   framed on this account.
3. The source-backed knowledge-map branch
   `SC-KM-BATCH-CONSOLIDATE-DISPATCH` and its three public source capsules.

## What it suggests

- Formula-first EOQ explanations are familiar. They normally answer “how much should I order?”
  and stop there.
- Freight or load-consolidation material normally leads with a rate, a full truck, or a generic
  cost-saving claim.
- The useful gap is not an unsupported promise that a new formula will save money. It is a clearer
  separation of **three different decisions hiding behind one purchase review**: quantity, wait,
  and dispatch.

That makes the candidate potentially broad enough for planners, buyers, and logistics readers while
remaining specific enough to use in an actual review. It must remain a framework: a local answer
still needs real demand, cost, capacity, rate, lead-time, and customer-commitment inputs.

## Creative reference signals only

- `TOP100-096` proves the attention value of a compact method-to-condition comparison. It does not
  prove performance or choose this topic.
- `TOP100-020` shows an asymmetric map can make one familiar work object lead to multiple distinct
  actions. Its AI subject, logos, copy, and visual expression are not transferable.
- `PIERRI-011` proves that a reader can compare detailed alternatives when criteria are genuinely
  shared. Its exact columns, surface, title, and colour treatment are not transferable.

## Decision

Proceed only as a bounded, source-backed **framework**: “EOQ cannot tell you when to dispatch.”
Do not turn this into a cost-saving claim, numerical recommendation, or generic `EOQ vs MOQ`
explainer.
