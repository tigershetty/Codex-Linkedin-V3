You can spend years being told that the route into leadership is to become more cross-functional, and still be left with the unhelpful question of what that is supposed to mean on Monday morning.

The advice becomes useful only when you connect it to the next decision your work affects.

A buyer who understands demand variability negotiates very differently from one who sees only the purchase price. A planner who can see a real capacity constraint builds a plan that has a chance of surviving contact with production. And an analyst becomes far more useful when the work begins with the operator’s decision rather than the dashboard.

That does not mean everyone needs to become a generalist. It means that depth in one function becomes more valuable when you can understand the language of the function immediately beside it.

Use this working map when choosing the next project, mentor, rotation, or conversation:

• Buying → learn to read demand.
• Planning → learn to see capacity.
• Manufacturing → learn to protect delivery.
• Logistics → learn to hear the customer promise.
• Analytics → learn to serve the decision.
• Strategy → learn to know the frontline workflow.

The map is not a universal career ladder. It is a starting point for a better question: what happens to my work after it leaves my desk, and what would I need to understand to improve that hand-off?
