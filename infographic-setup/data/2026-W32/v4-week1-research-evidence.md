## 1. Decision Summary

The research changes the Week 1 plan in four material ways:

1. **An artifact is necessary but not sufficient.** Tiger's `AR4` decision/reference posts have a private median of 732 impressions, but recent copyable AI/procurement workflows sit near a 251-impression median. Several already made the tool and output clear and still did not earn reach.
2. **Broad professional decisions and named mechanisms are the strongest account signal.** The career map reached 176,508 impressions, the Maersk integration map 57,077, and the strategic/tactical/operational distinction 28,100. These are outliers, not causal proof, but they invalidate a narrow “make the artifact visible” diagnosis.
3. **The market pain is not a shortage of AI tips.** Current evidence repeatedly points to forecast-to-plan misalignment, manual reporting, logistics cost leakage, weak data/integration, unclear decision ownership, and technology that has not become a dependable operating workflow.
4. **The automated creator clusters cannot select topics.** Manual review of all 15 `screen_A` combinations found only two clean thematic convergences, seven salvageable rows, and six incoherent rows. Stage-0 remains a discovery layer; every admitted topic below is rebuilt from the source posts and fresh evidence.

The revised content thesis is therefore:

> Start with a consequential decision that a planner, buyer, or transformation leader already owns. Use AI only where it materially improves the workflow. Make the inputs, calculation, output, human owner, and failure boundary inspectable.

## 11. Revised Week 1 Experiment

- **Hypothesis:** when an artifact-led post opens with the consequential role decision rather than a named AI tool/task, Day-7 members reached and saves will improve against Tiger's matched recent tool-led workflows.
- **One variable:** opening frame — `role decision first` versus `named-tool task first`.
- **Held constant:** intended audience, static artifact visibility, practical workflow depth, publication maturity, and source mode where feasible.
- **Primary signal:** Day-7 members reached and saves, reported separately.
- **Guardrail:** qualified follows and comments from planners, buyers, and transformation leaders.
- **Decision rule:** directional only until the decision-first frame repeats across at least two comparable V4 posts.
- **What this can disprove:** that the low recent results were primarily caused by visual quality or insufficiently visible artifacts.

## 12. Research Limits

- Tiger's 50-row private baseline is ranked and deterministic, not a full experimental census.
- The top post creates 53.2% of impressions; single-post patterns are especially unstable.
- Public LinkedIn interactions are not impressions, saves, qualified follows, or artifact use.
- Top-100 is curated, mostly static, and affected by creator scale; it cannot prove format prevalence or causality.
- BCG, PwC, Deloitte, Gartner, ISM, and KPMG are self-reported surveys with large-company and/or sponsor effects.
- Public datasets demonstrate transparent methods; actual operating decisions require company data and practitioner judgment.
- No candidate may use first-person authority until Tiger's contribution and failure boundary pass the source gate.
