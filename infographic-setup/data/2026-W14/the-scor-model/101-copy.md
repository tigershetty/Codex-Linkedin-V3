## LinkedIn Caption

There are over 250 supply chain metrics in common use. SCOR organises them into 6 processes and 5 performance attributes. It's the closest thing supply chain has to a universal language, and it's been around since 1996.

This is Episode 8 of Supply Chain 101. We've spent the last few episodes building up the vocabulary of how supply chains plan, produce, and respond to demand. Today we're stepping back and looking at the whole picture, because SCOR is the framework that ties all of it together.

SCOR stands for Supply Chain Operations Reference.
It was developed in 1996 and it does something deceptively simple: it says every supply chain on earth, regardless of industry, performs the same six processes.
- Plan sits at the centre. It's the process that balances demand and supply and coordinates everything else
- Source is how you acquire what you need
- Make is how you transform it
- Deliver is how you get it to the customer
- Return handles anything that comes back
- Enable is the foundation underneath all five: the rules, data, technology, and people that keep the system running

On the measurement side, SCOR gives you five performance attributes to evaluate how well those processes work:
- Reliability (do you deliver what you promised?)
- Responsiveness (how fast?)
- Agility (can you flex when things change?)
- Cost (what does it cost to operate?)
- Asset Management (how efficiently do you use what you have?)

SCOR is misunderstood only as an academic exercise, but it's more of a benchmarking tool.

A supply chain manager at a food company and one at an electronics firm can compare reliability scores because the definitions are standardised. That comparability is what makes it powerful, and it's what most internal KPI dashboards lack.

If you had to pick one of those five attributes your company is weakest on, which would it be?
