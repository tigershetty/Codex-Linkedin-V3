## Caption (built on Hook 1 — swap the opening line if you pick another hook)

Fixed-Price, Cost-Plus, Time and Materials. They are not three prices. They are three answers to one question: who carries the cost when the work runs over?

This week on Supply Chain 101 we are staying inside the contract. The earlier post looked at what actually lives in one. This one is about how it is priced, because once the clauses are set, the pricing model is what quietly decides who absorbs the risk when costs move.

Here is the simple version. Imagine the same job, agreed at the same number, hit by the same surprise: it runs over budget. Three contracts, three very different outcomes.

• Fixed-Price: you agree one number up front and the supplier delivers for that, whatever happens. If costs rise, the supplier absorbs it, and you pay for that safety in a premium baked into the price from day one.

• Time and Materials: you pay for the hours worked and the materials used. When the job runs long, the bill grows with it, so the risk is shared, but it leans toward you.

• Cost-Plus: you reimburse the supplier's actual costs and add an agreed fee on top. If the work overruns, you carry it, and the supplier still earns their margin.

Here's the thing most people miss. The lowest number on paper is often the one that quietly hands you all the risk.

So if a key supplier contract ran 20% over next quarter, which pricing model are you on, and would that surprise land on you or on them?

## Verified facts used (definitional — no invented statistics)
- **Fixed-Price**: price agreed up front; the supplier absorbs overruns; the buyer pays a risk premium baked into the price.
- **Time & Materials**: buyer pays for hours worked + materials used; overrun risk is shared but leans to the buyer.
- **Cost-Plus**: buyer reimburses actual cost plus an agreed fee; the buyer absorbs overruns; the supplier's margin is protected.
