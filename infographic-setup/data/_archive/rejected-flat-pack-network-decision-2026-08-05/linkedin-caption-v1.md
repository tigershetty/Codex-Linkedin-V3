# LinkedIn Caption — Product Geometry Shapes Network Options

**Status:** Draft — research-led; requires Tiger’s caption approval before publication.

IKEA did not just redesign furniture. It redesigned the network around it.

Its packaging team works to make products as tight and flat as possible. Less space and better
stacking means more product per shipment, fewer lorries on the road, and one way of keeping prices
low. IKEA’s own phrase is better: it does not see the point in shipping air.

That logic still matters. But the sourcing move IKEA announced in December 2025 shows why packaging
efficiency does not settle the whole network decision.

Reuters reported that IKEA planned to source more products from US factories as it worked towards a
more resilient and responsive network in the Americas. Inter IKEA’s FY25 results also reported
higher sourcing costs, including tariffs, while transportation and logistics costs remained
volatile.

So when a sourcing discussion begins with “Which country should we buy from?”, the more useful
place to start is one level lower:

**Which SKUs still earn the distance—and which ones need their network re-tested?**

A practical re-test looks at:

- **Pack density:** how much value fits into the volume being moved?
- **Landed cost:** transport, duty, handling, and inventory together.
- **Volatility:** where is the category exposed now?
- **Capacity:** can a credible regional source actually support it?
- **Response time:** what would a faster reaction actually protect?

Product geometry starts shaping the network before procurement asks where to buy.

*Sources: IKEA, “Our packaging journey”; Reuters, 5 December 2025; Inter IKEA Group FY25 results.*
