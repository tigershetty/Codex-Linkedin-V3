## Three Developed Directions

Use three genuinely different families. Utility, visual idea, and narrative are a useful default,
not a quota.

### Direction A

- **Concept:** The Flat-Pack Paradox — forensic flat-pack atlas.
- **Reader promise:** See why a product design decision can be a network-design decision.
- **Opening:** “IKEA didn't just redesign furniture. It redesigned the network around it.”
- **Visual object and eye path:** Native title → one unbranded carton opened as a technical specimen
  → central physical hinge → calm long-haul container path on one side and responsive regional
  path on the other → a small engraved “re-test by SKU” cue.
- **Useful object:** Product design is a network variable; the caption supplies the six-question
  SKU re-test.
- **Caption arc:** IKEA's flat-pack rationale → reported US sourcing shift and FY25 cost context →
  no universal nearshoring rule → practical portfolio question.
- **Bridge:** A future public field guide only if readers ask for the detailed re-test.
- **Claim modes and support route:** Sourced IKEA packaging facts; Reuters-attributed company move;
  Inter IKEA FY25 cost context; editorial lens marked as a lens.
- **Why it is original:** The object itself forms the network. It is neither brand mimicry nor a
  country comparison map.

### Direction B

- **Concept:** The Network Hinge — a precision tension sculpture.
- **Reader promise:** Treat sourcing as a moving balance of SKU-level forces.
- **Opening:** “Nearshoring is not a country decision. It is a SKU decision.”
- **Visual object and eye path:** Hinge → five tension arms → one flat product → global or
  regional operating geometry.
- **Useful object:** The six decision inputs.
- **Caption arc:** Introduce IKEA as case, then explain the review lens.
- **Bridge:** Future field guide.
- **Claim modes and support route:** Same sourced context, but no visual company claim.
- **Why it is original:** A single studio sculpture makes the trade-off tactile.
- **Why it loses:** It may become a beautiful generic balance metaphor before it earns the IKEA
  story.

### Direction C

- **Concept:** The SKU Passport — portable sourcing field card.
- **Reader promise:** Turn one vague network decision into a reviewable SKU checklist.
- **Opening:** “The source-country question is usually asked one level too high.”
- **Visual object and eye path:** A product passport → six physical inspection stamps → a route
  decision.
- **Useful object:** A concise review checklist.
- **Caption arc:** The IKEA case is a short proof of why the checklist matters.
- **Bridge:** Ready for a downloadable field guide later.
- **Claim modes and support route:** Framework plus attributed case.
- **Why it is original:** It gives a familiar sourcing question a portable operating object.
- **Why it loses:** It risks reading as a polished framework card, exactly where the flat-pack story
  has more emotional and visual power.

## Selection

**Selected direction:** Direction A — The Flat-Pack Paradox / Forensic Flat-Pack Atlas.
**Why it wins:** It gives one famous but unbranded physical premise a real public evidence base,
then transforms it into an original object that planners, buyers, and leaders can recognise without
a grid of explanations.
**3-second problem / promise:** One carton has become two networks.
**10-second insight:** The product's physical geometry is changing the sourcing decision.
**30-second useful action:** Re-test the portfolio per SKU against cube, landed cost, duty,
volatility, capacity, and response-time value.
**Coherence rationale:** The attention, comprehension, utility, and bridge atoms all support the
same promise: make the hidden network consequence visible, then give the reader a way to act on it.
**What the caption adds:** The sourced IKEA context, the careful claim boundary, and the six-input
SKU lens.
**What the visual provides:** The working metaphor, the network contrast, and the memorable first
principle that product design is network design.
