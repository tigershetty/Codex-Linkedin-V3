# LinkedIn Caption v2 — One Source Country, Five Network Decisions

**Status:** Draft — research-led; requires Tiger’s final public-use approval before publication.

Two SKUs can leave the same country and still create two entirely different network decisions.

IKEA’s public packaging work makes the physical part of that easier to see. It aims to make products as tight and flat as possible, so more units fit into each shipment. That changes transport capacity and cost before the sourcing conversation has even started.

Then the conditions around the product move as well. Reuters reported in December 2025 that IKEA planned to source more products from US factories while working towards a more resilient and responsive network in the Americas. Inter IKEA’s FY25 results also reported higher sourcing costs, including tariffs, while transport and logistics costs remained volatile.

There is no ready-made local-versus-global answer in that. The useful review is whether each SKU still earns its existing network.

Put these six things on the same page:

- pack density / cube
- total landed cost
- duty and trade exposure
- demand and transport volatility
- credible capacity close to the market
- what a faster response would genuinely protect

A compact, stable SKU may still justify distance. A bulky, fragile, or time-sensitive neighbour can create a different set of trade-offs, even from the same source country.

That is the decision model in the visual. It is not a map of IKEA’s network.

*Sources: IKEA, “Our packaging journey”; Reuters, 5 December 2025; Inter IKEA Group FY25 financial results.*
