## 1. The signature decision

Shetty's Desk needs recognisability, but recognisability is not a repeated template. For resource posts that deserve a reusable working reference, the signature is **The Field Guide Notebook**:

> A premium, practical operating reference that helps a reader orient themselves, understand one relationship, and take the next sensible step.

It should feel like someone has taken the time to make difficult work easier to see and discuss. It should not feel like a consultant slide, an AI-generated dashboard, a children’s worksheet, or an attempt to imitate a successful infographic creator.

This family is intentionally narrow. It is a return-value device for a method, distinction, decision, map, evidence display, or operating check. Posts whose purpose is recognition, timely context, career reflection, personal judgment, a real scene, or visual intrigue retain the freedom to select another family.

## 2. What makes it recognisably Shetty's Desk

The signal is a **grammar of useful work**, not a fixed composition.

| Recurring principle | What the reader experiences | What it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| One lived work or learning moment | "This is relevant to a situation I recognise." | abstract educational wallpaper |
| A visible information spine | "I can see how to read this." | a dense wall of equally weighted cards |
| Truthful visual structure | "The shape tells me what kind of relationship this is." | decorative funnels, Venn diagrams, and maturity ladders |
| Deliberate annotation | "This note tells me where the rule changes or what to check." | false certainty and generic advice |
| Product-grade exactness | "I could actually use this in a meeting." | image-model text errors and pseudo-proof |
| Humane restraint | "It respects my attention." | overbranding, decorative clutter, and a corporate slide look |

The desired reaction is not "I recognise the template." It is "I recognise the care and can use this." Repetition should come from the quality of the reader experience, not a permanent top bar, footer, hero object, background gradient, illustration character, or card pattern.

## 3. Family usage matrix

| Use Field Guide Notebook when… | Select another visual family when… |
|---|---|
| the reader needs a check, map, method, reference, distinction, or decision aid | the post is a personal reflection, point of view, observation, or story |
| exact wording, labels, paths, formulae, or source notes are part of the value | a scene, metaphor, photo, or native illustration carries the meaning better |
| a structured object can answer a question in 30 seconds | the central value is a fast timely response with no enduring object |
| a boundary, exception, or owner is essential to safe use | the core thought is intentionally simple and more structure would dilute it |
| the object could be saved, shared, printed, or returned to | the audience needs one memorable sentence rather than a reference |

Field Guide is not a reward for a topic being "important." It earns the production effort only when its structure creates more value than a well-written caption or a simpler visual.

## 4. Fixed visual grammar

The following are fixed **behaviours**, not fixed surface decoration.

### 4.1 Hierarchy and eye path

- Start with a compact reader moment and direct promise; make it legible at mobile feed size.
- Establish one spatial spine before presenting supporting modules.
- Let the eye move from promise -> model -> decisive modules -> boundary -> next action.
- Use whitespace, line weight, alignment, and contrast to make importance obvious.
- Keep the logo absent or quietly owned; it cannot become the reader's entry point.

### 4.2 Information-bearing material

The "notebook" name names a working-reference posture, not a literal aesthetic. A material may be used only when it has a reader job:

| Material | Legitimate job | Not permitted as |
|---|---|---|
| margin / annotation | scope, exception, assumption, or verification note | decorative handwriting |
| tab / index | orienting a section or stage | a random colourful sticker |
| rule line / grid | grouping, alignment, or comparison | faux stationery texture |
| marker / pointer | locating a decision, owner, or constraint | a generic "look here" flourish |
| evidence stamp / source cue | separating verified fact, assumption, or field rule | a fake certification or proof claim |
| accent field | distinguishing risk, route, state, or source class | an arbitrary pastel card background |

No fake torn paper, tape, spiral binding, fake handwritten type, paper grain, or dramatic shadow may be added simply to signal "notebook." If it does not improve scanning, orientation, or trust, remove it.

### 4.3 Colour and type roles

The Figma master should use a small semantic palette, tested for contrast and legibility. The first implementation direction is a light paper/neutral field with deep ink, a decisive signal blue, and reserved status accents for verified/pass, attention/validate, and exception/risk. Exact hex values and font families belong in the Figma variable and text-style library after contrast review; they are not to be improvised post by post.

Colour must state something:

- **ink:** primary content and structure;
- **signal:** active route, selected decision, or primary takeaway;
- **verified:** supported fact, passed check, or confirmed state;
- **validate:** input, uncertainty, or check required;
- **exception:** risk, boundary, conflict, or escalation;
- **muted:** explanatory context only.

Typography has three jobs: command attention at glance depth, guide the scan, and preserve exact use at small size. It may be editorial, technical, or utilitarian depending on the argument, but it must never mimic the rounded handwriting, treatment, or typographic identity of a saved creator.

### 4.4 The exactness rule

Formulas, table labels, source notes, technical terms, units, calculations, and owned marks are created in a deterministic surface and proofed at export size. An image model may be used for a separate scene or material exploration; it is never the final typesetter for a type-heavy Field Guide.

## 5. Variable visual expression

The following must change when the argument changes:

- spatial model and reading direction;
- ratio of text, data, illustration, and real material;
- number and type of modules;
- accent selection by semantic meaning;
- degree of visual warmth, technicality, or editorial energy;
- use of a physical metaphor, photograph, source artifact, or diagram;
- still-only versus a motion companion.

For example, a supplier-decision guide may use a conditional route and exception callouts; a career map may use branching pathways; an evidence display may use aligned comparisons; a meeting method may use a deliberately spare staged check. None need look like the same page.

## 6. Semantic shape standard

Visual form is part of the claim. The family adopts these rules:

- **Flow / tree:** conditional sequence, ownership hand-off, or a path through a decision.
- **Timeline / staged path:** order or elapsed progression.
- **Stack:** dependency, hierarchy, or foundational order.
- **Spectrum / curve:** continuous trade-off or range.
- **Venn:** genuine intersection, not visual association.
- **Aligned chart / table:** quantitative comparison with common units and scale.
- **Small multiples:** repeated comparison of the same question.
- **Network / map:** relationships, routes, dependencies, or hand-offs.
- **Rings:** a true core-periphery relationship.

Reject a spatial model when it introduces hierarchy, chronology, certainty, comparison, or overlap that the content cannot support. A strong Field Guide sometimes uses no diagram at all.

## 7. Three-depth signature

Every Field Guide must succeed at all three reading depths:

1. **Glance:** the reader recognises the moment and promise in about three seconds.
2. **Scan:** the reader can understand the main model and its organisation in about ten seconds.
3. **Use:** the reader can find the next check, decision, exception, or conversation move in about thirty seconds.

This replaces the old instinct to make a title large and fill the rest of the canvas. The visible promise, navigation, and usable action are the signature—not a decorative cover treatment.

## 8. Field Guide anatomy

The final page normally includes:

~~~
reader moment + direct promise
        -> one semantic navigational spine
        -> 3–7 distinct operating modules
        -> boundary / exception / verification note
        -> practical next action
        -> concise provenance cue when the truth class needs one
~~~

Each element must have a different cognitive job. A field guide with eight identical cards is not a field guide; it is a formatted list.

## 9. Anti-copy boundary

The source corpus is used for creative mechanisms, never end-to-end expression. In particular, do not reproduce:

- Pierri's lavender/pastel colour treatment, boxed-grid composition, stylised handwriting, title tags, recurring balls, illustrated characters, or animation choreography;
- a saved creator's exact topic framing, copy, label vocabulary, slide order, illustration, graphic geometry, or distinctive brand device;
- the appearance of a real customer dashboard, certificate, implementation, or performance result when only an internal example or research inference exists;
- the historical Cobalt Grid, default 2.5D/isometric operating scene, permanent footer strip, or generic four-card dashboard as a shortcut to brand consistency.

Borrow only the atom needed for the reader promise—such as a delayed explanation, a useful comparison, a structured reference, or caption-to-visual division—and transform the subject, argument, visual object, material, and composition.

## 10. Motion and the family

Motion is a companion, not an identity marker. A Field Guide may animate only when it makes a specific relationship easier to follow:

- one route moves through a decision;
- a state changes after a check;
- a constraint appears in the relevant stage;
- comparable items regroup;
- an operating sequence becomes clearer through reveal.

The motion brief must state what changes and why. Opening and final frames remain the approved still; exact type, formulae, numbers, source cues, and marks stay correct; the final static hold is long enough to use the guide. Do not use animated balls, cursors, loops, or decorative movement just because a reference creator does.

Meaningful animated transitions may aid comprehension when they preserve object identity and guide the reader through a real change. That research does **not** establish that animation improves LinkedIn distribution, so motion results are measured as a Shetty's Desk cohort experiment rather than assumed.

## 11. Quality bar

Before a Field Guide is selected as the public visual, verify:

- **usefulness:** it contains one specific practical gain, not a collection of facts;
- **truth:** the visual form, evidence, terms, and claims agree;
- **comprehension:** it passes glance / scan / use at feed size;
- **craft:** every label, contrast choice, source cue, line, and crop remains readable;
- **restraint:** every visual material and colour has an information job;
- **originality:** it visibly belongs to Shetty's Desk without resembling a source creator end to end;
- **voice:** the caption makes the object more useful and sounds like Tiger only where approved source material supports that voice;
- **learning:** the package records creative atoms and 24-hour, 7-day, and 28-day measurement snapshots.

## 12. Production handoff

For exact type-heavy work, build and maintain the master in Figma. The master holds page geometry, variables, text styles, components, export presets, and an animation-ready semantic layer map. Each post package records the Figma URL/key, export source, selected version, and review result. A printable PDF or website artifact may follow when it adds utility; it is not mandatory.

The specialist package also records why the Field Guide family was selected and how visual and
caption divide their work. `field-guide-admission.json` is the concise `select / repair / kill`
record; `field-guide-visual-copy-choreography.json` names visual teaching units, caption additions,
and deliberate repeats. `field-guide-manifest.json` binds those records to the exact Figma
file/node/version, local export/hash, claims, references, review, caption/hook, and analytics. A
recorded URL is not an exported or verified design.

The core package is:

~~~
field-guide-spec.json
field-guide-admission.json
field-guide-visual-copy-choreography.json
field-guide-manifest.json
field-guide-review.md
claim ledger appropriate to truth class
Figma master and exact export
caption.md
analytics.md
optional motion brief + static/GIF/MP4 evidence
~~~

## 13. Measurement guardrail

The family optimises for broad reach *and* return value, but it never assumes that saves, GIFs, or any external creator benchmark will deliver either. At 24 hours, 7 days, and 28 days, compare matched posts for impressions/reached members, relevant follower growth and profile visits, saves/sends, comments that indicate use, resource uptake, and return use. Treat every result as a transfer signal for the specific creative atoms, reader moment, and truth class—not a permanent rule.

## 14. Research foundation

- Pierri's public method and the supplied corpus inform resource design, but not a claim about the LinkedIn algorithm: [idea mapping](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/vincent-angelo-coach_you-how-do-i-find-ideas-for-viral-content-activity-7462502766175870977-No4E), [resource filters](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/vincent-angelo-coach_there-are-4-criteria-to-make-a-post-to-go-activity-7442579272290140160-roLR), [deep/wide framework development](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/vincent-angelo-coach_last-week-i-helped-will-go-viral-with-this-activity-7465764970631282688-d5_Y).
- [Tufte's *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*](https://www.edwardtufte.com/book/the-visual-display-of-quantitative-information/) informs information integrity and relational clarity, not a social visual style.
- [Cleveland and McGill](https://faculty.washington.edu/aragon/classes/hcde411/w13/readings/cleveland84.pdf) supports perceptually honest quantitative comparisons.
- [Bateman et al.](https://vis.csail.mit.edu/classes/6.859/readings/pdfs/Bateman-UsefulJunk.pdf), [Borkin et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24051797/), and [Heer & Robertson](https://idl.uw.edu/papers/animated-transitions) inform the balance between memorable treatment, comprehension, and meaningful movement.

The Field Guide Notebook is therefore a design hypothesis with disciplined evidence requirements, not a claim that one visual style is universally superior.
