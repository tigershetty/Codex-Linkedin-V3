## 1. Decision distinction — Forecasting vs Planning vs Buying

**What is actually working**

This post solves a vocabulary problem. In three seconds, the reader sees three related functions laid
out in equal vertical lanes and understands they are not interchangeable. The design does not attempt
to look clever; its proof is that every lane answers the same practical questions, so comparison costs
almost no effort.

**The engine underneath**

| Moment | Reader experience | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 seconds | “Those are different jobs, not synonyms.” | Three big labels and equal columns |
| 3–10 seconds | “I can explain the boundary.” | Same schema repeated across each lane |
| 10–30 seconds | “I can use this in the next cross-functional discussion.” | Shared hand-off failure and financial consequence |

**Why the caption matters**

The image separates the jobs; the caption turns separation into an operational tension: the forecast
changes, the plan lags, and the purchase order already exists. That is additive—not a transcript of
the graphic.

**Creative Genome guidance—not a topic recommendation**

Retrieve this mechanism whenever an opportunity involves **two to four concepts people confuse**, the
reader needs a shared vocabulary, and each concept can answer the same decision questions. It is not
right for an evolving story, a root-cause analysis, or an argument that needs a causal timeline. The
open variables are the topic, concepts, comparison rows, reader, consequence and material treatment;
none is selected by this record.

## 2. Recognition post — Supply-chain roles as birds

**What is actually working**

This is not a framework post. It earns attention through benign workplace recognition. The reader
first looks for their own role, gets a small laugh, then scans the rest of the team. The illustrations
are functional because they turn a familiar behaviour into a character that is easy to remember and
safe to tag.

**The engine underneath**

| Moment | Reader experience | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 seconds | “What bird am I?” | Unexpected category collision: supply-chain roles + birds |
| 3–10 seconds | “That is uncomfortably accurate.” | Repeated role -> character -> behaviour reveals |
| 10–30 seconds | “I should send this to my colleague.” | Affectionate, specific cross-functional recognition |

**Why the caption matters**

The image delivers the joke. The caption establishes the shared mission and gives people an easy way
to participate with a comment, tag, or alternate mapping.

**Creative Genome guidance—not a topic recommendation**

Retrieve this mechanism when the opportunity needs **recognition before instruction**: readers share a
role, identity, repeated moment, or tension that can be safely exaggerated through an original
metaphor. Do not use it to carry a technical definition, a sensitive criticism, or a claim that needs
evidence. The open variables are the group, metaphor, behaviour, number of archetypes, humour level
and participation prompt; none is selected by this record.

## 3. Practical proof — Spaghetti diagram before/after

**What is actually working**

This post makes the hidden problem visible before teaching it. The red, tangled routes turn waste into
something readers can see instantly. Only after that visual discovery does it deliver method, evidence
and the human insight that teams act differently when they can observe the real flow together.

**The engine underneath**

| Moment | Reader experience | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 seconds | “That process is a mess.” | A chaotic route map physically proves the headline |
| 3–10 seconds | “I can see how the redesign changes the flow.” | Same scene, before/after, one consistent contrast system |
| 10–30 seconds | “I could run this observation locally.” | Supporting method, evidence boundary, and action path |

**Why the caption matters**

The visual gives the discovery. The caption gives the narrative of why simple observation changes a
team conversation and contains the creator-attributed outcomes. Those outcomes remain unverified for
V4 use unless independently sourced.

**Creative Genome guidance—not a topic recommendation**

Retrieve this mechanism when a chosen opportunity has an **invisible but observable failure**, a true
before/after state, and enough input to show what changed. It is wrong for a simple definition,
speculation without a traceable scenario, or a case where the before state cannot be depicted. The open
variables are the system, visible failure, shared scene, intervention, evidence depth, and action path;
none is selected by this record.
