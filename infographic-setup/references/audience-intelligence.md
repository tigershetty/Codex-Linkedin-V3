## Audience Thesis

Shetty's Desk should first serve working supply-chain planners and planning managers,
purchasing/procurement practitioners, and supply-chain transformation leaders who want
faster, more defensible decisions in Excel, ERP, planning systems, and recurring operating
meetings.

They are hearing broad claims about AI but rarely receive a practical pathway from a real
work problem to the required inputs, method, artifact, validation, and human-owned decision.
They do not need more definitions or generic AI lists. They need useful evidence, a method
they can inspect, and language they can defend in a meeting.

Early-career professionals and cross-functional colleagues remain valuable secondary reach
audiences. They do not override the primary practitioner when topics are selected.

## Primary Segments

| Priority | Segment | What they are trying to do | What they save or use | What they ignore |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Planners and planning managers | Convert uncertain demand, supply, inventory, and capacity inputs into an explainable decision | formulas, diagnostic models, scenario boards, review packs, Excel/ERP workflows, validation checks | definitions, dashboard decoration, AI demos without operating inputs |
| 1 | Purchasing and procurement practitioners | Compare suppliers, defend cost/risk choices, reduce manual work, and move from transactions to better decisions | sourcing models, TCO/should-cost logic, supplier decision maps, checklists, public-data workflows | vague strategic-procurement advice and vendor-first content |
| 1 | SCM transformation leaders | Translate AI, data, systems, and process change into safe adoption and measurable operating value | implementation maps, operating models, control gates, maturity tests, adoption evidence, artifact packages | AI hype, software feature tours, unsupported savings claims |
| 2 | Early-career SC professionals | Understand the function, grow faster, and speak with confidence | career maps, role ladders, concept maps, meeting language | generic motivation and executive jargon |
| 2 | Cross-functional business peers | Understand why supply chain requests data, protects flexibility, or resists a change | plain-English consequence maps and simple systems diagrams | deep acronyms and tool detail without business consequence |

## Audience Jobs To Serve

Every topic should serve at least one job:

| Job | Reader thought | Content shape |
|---|---|---|
| Explain this clearly | "I need to explain this to someone who does not get supply chain." | simple system diagram |
| Make a better decision | "Which option should we choose and why?" | trade-off map |
| Avoid a mistake | "Where does this usually break?" | failure-mode map |
| Look sharper at work | "What would a senior person know that I do not?" | maturity ladder / decision map |
| Save time | "Can I use this in my next workflow?" | template, prompt, checklist |
| Understand AI safely | "Can this tool really do this, and how do I verify it?" | workflow + verification step |

## Evidence Signals To Watch

V4 uses public evidence rather than requiring practitioner interviews or private validation.

| Signal | What qualifies | What does not qualify |
|---|---|---|
| Audience pain | a concrete decision, recurring task, failure, cost, delay, risk, or career pressure visible in public practitioner material | a broad trend with no role or consequence |
| Near-peer evidence | a mechanic that repeats above comparable creators' own mature baselines | raw likes from one large account |
| Curated-success evidence | a Top-100 case or other known-success reference with clear transfer logic | frequency in the reference set treated as causal proof |
| Public proof path | official data, standards, peer-reviewed research, formula/model, documented method, or transparent worked example | unattributed statistics or AI-generated claims |
| Artifact feasibility | an inspectable calculator, workbook, dashboard, decision board, map, checklist, benchmark, or validated workflow | a decorative visual or generic prompt list |
| Timely relevance | a verified event, regulation, company move, market signal, or tool change that alters a practitioner decision | generic AI news with no supply-chain implication |

## High-Value Topic Premises

Use these as topic-shaping lenses:

- "The thing everyone measures is not the thing that decides the outcome."
- "The role thinks it owns the decision, but the constraint sits somewhere else."
- "The metric looks good while the system is getting worse."
- "The lowest price quietly moves the risk to you."
- "The meeting exists because the pre-work failed."
- "The AI workflow is useful only if the input data is structured and the human verifies the output."
- "The career jump happens when you stop doing the analysis and start framing the decision."

## What Makes Tiger Credible

Tiger's credibility is strongest when the post uses:

- practical purchasing/replenishment context,
- team and role-level specificity,
- operational trade-offs,
- realistic constraints,
- honest AI limits,
- a manager's view of how work actually moves through people and systems.

Public research can strengthen these points, but it cannot invent Tiger's experience. A
flagship first-person piece needs an approved Tiger source note stating his real judgment,
failure boundary, first check, human owner, and uncertainty.

## Minimum Audience Test

Before a topic proceeds, answer:

1. Who exactly is this for?
2. What decision, recurring task, failure, cost, risk, or ambition do they recognize?
3. What public evidence shows that the subject deserves attention?
4. What will they understand, decide, or do differently?
5. What artifact or reusable takeaway could they inspect or use?
6. Which channel has a real job: LinkedIn, Substack, website, or a deliberate combination?
7. What can Tiger genuinely add, and is the Tiger source note available?

If these answers are weak, the topic is not ready.

## Optional Curated-Success Packaging Test

After the audience test, use `top100-reference-intelligence.md` only when it materially improves
the promise or information structure:

1. If a curated-success reference is useful, which caption promise does this topic adapt? Otherwise record `not used` and name the stronger evidence lane.
2. Which curated-winner format hypothesis makes the idea easier to understand?
3. Why would the audience save the image, not just like it?
4. What task, meeting, or career moment does the reference mechanic map to?
5. What is the Shetty's Desk originality layer?

The reference supplies a mechanic hypothesis, not proof that the mechanic caused reach. Stage 0
recovered an overwhelmingly single-image Top-100 sample, so it cannot decide the native format.
The best topics combine audience pain, public evidence, buildable utility, Tiger's real judgment,
and packaging worth testing on Tiger's account—even when no Top-100 reference is needed.
