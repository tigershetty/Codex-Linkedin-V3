## Audience Thesis

Shetty's Desk should serve supply-chain professionals who want to become sharper operators, better managers, and more credible cross-functional voices.

The audience is not just looking for definitions. They want the line that helps them explain a trade-off in a meeting, the framework that makes their job easier, and the mental model that makes them look one level more senior.

## Primary Segments

| Segment | What they are trying to do | What they save | What they ignore |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early-career SC professionals | Understand the field, grow faster, speak with confidence | career maps, ladders, concept maps, "how the function works" explainers | generic thought leadership, executive jargon |
| Practitioners | Solve recurring work friction in procurement, planning, logistics, inventory | checklists, templates, decision rules, formulas, meeting-ready diagrams | high-level definitions with no action |
| Managers / senior managers | Explain trade-offs, align teams, defend decisions to leadership | operating models, risk/cost frameworks, decision maps, escalation logic | tool demos without business consequence |
| Non-SC business peers | Understand why supply chain makes certain requests or says no | plain-English analogies, consequence maps, simple systems diagrams | deep acronyms, vendor/tool detail |
| AI-curious SC teams | Build current AI execution systems for real workflows | role plugins, reusable skills, connected context, worker maps, control gates, artifact packages, schedules, and human ownership | ordinary-chat tutorials presented as advanced, hype, generic AI lists, unsourced productivity claims |

## Audience Jobs To Serve

Every topic should serve at least one job:

| Job | Reader thought | Content shape |
|---|---|---|
| Explain this clearly | "I need to explain this to someone who does not get supply chain." | simple system diagram |
| Make a better decision | "Which option should we choose and why?" | trade-off map |
| Avoid a mistake | "Where does this usually break?" | failure-mode map |
| Look sharper at work | "What would a senior person know that I do not?" | maturity ladder / decision map |
| Save time | "Can I use this in my next workflow?" | template, prompt, checklist |
| Understand AI safely | "Can this tool really do this, and how do I verify it?" | workflow + verification step |

## High-Value Topic Premises

Use these as topic-shaping lenses:

- "The thing everyone measures is not the thing that decides the outcome."
- "The role thinks it owns the decision, but the constraint sits somewhere else."
- "The metric looks good while the system is getting worse."
- "The lowest price quietly moves the risk to you."
- "The meeting exists because the pre-work failed."
- "The AI workflow is useful only if the input data is structured and the human verifies the output."
- "The career jump happens when you stop doing the analysis and start framing the decision."

## What Makes Tiger Credible

Tiger's credibility is strongest when the post uses:

- practical purchasing/replenishment context,
- team and role-level specificity,
- operational trade-offs,
- realistic constraints,
- honest AI limits,
- a manager's view of how work actually moves through people and systems.

The content should sound like someone who has sat in the meeting, not someone summarizing a textbook.

## Minimum Audience Test

Before a topic proceeds, answer:

1. Who exactly is this for?
2. What will they understand or do differently after reading?
3. Why would they save it?
4. What meeting, task, or career moment does it help with?
5. What can Tiger add that a generic AI account cannot?

If these answers are weak, the topic is not ready.
