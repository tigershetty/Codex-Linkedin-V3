# V5 Field Guide Notebook — Production System

**Status:** specialist production authority for a Shetty's Desk resource post or durable working reference.

**Purpose:** make an operational idea easier to recognise, discuss, and use. The reader should leave with a compact working reference, not a decorative summary of a concept they already know.

This specialist route does not replace the Fast Post Loop, Creative Genome, Tiger voice, or the requirement to select a visual family deliberately.

## 1. The decision

The Field Guide Notebook is a **dedicated resource-post family**, not the Shetty's Desk default. Use it when a reader benefits from keeping a structured reference at hand: a decision sequence, distinction, check, operating method, evidence display, or recurring meeting aid.

It is not the right answer merely because a post is educational. A timely reaction, a relatable work moment, a career reflection, a visual metaphor, or a simple point of view may need a different creative family and a much lighter production route.

> One real reader moment + one truthful spatial model + a compact usable reference = a reason to stop, understand, save, and return.

This is a hypothesis to test with Shetty's Desk cohorts. It is not a platform-performance fact and does not imply that every useful post needs a field guide.

## 2. What transfers from Pierri — and what does not

Vincent Pierri's corpus is useful source intelligence for how a dense visual can become a usable resource: a clear problem, high information density, a dominant spatial model, and a caption that adds context rather than reading the visual aloud. His method is an input to the Creative Genome, not a visual skin or universal content law.

Transfer these mechanisms:

- map broad expertise into smaller, reusable reference objects;
- make the reader's immediate pain or decision visible before styling it;
- use one strong visual structure to make dense information scannable;
- divide labour between visual (the object) and caption (context, limitations, judgment, or next move);
- use motion only where it reveals an actual sequence, state change, relationship, or route.

Reject these shortcuts:

- assuming a creator's save-rate benchmark, hook formula, or animation preference is a LinkedIn rule without account-level evidence;
- treating "actionable" as a requirement for every career, timely, identity, or perspective post;
- using a pyramid, Venn, funnel, ring, spectrum, or flowchart as a decorative container;
- reproducing Pierri's pastel/lavender palette, handwriting, boxy module treatment, animated balls, phrasing, or composition;
- making an infographic look like a workbook when no reader action exists.

Edward Tufte's work is useful here as a comprehension discipline, not a social-media growth guide: show relationships honestly, keep the data-bearing signal legible, use small multiples only for the same question, and do not use visual ornament to disguise weak information. Controlled perception research adds a practical constraint: use position and aligned length before area, angle, or 3D for quantitative comparisons. A distinct physical metaphor can improve recall, but only when it does not obscure the underlying information.

## 3. Admission: when a Field Guide earns production

Start with the reader, not the framework name. The direction passes when all of the following are true:

1. **Recognisable moment** — a planner, buyer, logistics practitioner, transformation leader, or broader learner can recognise the situation without a long preamble.
2. **One useful promise** — the visual helps them decide, check, explain, compare, or prepare for one thing.
3. **Truthful structure** — the central relationship has a spatial form that expresses what is true.
4. **Return value** — a reader could plausibly save, screenshot, reuse, or send the object without the caption.
5. **Bounded claim** — the content can say exactly what it knows, what it proposes, and where it stops.

Do **not** use this family when the essential value is a personal story, a single sharp observation, a current event that needs speed, an illustrative metaphor, or an argument that has no reusable structure. Select another family; do not force a field guide to justify its existence.

### Four adapted resource filters

Use these as prompts for resource posts, not gates for the whole editorial portfolio:

| Filter | Production question | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|
| Lived tension | What moment will the reader recognise? | Generic title with no operating or career moment |
| Practical handle | What can the reader do, check, or explain differently? | Advice that only says "be strategic" or "use AI" |
| Useful angle | What does this make clearer than the familiar explanation? | A relabelled textbook definition |
| Information density | Does every module earn its space? | More labels but no more decisions, distinctions, or actions |

These filters do **not** claim that low competition or high information density causes reach. They help us design an object worth keeping.

## 4. Claim truth classes

Choose the truth class before choosing visual treatment. A post can contain more than one class, but one must be primary and each secondary claim carries its own support burden.

| Truth class | What the guide may do | Minimum internal record | It must not imply |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Framework** | organise a concept, distinction, role map, or shared language | attribution where applicable; scope; failure boundary | a proven outcome or universal law |
| **Operating method** | give a sequence, checklist, decision rule, or meeting aid | correct logic; owner; inputs; exception and next action | that it has been deployed successfully without a documented case |
| **Evidence display** | show sourced quantities, a comparison, pattern, or timeline | source, date, unit, scope, calculation and limitation | precision or causality beyond the evidence |
| **Case or event** | explain a documented company, public event, deployment, or failure | primary/credible source; reporting party; case-specific limitation | that one case generalises to every operation |
| **Hypothesis or simulation** | make an assumption visible and testable | label, inputs, method, and uncertainty | that a fixture or modelled result is customer proof |
| **Tiger judgment** | offer an explicitly sourced professional interpretation | approved Tiger source record and judgment framing | invented experience, employer context, or personal result |

If support does not match wording, narrow the wording or change the truth class. Do not add synthetic numbers, customer-looking screenshots, or vague "research shows" copy to make the guide feel more authoritative.

## 5. Semantic shape rule: the shape is a claim

The spatial model must make the relationship more truthful, not merely more attractive. Select it after the reader promise and truth class are clear.

| If the central claim is… | Prefer… | Do not use it to imply… |
|---|---|---|
| a conditional decision or hand-off | decision tree / flowchart | a universal linear process |
| a sequence through time or work stages | timeline / staged path | that all work has a fixed duration or no iteration |
| a hierarchy, dependency, or load-bearing order | stack / layered system | that a hierarchy is automatically a maturity model |
| a trade-off across a continuous range | spectrum / curve | discrete categories where none exist |
| genuine overlap between two or three conditions | Venn / overlap map | superficial association or a complete taxonomy |
| comparison of measurable values | aligned table, dot plot, or bar chart on a common scale | accuracy from area, angle, perspective, or decorative 3D |
| a repeated check across comparable conditions | small multiples / repeated modules | false equivalence between unlike cases |
| a network of dependencies, routes, or ownership | node-link map / route map | a single required sequence |
| a role-to-decision-language lookup without verified receiver ownership | asymmetric translator atlas | a fake operating network or required sequence |
| a core-periphery relationship | rings / concentric layers | temporal progression or priority where it is absent |
| a role/career/operating landscape | map with explicit pathways and boundaries | a guaranteed path or complete universe |

If no spatial model clarifies the claim, use editorial typography, an annotated real artifact, or a lighter post family. Do not force a shape.

## 6. Three reading depths

Every Field Guide has three intentional layers. This is how it stays useful without becoming a wall of text.

| Depth | Reader asks | Design job | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Glance** (about 3 seconds) | "Is this about my situation, and why should I stop?" | reader moment, promise, dominant shape, clear entry point | a practitioner can name topic and payoff at feed size |
| **Scan** (about 10 seconds) | "What is the model and how is it organised?" | navigational spine, hierarchy, 3–7 modules, visible relationships | a reader can restate the central model without the caption |
| **Use** (about 30 seconds) | "What do I do with this in my next decision or conversation?" | check, boundary, exception, owner, or next move | a reader can identify an action and when not to use it |

The title is not the entire glance layer; it works with a visual cue that makes the problem recognisable. The use layer is not a forced CTA; it is an honest next move inside the object.

## 7. Anatomy of a working reference

Build the guide in this order. The final layout can vary, but every component needs a job.

1. **Reader moment and promise** — name the situation and one outcome; avoid abstract labels such as "Supply Chain Strategy Framework" unless the situation does the work.
2. **Navigational spine** — choose one honest path, comparison, map, hierarchy, or decision frame.
3. **Three to seven operating modules** — each module answers a distinct question. It may be a check, decision, input, signal, role, trade-off, or evidence point. Fewer modules are better than an artificial seven.
4. **Boundary / exception / field note** — identify when the simple rule breaks, what must be verified, or who owns escalation. This protects the reader from false certainty.
5. **Practical next action** — a meeting question, a check to run, an input to collect, or a small experiment. It must follow from the guide rather than advertise the author.
6. **Provenance cue when needed** — concise source or method note for factual, calculated, case, or AI-capability claims. Keep it legible; do not turn the visual into a bibliography.

The visual carries the reusable object. The caption adds only what the object cannot carry cleanly: context, why it matters, caveat, Tiger's approved view, worked application, source depth, or a related resource.

## 8. Visual grammar: fixed discipline, variable expression

The family should become recognisable through behaviour, not by repeating a skin.

### Fixed discipline

- **Product-grade information hierarchy:** a reader can find the promise, spine, modules, boundary, and action without hunting.
- **One primary reading route:** titles, rules, labels, and whitespace guide the eye rather than compete for it.
- **Material only when it informs use:** a tab means a section, a margin note means a caveat, a rule line groups related information, and an annotation pinpoints a decision. Faux paper, torn tape, fake handwriting, or random shadows have no place without a job.
- **Semantic colour:** colour distinguishes state, confidence, source type, priority, risk, or path; it never exists merely to decorate a module.
- **Exact information:** formulas, figures, source notes, official marks, and technical labels are deterministic and proofed. No image model is the final typesetter.
- **Editorial restraint:** no default dashboard-card grid, dark Cobalt Grid, 2.5D operating scene, permanent footer, or oversized logo lock-up.

### Variable expression

- spatial model and eye path;
- information density, scale, and number of modules;
- physical metaphor or material cue, where it supports comprehension;
- type pairing, accent colour, image or illustration treatment;
- social image, Figma master, printable PDF, web artifact, or motion companion.

The selected spatial model, not a preset layout, decides the composition. A comparison might be an aligned table; a constraint may need a route map; a meeting check might need a deliberately spare sequence. Repeating a cover, colourway, card shape, or title bar is not a substitute for signature.

## 9. Production surface: exactness before novelty

Use the renderer that preserves the reader value:

| Output need | Preferred surface | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| exact type, labels, formulae, paths, and repeatable module system | **Figma master** | deterministic layout, reusable components, inspectable source, and motion handoff |
| printable or downloadable working reference | Figma export or document/PDF derived from the master | durable, exact, and readable offline |
| data calculation or editable decision model | spreadsheet / calculator | the model, inputs, and formulas are the value |
| interaction or repeated filtering | website UI | a reader gains something a still cannot provide |
| scene, object, or unusual visual metaphor | image engine as a separate native image | composition and material are the value, not exact dense text |

GPT Image or another image engine can explore a physical material, a mock-up, or a conceptual scene. It must not create the final type-heavy Field Guide. Finish exact working references in Figma or another deterministic surface, and preserve the source file and export path in the post package.

## 10. Motion eligibility

Motion is opt-in and remains subordinate to the still. Before storyboarding, complete this sentence:

> Motion helps because the reader needs to see **[specific sequence, state change, path, regrouping, or cause-and-effect]** in **[this reading order]**.

If the sentence is weak, export a still only. Good candidates include a decision path resolving, inputs moving through a check, a constraint becoming visible, or a before/after regrouping. Do not animate titles, decorative objects, every module, or numbers simply to look alive.

When motion is justified:

- opening and final frame are the approved complete still;
- keep simultaneously moving information clusters small (normally no more than three or four);
- preserve a readable static hold at the end;
- keep text, exact values, and logos locked unless their reveal itself communicates something true;
- export a still companion and record why motion earned the additional complexity.

This follows the general evidence that meaningful staged transitions can preserve object identity and help a reader follow change; it is not evidence that GIFs outperform static LinkedIn posts.

## 11. Required package and QA

For a Field Guide, add these artefacts to the selected standard or flagship package:

~~~
field-guide-spec.json
claim-ledger.md                         # only as deep as the truth class requires
field-guide-review.md
figma-master.md                         # file URL/key, export path, component/version record
visual.{png|pdf}                        # selected public master
caption.md
analytics.md
motion-brief.md                         # only if motion passes eligibility
~~~

### Review sequence

1. **Promise check:** reader, moment, and one promised payoff are visible without the caption.
2. **Semantic check:** the selected shape makes the claimed relationship clearer and does not imply a stronger relationship than the support allows.
3. **Glance / scan / use check:** pass the 3-second, 10-second, and 30-second tests with real mobile viewing conditions.
4. **Truth check:** every number, named case, causal phrase, tool capability, formula, and first-person statement meets its truth-class requirement.
5. **Reader safety check:** an exception, boundary, verification step, or owner appears where the guide could otherwise create false certainty.
6. **Craft check:** line weight, colour contrast, hierarchy, labels, crop, and source note remain legible at LinkedIn size and in a static export.
7. **Originality check:** the output borrows mechanics but cannot be mistaken for a Pierri post or any one saved source.
8. **Caption check:** caption adds operating context or judgment; it does not narrate the diagram.
9. **Motion check:** only if eligible; review its static counterpart and every meaningful transition.

Hard failures:

- decorative geometry that misstates the relationship;
- modules that contain labels but no independent decision, check, distinction, or evidence;
- evidence-looking visuals built from synthetic or untraceable numbers;
- a generic dashboard/card grid substituted for an information model;
- an aesthetic copy of a source creator;
- a beautiful guide with no boundary or usable next move;
- animation whose only job is visual surprise.

## 12. Measurement and learning

Measure the Field Guide against its declared job, not against a borrowed creator benchmark. Preserve 24-hour, 7-day, and 28-day snapshots and connect them to the exact creative atoms and truth class.

| Job | Primary signal | Explanatory signals |
|---|---|---|
| recognition / reach | impressions and reached members | profile visits, audience relevance sample |
| practical return value | saves, sends, downloads, starts, completions | comments that describe use, repeat visits |
| trust / relationship | relevant followers, replies, repeat readers | Substack visits or voluntary subscriptions |
| durable artifact | return use and completion | website visits, tool starts, feedback |

Compare posts at matched age and, where possible, within comparable format/topic cohorts. A save rate, GIF result, or one high-performing post is an account-specific learning signal, not a platform law. Record an atom-level result as transferred, unclear, or did_not_transfer; do not rewrite the source reference's positive creative signal.

## 13. Research basis and limits

These sources shape the system at different levels of confidence:

- **Pierri method / corpus:** a creative-method source. Its useful design principles are adapted as prompts, not taken as verified platform causal claims. See the local references/Vincent Pierri Reference corpus and Pierri's public posts on [idea mapping](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/vincent-angelo-coach_you-how-do-i-find-ideas-for-viral-content-activity-7462502766175870977-No4E), [resource filters](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/vincent-angelo-coach_there-are-4-criteria-to-make-a-post-to-go-activity-7442579272290140160-roLR), and [framework depth](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/vincent-angelo-coach_last-week-i-helped-will-go-viral-with-this-activity-7465764970631282688-d5_Y).
- **Information design:** [Edward Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*](https://www.edwardtufte.com/book/the-visual-display-of-quantitative-information/) informs truthful relationships, small multiples, and data-bearing signal; it is not social-platform performance evidence.
- **Quantitative perception:** [Cleveland and McGill (1984)](https://faculty.washington.edu/aragon/classes/hcde411/w13/readings/cleveland84.pdf) supports the preference for aligned position/length over area, angle, and perspective when readers compare quantities.
- **Memorability versus comprehension:** [Bateman et al., "Useful Junk?"](https://vis.csail.mit.edu/classes/6.859/readings/pdfs/Bateman-UsefulJunk.pdf) and [Borkin et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24051797/) support testing distinctive visual treatment without sacrificing readability; neither licenses ornamental clutter.
- **Motion:** [Heer and Robertson](https://idl.uw.edu/papers/animated-transitions) informs meaningful staged transitions. It does not establish an algorithmic advantage for animated LinkedIn posts.

## 14. Implementation checklist

Before production, the creative owner can answer all of these in one page:

- What exact moment is the reader in?
- What can they identify, decide, check, or explain after 30 seconds?
- What truth class governs the main claim?
- Which shape makes that relationship truthful, and why?
- What belongs to the visual versus the caption?
- What is fixed in the Field Guide grammar, and what is intentionally variable for this argument?
- What boundary prevents false certainty?
- Why is the selected production surface the most accurate one?
- If motion is proposed, what specific change does it let the reader see?
- Which 24-hour, 7-day, and 28-day signals will test this particular bet?

If the answers are weak, step back to Creative Genome retrieval or choose another visual family.
