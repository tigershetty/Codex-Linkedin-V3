# V5 Working Infographic System — High-Density Resource Graphics

**Status:** active V5 production authority for a saveable diagram, framework, map, comparison,
diagnostic, or decision object.

**Purpose:** turn a real supply-chain tension into a visual thought object that a stranger can
understand quickly, explore deeply, and return to at work.

This is the corrective to the earlier V5 Field Guide default. A working infographic is not a warm
instruction sheet, a generic card grid, or an image-model scene with labels on top. It is a
high-density editorial diagram whose shape carries the argument.

## 1. The change

Vincent Pierri's supplied corpus is the strongest current reference for **resource-graphic
mechanics**: an outcome people recognise, a precise micro-problem, one dominant spatial argument,
dense but navigable detail, and a caption that does a different job from the graphic.

It is a creative benchmark, not a formula, identity transplant, or LinkedIn algorithm rule. We
borrow the level of editorial judgement and useful density; we do not copy his marker lettering,
pastel skin, moving balls, client proof, layout proportions, or particular composition.

The active production distinction is:

```text
Field Guide Notebook = one precise, repeat-use artifact family
Working Infographic = the broader high-density visual-framework system
```

Field Guide remains available when exact operational reference is the best answer. It is no longer
the default visual posture for saveable content.

## 2. When to use this route

Select a Working Infographic when the reader needs to identify, compare, navigate, diagnose,
prepare, or explain something that becomes materially easier when it is spatial.

For a resource graphic, begin with Pierri's useful **narrow → deep → wide** move:

1. Find a small, real work moment rather than a broad topic.
2. Go deep enough to expose variables, conditions, examples, decisions, or failure modes that a
   generic article would miss.
3. Phrase the payoff broadly enough that a capable stranger can recognise why it matters.

Use the following four filters as prompts for resource graphics, never as a veto against broad
career, recognition, timely, or perspective posts:

| Prompt | Strong answer | Weak answer |
|---|---|---|
| Lived tension | A reader can name the moment they are in. | "Supply chain is important." |
| Immediate handle | The image changes a question, check, or conversation. | A relabelled definition. |
| Useful angle | It makes a familiar issue more precise or usable. | Familiar advice in new colours. |
| Earned density | Extra detail removes uncertainty or supports use. | More labels without more value. |

## 3. Select the visual argument, not a skin

Develop three **semantic containers** before the finish route. They must make different truth
claims, not merely change palette or headline.

| The argument is really about… | Choose a thought object | Do not force it into… |
|---|---|---|
| a conditional next move | decision tree, path, diagnostic | a linear list that hides branches |
| comparable alternatives | shared-axis comparison or small multiples | unequal cards or a fake spectrum |
| a trade-off or range | spectrum, slope, two-axis field | bins that pretend to be continuous |
| connected owners / hand-offs | map, route, network, swimlane | a circle or hub without real edges |
| nested dependencies | stack, layers, cutaway, ladder | a hierarchy when order is unproven |
| a repeatable sequence | workflow, calendar, progressive reveal | a map that disguises sequence |
| an orienting body of knowledge | taxonomy, constellation, annotated landscape | an unscoped directory |
| measurements | chart, small multiples, table with graphical cues | ornamental geometry, 3D depth, or invented values |

The shape is a claim. If it gives the reader a false sense of order, precision, overlap, or
causality, change the shape.

## 4. The high-density anatomy

A Working Infographic normally contains these jobs; the layout decides their form.

1. **Reader outcome / tension** — one oversized, plain-language promise at feed size.
2. **Semantic word-object** — a key term becomes a useful visual anchor (tag, tab, stamp, route
   marker, or field), never a decorative sticker by default.
3. **One dominant spatial argument** — the reader can identify how to read it in seconds.
4. **Operational modules** — each module includes a specific condition, distinction, example,
   variable, action, or exception. It earns its space.
5. **Micro-detail** — small but legible prompts, boundaries, units, or examples reward the reader
   who stays. This is what makes a save more rational than a quick glance.
6. **Boundary / next move** — prevent a useful simplification from becoming false certainty.

There is no fixed three-to-seven-module ceiling. Density is earned by hierarchy, grouping, and
mobile legibility—not by leaving a large blank surface to look premium. Conversely, a dense page
does not earn a smaller type size than a reader can use.

## 5. Shetty's Desk signature: Operational Cartography

The ownable signal is **Operational Cartography**: complex working situations are made visible as a
route, field, comparison, or operating object. It should feel like a colleague has made a difficult
meeting or decision easier to see—not like a consulting deck or copied creator template.

Fixed behaviours:

- near-white or intentionally calm ground; dark high-contrast ink; no permanent Cobalt/Grid frame;
- two to five semantic colour fields only when they differentiate a state, route, question, or
  evidence class;
- fine dark outlines and connectors that establish relationships; no decorative network lines;
- one large typographic promise plus a distinct but owned semantic word-object; do not make
  handwritten marker type an automatic brand device;
- a spatial object with varied subparts, not a generic four-card dashboard;
- short labels with real working language: dates, owners, commitments, buffers, constraints,
  checks, inputs, outputs, or decision rights where relevant;
- small proof or boundary notes that are readable but subordinate.

Variable by argument: palette, geometry, type treatment, density, illustration, physical metaphor,
animation, and exact layout. Recognition comes from the reader repeatedly receiving a clear,
well-made operational thought object—not from seeing the same format every week.

## 6. Caption and visual are two different products

The graphic holds the reusable model. The caption earns the click, supplies a lived moment,
explains why the model matters, states a limit, and invites an honest discussion.

Choose one hook route rather than mechanically hiding the thesis:

- **outcome + process tease:** a recognisable result, then the useful model;
- **blindspot:** a decision most teams make too early or too late;
- **named work moment:** a supplier date, a forecast conflict, a freight quote, a meeting, a
  customer commitment;
- **validated proof:** only when Tiger has an approved source for the exact claim.

Do not invent personal experience, social proof, performance numbers, or an anti-thesis merely to
create an open loop. Tiger's voice remains the writing authority.

## 7. Figma, image models, and motion

| Tool | Proper job |
|---|---|
| Figma | editable high-density layout, exact type, diagram geometry, reusable atoms, source proofing |
| GPT Image | scene, material, metaphor, or mood exploration where illustration is the argument; never final typesetting for a resource graphic |
| Deterministic motion / Figma timeline | move, regroup, fill, expand, trace, or reveal a component whose change teaches something |
| Website / spreadsheet | interaction or calculation that makes the reader materially more capable than a static visual |

Build a Figma graphic from semantic components so motion can manipulate the actual route, layer,
point, fill, or state. Do not animate a complete flattened screenshot with ambient dots merely
because animation exists. Motion must complete this sentence:

> Motion helps because it lets the reader see **[named relationship change]** that the static page
> cannot communicate as clearly.

The static final state must always remain independently useful.

For the editable-source contract and component inventory, use
`working-infographic-figma-master-v1.md`.

Selected packages use `templates/working-infographic-brief-template.md`,
`templates/working-infographic-manifest-template.json`, and
`templates/working-infographic-review-template.md` rather than inheriting Field Guide paperwork.

## 8. Review and learning

Before release, verify:

- **3 seconds:** the reader understands the work moment or promise;
- **10 seconds:** the spatial relation and reading route are obvious;
- **30 seconds:** one useful action, distinction, check, or question is available;
- **deep read:** micro-detail rewards attention without contradicting the headline;
- **truth:** numbers, examples, sources, and ownership carry their required support;
- **originality:** the result uses a transformed creative mechanism, not Pierri's distinctive
  expression or any single saved post's composition;
- **caption:** it adds tension/context/judgement instead of narrating labels;
- **motion:** only if it shows a specific state/sequence/change and preserves a complete still.

At 24 hours, seven days, and 28 days, record the exact spatial family, density level, hook route,
motion decision, and creative atoms alongside reach, saves, sends, profile visits, relevance, and
return use. Pierri's public performance claims and third-party save-rate thresholds are not
benchmarks or gates.
