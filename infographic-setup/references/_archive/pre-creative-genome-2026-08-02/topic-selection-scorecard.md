# V4 Topic Selection Scorecard

**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Status:** Active V4 gate before research, caption, or visual work
**Purpose:** prevent the calendar or creator inspiration from producing polished but low-value topics.

## Rule

No new post proceeds from candidate topic to production until it has a score.

Minimum score: **75/100**
World-class target: **85+/100**

Before scoring, read `v4-audience-growth-operating-system.md` and `audience-intelligence.md`.
Define the audience problem, public evidence path, artifact path, channel role, strongest evidence
lane, and the one mechanic or promise being tested. Consult `top100-reference-intelligence.md`
after qualification only when a curated-winner packaging hypothesis can improve the execution.

This is an editorial admission score for one topic. It is not a universal creator score,
performance score, causal model, or substitute for matched comparisons.

## Scorecard

| Dimension | Weight | Question |
|---|---:|---|
| Audience pain and role specificity | 20 | Does a planner, planning manager, purchasing practitioner, or SCM transformation leader recognize a consequential decision, recurring task, failure, cost, risk, or ambition? |
| Public evidence path | 15 | Can the claim be supported with official data, standards, peer-reviewed research, documented methods, formulas, public company evidence, or a transparent worked example? |
| Market and competitive signal | 15 | Is there Tiger, near-peer, curated-success, practitioner, search, Substack, or timely evidence explaining why the topic deserves attention? |
| Artifact and workflow utility | 15 | Can the reader inspect, adapt, copy, or run a calculator, workbook, model, dashboard, decision board, checklist, benchmark, or validated workflow? |
| Tiger contribution | 10 | Can Tiger add a real stance, operating interpretation, failure boundary, first check, or uncertainty without inventing experience? |
| Reach and distribution fit | 10 | Does the promise work as standalone LinkedIn value, and is there a justified deeper role for Substack or the website? |
| Freshness and timing | 5 | Is there a verified current reason to publish now, or is the evergreen decision repeatedly relevant? |
| Visual and explanatory potential | 5 | Is there a strong information structure that makes the reasoning easier to understand? |
| Portfolio fit | 5 | Does it add a useful problem, audience, proof, artifact, or format to the five-post weekly portfolio without duplicating recent work? |

If Top-100 fit is only aesthetic, record it as inspiration rather than an evidence classification
and rely on a stronger audience, peer, Tiger, or public-proof signal. Do not force a reference to justify the topic. Never claim
that a pattern caused performance merely because it appears in the Top-100 set or an automated
screen.

## Reframing Guide

| Weak frame | Stronger frame |
|---|---|
| What is X? | Why does X fail / matter / cost money / change the decision? |
| Tool can do Y | Role is stuck at Z; tool helps if these inputs are clean |
| Here are 5 types | Which type should you use, and what happens if you pick wrong? |
| Concept explanation | Meeting-room decision map |
| Trend list | What this changes for a specific role |
| Definition | Hidden trade-off |
| Popular creator post | Mechanic worth testing, with the audience and dependency made explicit |
| Generic AI news | Verified change translated into a planner, buyer, or transformation decision |
| Download for its own sake | Artifact that improves a specific decision or recurring workflow |

## Hard Rejection Tests

Reject or reframe if:

- the topic can be answered by a generic textbook paragraph,
- the audience segment is "everyone in supply chain,"
- the only evidence is that a large creator posted about it,
- the Top-100 reference is treated as causal performance proof,
- the claim has no public evidence or transparent demonstration path,
- there is no meeting-room or workflow use,
- there is no inspectable artifact or reusable takeaway,
- the visual would naturally become a generic 4-card grid,
- the only reason to make it is "it is next in the calendar,"
- Tiger has no credible contribution beyond the definition,
- first-person authority would require an experience, result, or belief Tiger has not supplied,
- the Substack or website destination merely duplicates the LinkedIn post.
