## What This Series Is

AI for Supply Chain is a 90-day series of single-image infographics that show supply chain professionals how to use AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) to become extraordinary at their existing role. Every post title is a supply chain problem. AI is the method inside, not the headline.

**Series identity**: "AI for Supply Chain" — referenced naturally in captions (not as a rigid label)
**Positioning**: Supply chain first. AI is the method, not the subject.
**Tools**: Free-tier focused (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini). Copilot noted as bonus where relevant.

## Three Post Types — Three Structures

### Type A — Cheat Sheet (drives saves + shares)
**Structure:**
1. **Hook** (1–2 sentences) — role-specific problem, then promise of the prompt list
2. **Series Frame** (1 sentence) — position in AI for Supply Chain series
3. **Framing** (2–3 sentences) — why these specific prompts matter, what levers they target
4. **3–4 Highlight Prompts** — pick the most impactful from the list, explain WHY each matters
5. **Money Shot** (1 sentence) — the one prompt that gets budget approval or changes a conversation
6. **CTA** — specific action: "try prompt #X with [your actual data]"
7. **Sign-off**

**Word count**: 250–350 words. These are the densest captions because they carry prompt context.

### Type B — Workflow (gets shared by managers to teams)
**Structure:**
1. **Hook** (1–2 sentences) — the problem this workflow solves, with a time or cost frame
2. **Series Frame** (1 sentence) — position in AI for Supply Chain series
3. **Workflow Walk-through** (3–5 sentences) — step-by-step, referencing the visual stages
4. **The Insight** (1–2 sentences) — what the AI does vs. what you still do (the human+AI boundary)
5. **CTA** — specific trigger: "before your next [meeting/review/cycle], try this"
6. **Sign-off**

**Word count**: 200–300 words. Tighter than cheat sheets. The visual does more work.

### Type C — Conversation Starter (drives comments + reach)
**Structure:**
1. **Hook** (1–2 sentences) — bold opinion or reframe that provokes thought
2. **Series Frame** (1 sentence) — position in AI for Supply Chain series
3. **The Argument** (3–5 sentences) — build the case with SC-specific examples
4. **The Practical Takeaway** (1–2 sentences) — what to do with this insight
5. **CTA** — open question that invites reflection and comment
6. **Sign-off**

**Word count**: 200–300 words. Lightest on detail, heaviest on opinion.

## Hook Rules (all types)

Inherited from the 101 and deep dive hook taxonomy. Same 10 types apply:

| # | Type | AI for SC Adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Question-Why | Frame as a role-specific frustration, not abstract curiosity |
| 2 | Question-How | Ground in a specific workflow or task the reader does weekly |
| 3 | Stat-Lead | Use a number that quantifies time saved, cost found, or error exposed |
| 4 | Contrarian | Challenge a common assumption about AI OR about how SC work is done |
| 5 | Paradox | Juxtapose good aggregate metric with hidden problem underneath |
| 6 | Personal-Reflection | "I built this workflow because..." or "The question I get most..." |
| 7 | Result-First | Start with what the prompt produced, then explain the method |
| 8 | Timeline-Shock | Contrast manual time vs. AI-assisted time for the same task |
| 9 | Comparison-Gap | Two practitioners: one with AI workflows, one without — show the gap |
| 10 | Decision-Pressure | "Which of your [tasks/suppliers/SKUs] would you test first?" |

**Hard rules (inherited, non-negotiable):**
- Each hook opens with a DIFFERENT first word
- Each hook uses a DIFFERENT structural device
- No em dashes anywhere. Use periods.
- No AI slop language (unlock, game-changer, revolutionise, dive into, harness, leverage, empower)
- Write as Tiger would say it out loud
- The hook becomes the verbatim opening line of the published post

## Voice Rules from tiger-voice.md (applied to AI for SC)

### Rhythm and Flow
- Write sentences that connect ideas with reasoning: "because", "so", "which means", "and then"
- Vary sentence length: mix longer flowing sentences with occasional shorter ones for emphasis
- Build through ideas rather than listing them. Add reasoning as you go.
- Allow natural imperfections. Not every sentence needs to be pristine.

### Opinion Style
- Do NOT use "My take:" as the default opinion marker
- Use Tiger's natural bridges and rotate between posts:
  - "My view on this is that..."
  - "Here's the thing most people miss..."
  - "If you look at the bigger picture..."
  - "If I were in your shoes..."

### Pattern Breakers (use at least 1 per post)
- Start a sentence with "And" or "But"
- Include one specific detail from Tiger's real experience
- Use a parenthetical aside mid-sentence (signals thinking in real time)
- Vary bullet/section structure — not all sections need the same format
- Let a sentence run longer than feels "optimal" when working through an idea

### Hard Rules
See `tiger-voice.md` — Hard Rules section. Non-negotiable across all pipelines.

## Writing Quality Checks (inherited from both voices)

These apply to ALL captions regardless of post type:

### Language
- Hard Rules apply (see `tiger-voice.md`) — no em dashes, no AI slop, no hedging, no choppy fragments
- No corporate: paradigm, synergy, stakeholder alignment, best-in-class, value proposition
- Sentences connect and flow with reasoning ("because", "so", "which means"). Tiger's voice is conversational authority with connective tissue, not bullet-point staccato.

### Structure
- Hook is always the first line of the published post. No preamble.
- Series frame is light — one sentence maximum. Not a billboard.
- Bullets use labels when it helps scanning, but vary format across bullets. Not all need the same bold-label-plus-explanation structure.
- Opinion uses Tiger's natural bridges ("My view on this is that...", "Here's the thing most people miss..."). Rotate between posts. Do NOT default to "My take:" every time.
- CTA is always a specific, actionable question — not "what do you think?"
- Sign-off: "Follow Poornajith Shetty for more supply chain insights and save this for [specific use]."
- Vary the overall structure between posts. The type-specific templates (A/B/C) guide section order, but how each section is expressed must not be identical across posts.

### Content
- Every prompt referenced in the caption must be specific enough to copy-paste
- "Try prompt #X with your actual [data type]" — always name the data type
- The human+AI boundary must be clear: what AI does, what you still validate
- Never position AI as replacing judgement. Position it as compressing the analysis so you have time for judgement.
- Free-tier tools only. If referencing a specific tool (Claude for contracts, Gemini for web search), explain why that tool fits.

## What AI for SC Voice is NOT

```
NOT THIS (AI hype):
"AI is revolutionising supply chain management. These game-changing
prompts will unlock unprecedented efficiency in your operations."

NOT THIS (too generic):
"Use AI to analyse your data and find insights that improve
your supply chain performance."

NOT THIS (too academic):
"Leveraging large language models for demand signal decomposition
enables practitioners to identify systematic forecast bias through
statistical analysis of historical variance patterns."

NOT THIS (101 voice in an AI for SC post):
"AI is a tool that helps computers do smart things with data.
In supply chain, it can help with planning and buying."

NOT THIS (deep dive voice in an AI for SC post):
"Procter & Gamble's 2024 deployment of demand sensing across
4,200 SKUs reduced bias by 34% while improving WMAPE by 6 points."
```

**The test**: Could a supply chain professional with 2+ years of experience read this post and immediately know which prompt to try first with their own data? If yes, the voice is right.
