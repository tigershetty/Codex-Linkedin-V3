# LinkedIn Content-Market-Fit Audit

**Collection date:** 2 August 2026  
**Mode:** logged-in, read-only LinkedIn inspection plus repository analysis  
**Account:** Poornajith Shetty / Shetty's Desk

## Executive conclusion

The account has not found one uniform content formula. It has found three promising content-market mechanisms:

1. **Identity/navigation artifacts** for supply-chain practitioners, led by the six-path career map.
2. **Decision-boundary artifacts** that make a planning or inventory choice easier, led by the strategic/tactical/operational planning distinction, ABC-XYZ segmentation, and safety-stock uncertainty.
3. **Named-company operating-model cases** that explain a visible strategic move, led by the Maersk pivot.

The two extreme reach outliers are genuine statistical exceptions: together they generated **70.47%** of the 331,620 impressions summed across 60 post pages. They should not become the baseline expectation. The account median is **508.5 impressions**, while 45 of 60 posts were below 1,000. The top career post reached 176,598 impressions, **347 times the median**; the Maersk post reached 57,087, **112 times the median**.

Reach is only half of the result. Smaller operational posts sometimes converted much more efficiently. The ABC-XYZ post produced a **0.614% save rate** and **0.457% follower conversion**, and the safety-stock post produced **0.553% follower conversion**. Those rates were higher than the career map's 0.233% save rate and 0.191% follower conversion. The recommended strategy is therefore a barbell: use identity and named-company mechanisms for qualified discovery, while using decision artifacts to generate saves, relevant followers, tool use, and customer conversations.

The largest unresolved opportunity is AI for supply chain. The curated save set and current-market corpus both show substantial AI interest, but Tiger's current tool-first packaging has not earned native validation. “Excel can…, but ChatGPT can…” reached 68 impressions; “Power BI can…, but Claude can…” reached 103. The scientific response is not to abandon AI or assume demand equals fit. It is to test the same operating pains with **decision-first, role-specific, human-validated workflows**.

## Methodology and evidence hierarchy

### Layer A — observed account performance

Every published post that was reasonably accessible in the logged-in session was opened through its native post-summary analytics page. The census contains **60 posts** with impressions, reactions, comments, reposts, saves, sends, post-attributed profile viewers, and followers gained where LinkedIn exposed them.

This is the strongest account-specific evidence, but it remains observational. Publication time, network distribution, topic, audience breadth, visual, and copy changed together. The audit identifies associations and hypotheses, not causal mechanics.

### Layer B — intentionally curated references

The saved-post collection is explicitly treated as an **intentionally curated high-performing/interesting reference set**. The user saved the posts because they judged them interesting and observed strong likes, impressions, and comments. It is not treated as a random taste sample.

The current live census contains **480 unique saved cards**: 463 with activity IDs, 17 without an exposed activity URL, 435 with an image preview, and 45 without one. Loading continued until the pagination control disappeared and repeated end checks added no records.

This layer provides creative-pattern evidence: hooks, promises, structures, proof styles, visuals, and CTAs worth studying. It does not prove that the saved post's topic is demanded by Tiger's target audience. The saved-list surface did not expose reactions, comments, reposts, or impressions, so those fields remain missing unless a record also appears in a repository reference source.

The existing Top-100 library contains 72 unique recovered cases; **61 match the live saved census by activity ID**. The overlap confirms that the repository library is a concentrated, older reference slice of the larger current curated collection. It does not make the remaining 419 saved posts performance-quantified.

### Layer C — recurring pains and market signals

The Stage-0 current-market corpus contains 402 posts. Its coded topic counts are used directionally because the automated meaning taxonomy failed its own reliability gate. Public interactions in that corpus are also not comparable to Tiger's impressions.

The strongest directional coded clusters are AI (91 posts, 17 creators, 9,018 public interactions), procurement (66/9/2,751), career (40/15/3,163), strategy (23/11/1,247), planning (20/6/1,498), logistics (19/6/988), and inventory (9/5/303). These counts help prioritize interviews and experiments; they do not establish content-market fit.

## Why the two extreme viral posts escaped the baseline

### 1. The career map solved an identity decision, not just an educational topic

The hook makes a high-stakes professional uncertainty legible: someone can have ten years of tenure and still not know which career they are building. The visual then resolves that anxiety with a complete six-lane branching map from entry roles to executive destinations.

That combination expands relevance beyond one supply-chain function while staying category-specific. It is easy to save for self-reference, share with colleagues, and use in a career conversation. A visible comment from a senior global product/configuration leader supplied detailed practitioner guidance about operational grounding, cross-functional understanding, and impact over titles. That is evidence of substantive resonance; it is not proof that the comment caused distribution.

### 2. The Maersk post paired a famous actor with a counterintuitive visible move

“Why is the world's second-largest shipping company buying warehouses instead of ships?” creates immediate information gain. The post does not stop at company news: its before/after visual explains the mechanism from ocean-centric carrier to integrated logistics ecosystem, with acquisitions, digitization, and factory-to-door service.

The named company makes the story legible to a broad professional audience, while the operating-model explanation keeps it useful to supply-chain practitioners. Its 0.201% follower conversion was slightly higher than the career map's 0.191%, even though the career map produced more than three times the absolute follower gains.

### 3. The shared mechanism is complete translation

Both posts translate a consequential but fuzzy question into a complete visible system:

- one translates career ambiguity into paths and destinations;
- the other translates a corporate move into an operating-model shift.

The third breakout uses the same deeper mechanic: it translates planning confusion into decision horizons and shows the operational cost of applying the wrong cadence. The reusable hypothesis is not “use a question hook” or “make an infographic.” It is: **start with a consequential gap, then deliver a specific visual system that closes it**.

### 4. What did not escape

Weak posts frequently carried one of four packaging problems:

- an abstract maxim without a concrete decision scene;
- a familiar concept presented as a lesson rather than an unresolved operating choice;
- a metaphor whose supply-chain consequence was not immediately obvious;
- a repeated “existing tool can X, but AI tool can Y” construction.

The latter is particularly risky. The Excel/ChatGPT and Power BI/Claude openings are structurally interchangeable, put the tool ahead of the job, and lack the lived operating boundary that distinguishes Tiger's strongest work. One weak post cannot diagnose causality, but the repeated low outcomes justify a decision-first repackaging experiment.

## Saved, published, and performed: the gaps

### The saved collection is broader than the brand's operating market

The heuristic database tags 150 saved records toward creators/marketers and 163 toward AI workflows/adoption/governance. Only 68 are tagged toward planners, 51 toward procurement practitioners, 47 toward transformation leads, and 35 toward broad supply-chain practitioners. These are low-confidence automated routing tags, but the size of the creator/AI component is visually and textually clear enough to flag a strategic risk: what makes a post attractive as a creator reference is not necessarily what makes it valuable to a supply-chain practitioner.

### The save set over-represents conversion mechanics

The saved census contains 128 platform-engagement CTAs, 107 commercial-conversion CTAs, 85 traffic/owned-audience CTAs, and 45 keyword gates under the heuristic coding. Only five were classified as practitioner discussion. These counts are not population estimates. They show what is present in the curated set and what could leak into future packaging.

For Shetty's Desk, keyword gates, interchangeable short-line cadence, and generic engagement questions would make the work feel more like creator optimization than operator translation. The reference set should supply visual and rhetorical mechanics, while Tiger's source gate supplies the stance, failure boundary, first check, human owner, and uncertainty.

### The best saved visual mechanisms are still useful

The curated set contains 130 workflow/process structures, 78 chart/data/formula structures, 73 comparisons/matrices, 29 checklists/grids, 20 reference sheets, and 15 decision trees under the heuristic visual coding. These align well with the structures that performed for Tiger. The safe adaptation is to borrow the **information architecture**, not the creator's topic, voice, CTA, or claim.

### AI demand is real enough to test, not proven enough to scale

AI is the largest saved-topic cluster and the largest directional current-market cluster. Yet the account's current AI/tool-led posts sit near the weak end. This is a clean three-layer disagreement:

- curated references say the packaging space is creatively active;
- market signals say the audience pain is recurring;
- observed account performance says current packaging has not worked.

That disagreement is exactly where a controlled V4 experiment belongs.

## Generic or AI-slop-like packaging signals

Treat the following as a pre-publication risk checklist:

- The opening can swap in a different tool name without changing the argument.
- Short stacked fragments replace connected reasoning.
- The post claims a transformation but omits inputs, artifact, validation, failure boundary, or owner.
- The visual is polished but could apply to any industry.
- The caption and image duplicate one another.
- A generic “Thoughts?” or keyword comment gate substitutes for a practitioner decision question.
- Personal authority is implied without a specific public-safe experience or source.
- Every post uses the same hook, line breaks, and CTA.

The antidote is not longer copy by itself. Preserve Tiger's strongest voice pattern: context → operating issue → decision, with calm connected reasoning, one real distinction, public-safe detail, honest uncertainty, and no invented personal experience.

## 90-day experiment roadmap

The detailed weekly matrix is in `90-day-experiment-roadmap.csv`. Continue the V4 publishing cadence, but designate one anchor experiment each week and use the remaining posts as replications, controls, or audience-depth work.

### Days 1-7 — establish measurement discipline

- Build a post ledger with topic, audience, format, hypothesis, controlled variable, and publication time.
- Capture outcomes at 24 hours, 7 days, and 28 days.
- Manually code qualified comments and relevant followers.
- Establish personal medians by topic, format, and age. Do not use the viral mean as the baseline.

### Weeks 1-4 — replicate the proven mechanisms

- Two career/capability navigation artifacts with different real decisions.
- Two planning/inventory decision artifacts, including one complete matrix and one worked decision board.
- Control the format and CTA while varying the opening mechanism.
- Keep a hypothesis only after at least two aligned results.

### Weeks 5-8 — test case-study discovery and AI utility

- Publish two sourced named-company operating-model cases.
- Match a famous-company curiosity opening against an operating-consequence opening.
- Rebuild two AI posts around the operator's decision, safe sample, validation, and human owner; introduce the tool only after the job is clear.
- Measure unique artifact/tool use and customer conversations, not just LinkedIn response.

### Weeks 9-12 — test role depth and authentic authority

- Run procurement/action-queue utility tests with buyer/category-manager targeting.
- Compare a source-gated operational failure boundary with a polished generic explanation.
- Test a reflective practitioner question against no CTA; do not use keyword gates.
- Continue the best-performing topic as a sequence so audiences can compound understanding.

### Week 13 — portfolio decision

Compare only matched age, format, and audience cohorts. For each hypothesis choose `scale`, `revise`, or `kill`. One high-view post cannot qualify a mechanism; one weak post cannot kill a market unless its predicted downstream outcomes also fail.

## Measurement scorecard

### Reach context

- Median impressions at matched publication age.
- Post-attributed profile-viewer rate.
- Relevant-follower conversion, not follower count alone.

### Intent and professional utility

- Save rate.
- Send and repost rates.
- Qualified comments: comments that describe a current state, operating failure, counterexample, correction, role-specific question, or implementation constraint.
- Qualified-comment share: qualified comments / all comments.

### Owned and commercial outcomes

- Signups attributable to the post.
- Unique tool users and completed artifact runs.
- Return tool usage where measurable.
- Customer conversations attributable to the topic/artifact.
- Practitioner roles represented in those conversations.

### Decision rule

An experiment should normally improve at least two quality outcomes across two replications before scaling. A practical early gate is:

- save rate or relevant-follower conversion at least 25% above the matched personal median; and
- at least three qualified practitioner comments, or a materially higher qualified-comment share; or
- a downstream action such as tool use, signup, or customer conversation that the control did not generate.

These are pilot thresholds, not universal benchmarks. Reset them after four weeks of matched-age data.

## Limitations

- LinkedIn's distribution system is unobserved; no mechanism is causal.
- Metrics continue to drift after collection.
- The annual aggregate and per-post components do not fully reconcile.
- The saved list did not expose performance metrics.
- Seventeen saved cards had no exposed activity URL and use conservative unresolved keys.
- Dynamic loading can omit items despite the stable end-of-list procedure.
- Saved and current-market posts are not random samples.
- Stage-0 automated meaning tags failed the reliability gate; large-scale semantic counts are directional.
- Most visual-structure tags are inferred from captions, codes, and preview presence. Only the strategically important outliers received manual lightbox review in this pass.
- Creator-relative public interactions cannot be compared with impression-normalized native rates.
- A visible comment can establish practitioner substance, not represent the entire audience or explain reach.

## Recommended operating change

Keep the V4 source gate as the editorial authority and add one research gate before production:

> A topic enters production only when the team can state the target role, live decision, failure boundary, artifact, validation method, and success metric—and identify which of the three evidence layers supports each claim.

This preserves Tiger's operational voice while letting the saved collection do what it is best at: supplying adaptable visual and packaging intelligence.
