# Shetty's Desk — Supply Chain 101: 90-Day Content Plan
**Version**: 2
**Used by**: `/101` skill (topic lookup)
**Period**: March – June 2026
**Format**: 24 single-image infographics, 2 per week, 12 weeks

---

## Series Logic

Each infographic builds on the previous one without requiring it. A follower who discovers the series at post 14 can understand it fully, but a follower from post 1 will see how concepts connect.

**Month 1 — The Language**: Shared vocabulary. What a supply chain is, how its functions, planning layers, and production strategies relate.
**Month 2 — The Mechanics**: How supply chains buy, hold inventory, manage cost, and move goods. The operating system.
**Month 3 — The Strategy**: Decisions that separate average supply chains from competitive advantages. Supply chain meets business performance.

## MONTH 2: THE MECHANICS

| # | Topic | The Question It Answers | Hook | Visual Format | Caption Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | Purchasing vs. Procurement | Are these different roles or just different words? | Purchasing buys what someone asked for. | 10 | Direct vs. Indirect Procurement | We buy raw materials AND office supplies. Same team? | Direct procurement is what goes into your product. Indirect procurement is everything else. | 11 | The Bullwhip Effect | Why does my supplier see wild demand swings when my retail sales are stable? | A 5% increase in consumer demand can become a 40% spike at the raw material supplier. Below: 4 root causes in 2×2 grid. | Open with hook. Reference P&G Pampers as origin. 4 causes. Reframe: not a forecasting problem, it is an information-sharing problem. | 12 | Safety Stock | How much extra inventory should I hold just in case? | Safety stock is the inventory you hope you never need but cannot afford not to have. Most companies either hold too much or calculate it with a formula from 1958 and never revisit. | Inventory sawtooth diagram: cycle stock, safety stock, reorder point, lead time. Below: 3 variables (demand variability, supply variability, service level). | Open with hook. Three inputs in plain language. The mistake: using average lead time instead of lead time variability. Contrarian: reducing lead time variability is a better lever than tweaking safety stock formulas. Close with: when did you last recalculate your safety stock parameters? |
| 13 | Lead Time: The Hidden Variable | My supplier says 6 weeks. Is that the only lead time I should care about? | There are at least 5 types of lead time in your supply chain. The gap between what you measure and what you should measure is where delivery promises die. | Stacked bar/timeline: Information LT + Supplier LT + Manufacturing LT + Transportation LT + Receiving/QC LT = Cumulative LT. Different shades per segment. | Open with hook. Define each type in one sentence. Insight: cutting 3 days of decision time (information LT) often costs nothing and delivers as much value as cutting 3 days of transit time. Close with: have you mapped the full cumulative lead time for your top 10 SKUs? |
| 14 | ABC/XYZ Inventory Classification | I have 10,000 SKUs. How do I decide which ones get attention? | 20% of your SKUs drive 80% of your revenue. But revenue alone does not tell you how to plan them. The second dimension — demand predictability — changes everything. | 3×3 matrix. Rows: A/B/C (revenue). Columns: X/Y/Z (demand stability). Each box: short planning implication. Colour-coded green/amber/red. | Open with Pareto hook. ABC = value contribution, XYZ = demand pattern. AX = automate. AZ = manual attention. CZ = consider discontinuing. Close with: how many SKUs fall in CZ, and does anyone review them? |
| 15 | Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) | We chose the cheapest supplier. Why are we spending more? | Unit price is 30–50% of what you actually pay. The rest is invisible: freight, quality failures, payment terms, lead time buffers, inventory holding, admin overhead. That total is TCO. | Iceberg. Above waterline: Unit Price. Below: Transport, Customs, Quality costs, Inventory carrying, Admin, Lead time buffer, Supplier management. Below-waterline 2–3× larger visually. | Open with hook. One example: supplier 15% cheaper on unit price but 3× longer lead time requires 3× more safety stock, erasing the saving. Close with: does your procurement team evaluate on unit price or total cost? |
| 16 | 1PL → 2PL → 3PL → 4PL → 5PL | We use a 3PL. Our competitor uses a 4PL. What are we missing? | Five levels of logistics outsourcing. Each one trades control for capability. Each step: PL level, one-line definition, example company, gain vs give up. 1PL (brewery delivering own beer) → 5PL (network-level, e-commerce platforms). | Open with hook. Walk up the ladder. Decision point: when complexity outgrows your team's ability to coordinate, that is the signal to move up. Close with: what level does your logistics operation actually require? |

**No overlap**: Episodes 1–24 are published. All topics below are new — zero direct repeats.
