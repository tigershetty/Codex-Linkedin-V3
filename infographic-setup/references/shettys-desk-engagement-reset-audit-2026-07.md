# Shetty's Desk Engagement Reset Audit

**Date:** 2026-07-20  
**Status:** Evidence base for workflow redesign  
**Scope:** 45 logged LinkedIn posts, the Top-100 reference set, current workflow files, creator systems, and live public creator surfaces.

## Verdict

The account has not hit an account-level ceiling. It has already demonstrated exceptional reach.

The recent decline is concentrated in a run of generic procurement-curriculum posts. The production system became better at making complete, polished artifacts than at proving that the audience wanted the underlying idea. The reset is therefore not "make better infographics." It is:

> Find a tension the audience already feels, add a point of view Tiger can credibly own, prove it with operating evidence, and only then decide whether the best carrier is a story, diagram, tool, resource, or visual artifact.

## Why engagement stayed below 10

### 1. The topic portfolio narrowed into curriculum

The winning posts answer questions people use to locate themselves:

- Where can my career go?
- Which planning layer is failing?
- What does this logistics model mean for ownership?
- What uncertainty is this inventory protecting?

The recent sequence mostly answers "what is this procurement concept?" Accurate education is abundant. Without a new consequence, identity, or operating observation, it is easy to consume and easier to ignore.

### 2. The hook often describes the topic instead of opening a tension

"Every team has a process" and similar universal statements create little curiosity. The strongest hooks in the account contain a contradiction or a cost:

- ten years of experience but no visible career path,
- efficient at one planning horizon and expensive at another,
- owning everything versus orchestrating everything,
- a clean formula facing an uncertain world.

### 3. Tiger's lived authority is underused

Recent captions explain well, but many could be published by any supply-chain educator. They rarely tell the reader what Tiger observed in a plant, planning review, supplier conversation, implementation, or career decision. The creator benchmarks that work best expose the source of the insight: 784 posts, 100,000 DMs, 20 reviewers, 25 years in procurement, recurring director conversations, or a concrete client outcome.

### 4. The workflow mistakes visual selection for demand evidence

The Top-100 is called a proof set in several workflow files, yet the corpus includes duplicates, reposts, unsigned images, and low-engagement artifacts. It proves visual and caption mechanics, not audience demand. A beautiful maturity grid with a few reactions is still a weak performance reference.

### 5. Production completeness arrives before idea validation

The current system can produce a still, motion version, caption, hooks, resource pack, and website page before it has three audience receipts or one expert challenge. This makes iteration expensive and biases the team toward defending work already made.

### 6. The post is treated as the finish line

High-performing creator systems compound a post through a newsletter, recurring series, community, collaborative draft, course, research report, or maintained resource library. Shetty's Desk has begun building resources, but the distribution and feedback loop is not yet a mandatory part of the brief.

### 7. The account's strongest audience signal is broader than procurement process education

Career identity, planning decisions, recognizable companies, and operating trade-offs have produced the largest reach. Procurement should remain a pillar, but seven consecutive curriculum-like procurement posts create a narrower interest signal and fewer reasons for the broader supply-chain audience to participate.

## New portfolio model

This is a testable four-slot weekly portfolio, not a permanent frequency rule.

| Slot | Job | Preferred carrier | Production depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: Broad identity | Help a supply-chain professional locate themselves, their role, or their next move. | Map, ladder, story, or annotated photo. | Flagship when evidence is strong. |
| B: Operating decision | Make one planning, procurement, manufacturing, or logistics trade-off visible. | Decision table, contrast, process breakpoint, or field note. | Flagship or medium. |
| C: AI experiment | Show what a tool did with real context, output, failure, and planner review. | Before/after artifact, screen evidence, workflow, or short demo. | Medium; resource only after validation. |
| D: Conversation / proof | Share a practitioner pattern, case, result, misconception, or unresolved question. | Text, photo, poll with analysis, or mini-chart. | Light and fast. |

The mix creates more learning attempts without requiring four expensive visual/resource packages.

## Required success measures

Do not optimize only for reactions.

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Qualified reach | Did the intended roles see it? |
| Saves per 1,000 impressions | Is it useful enough to revisit? |
| Unique commenters | Did it attract multiple voices rather than one long exchange? |
| Author reply ratio | Did Shetty's Desk turn comments into conversation? |
| Comment specificity | Are people sharing examples, disagreement, or decisions? |
| Followers per 1,000 impressions | Did the post make the future content promise clear? |
| Resource intent and completed downloads | Did interest become action? |
| Profile and website actions | Did the post deepen trust beyond the feed? |
| Production hours per qualified outcome | Is the format worth repeating? |

## 30-day reset experiment

1. Select 12 candidate tensions from audience receipts and historical winners, not calendar order.
2. Score all 12. Produce only the top 4 flagships.
3. Run 4 lighter story, field-note, or contrarian tests around the same IP lanes.
4. Invite at least three practitioners to challenge each technical flagship before final rendering.
5. Publish each flagship with a named distribution plan and a follow-up post within seven days.
6. At day 30, compare by audience role, content job, evidence mode, carrier, and production effort.

### Reset success threshold

The first goal is not another 175,000-impression outlier. It is to improve the floor:

- fewer than 25% of posts at 10 or fewer interactions,
- median interactions above the March-April baseline of 13,
- at least two posts with comments that materially change or extend the framework,
- complete classification and 7-day learning notes for every post,
- no flagship produced without the demand-proof gate.

## Evidence Limits

- The engagement diagnosis uses the 45 posts present in the local analytics log. It cannot see private LinkedIn metrics, deleted posts, audience quality, or unlogged experiments.
- Public LinkedIn reactions and comments are indexed snapshots, not complete account analytics. They are used as directional evidence only.
- The Top-100 audit deliberately leaves unresolved and corpus-only identities unresolved rather than inventing a live match. Reposts are separated from original creators where evidence supports that distinction.
- Creator tactics and Shetty's Desk performance are observational. The audit ranks hypotheses and controllable workflow changes; it does not claim that one format, hook, posting time, or creator tactic causes reach.
- The 30-day reset is the validation step. Future workflow changes should follow measured results, not confidence in this document.

## The core editorial test

Before production, answer this in one sentence:

> What will a specific supply-chain professional recognize, reconsider, or decide differently because Tiger published this now?

If the answer is only "they will understand the concept," the topic is not ready.
