# Voice Interview Plan — 5 Sessions

## Method
Ruben Hassid's Taste Interviewer: 100 questions across 7 categories.
Tiger answers via voice-to-text. Each session ~15-20 questions, ~10 minutes.

## Progress

### Session 1 — COMPLETE (2026-03-15)
**Categories covered:** Writing Mechanics (partial), Aesthetic Crimes (partial)
**Questions answered:** ~10
**Key findings:**
- Rejects choppy AI fragment style above all else
- Natural rhythm: long flowing sentences with connective tissue ("so", "right", "because")
- Builds up to the point: context → information → conclusion
- Uses broad vocabulary naturally (elucidate, utilize) — individual words aren't the problem, patterns are
- Opinion bridges: "My view on this is", "Here's the thing most people miss", "If I were in your shoes"
- Preferred Version C style: conversational entry + clean distinction + memorable close
- Simplifies by making relatable, not by dumbing down
- Diplomatic disagreement: leads with gaps and constraints, shares facts, then lands position subtly

### Session 2 — NEXT
**Category:** Beliefs & Contrarian Takes (15 questions)
**Focus:** Tiger's actual opinions on supply chain — hot takes, what the industry gets wrong, defensible positions that make his content distinctly his
**Questions to ask:**
1. What is one thing most supply chain professionals believe that you think is wrong?
2. What supply chain trend are people most excited about that you think is overhyped?
3. What is an unpopular opinion you hold about how supply chains should be managed?
4. What do consultants get wrong about supply chain most often?
5. What is the most common mistake companies make with their supply chain strategy?
6. What do you believe about supplier relationships that goes against common wisdom?
7. If you could change one thing about how the industry trains new supply chain professionals, what would it be?
8. What technology in supply chain do you think is oversold?
9. What is something you used to believe about supply chain that you no longer do?
10. What is a hard truth about supply chain that most leaders avoid?
11. When someone describes themselves as a "supply chain expert" on LinkedIn, what signals tell you whether they actually are?
12. What is one area where academic supply chain theory completely fails in practice?
13. What do most people get wrong about procurement vs sourcing?
14. If you had to argue against the current push for supply chain resilience, what would you say?
15. What belief do you hold about demand planning that most planners would disagree with?

### Session 3
**Category:** Voice & Personality (15 questions)
**Focus:** How Tiger uses humour, how his tone shifts between casual and serious, how he expresses emotion in writing, what his defaults are when relaxed vs when arguing a point

### Session 4
**Category:** Structural Preferences (15 questions) + Hard Nos (10 questions)
**Focus:** How Tiger organises ideas, his relationship with formatting (bullets, headers, lists), transition methods, untouchable subjects, absolute refusals

### Session 5
**Categories:** Red Flags (10 questions) + Writing Mechanics (remaining ~10 questions) + Aesthetic Crimes (remaining ~10 questions)
**Focus:** What destroys trust in content, remaining gaps in sentence patterns, closing patterns, punctuation habits
