## LinkedIn Caption

The answer to a bad forecast isn't a better forecast. It's recognising that forecasting was never meant to be the only tool.

This is Episode 17 of Supply Chain 101. We're now moving into the Strategy of supply chains. We've covered the language and the mechanics. Now we're looking at the decisions that separate average supply chains from competitive advantages.

And it starts with demand, because everything in a supply chain flows from what customers want, when they want it, and whether you can influence either.

- Demand planning is what most people mean when they say "forecasting." It uses historical data, market intelligence, and statistical models to predict what customers will buy months from now. It's essential, and it's also inherently limited, because the further out you look, the less accurate it gets. A 60-75% accuracy rate is considered normal, and that remaining 25-40% is where most supply chain pain comes from.

- Demand sensing is the short-term correction. It uses real-time signals (point-of-sale data, web traffic, order patterns, weather, events) to detect what's actually happening right now, not what the model predicted three months ago. It operates in days to weeks and typically achieves 80-90% accuracy because it's working with current information rather than projections.

- Demand shaping is the most underused of the three. Instead of predicting or detecting demand, it actively influences it. Pricing changes, promotions, product bundling, substitution offers, allocation decisions. Airlines do this constantly: dynamic pricing is demand shaping in its purest form.

My view on this is that most supply chains treat demand as something to predict rather than something to manage. Planning, sensing, and shaping aren't alternatives. They're layers, and the companies that run all three are working with a fundamentally different demand picture.

Does your supply chain team have a seat at the table when pricing decisions are made?

1. [Question-Why]: "Why is your demand forecast always wrong? Because forecasting is only one of three demand capabilities, and the other two, sensing and shaping, are what close the gap that forecasting can't."
2. [Question-How]: "How does an airline sell the same seat at 12 different prices and call it supply chain management? Because they're not just forecasting demand. They're shaping it. And that's the demand capability most supply chains haven't even started using."
3. [Stat-Lead]: "Demand planning predicts what customers will want. Demand sensing detects what they want right now. Demand shaping makes them want what you have. Three capabilities, most companies only do the first, and they do it with 60-75% accuracy at best."
4. [Contrarian]: "The answer to a bad forecast isn't a better forecast. It's recognising that forecasting was never meant to be the only tool. Sensing closes the gap short-term. Shaping redirects demand so the gap matters less. Most supply chains are trying to solve a three-tool problem with one tool."
5. [Paradox]: "Demand planning looks months ahead and gets it 60-75% right. Demand sensing looks days ahead and gets it 80-90% right. Demand shaping doesn't predict at all. It changes what customers buy. The most powerful demand capability is the one that doesn't try to forecast."
6. [Personal-Reflection]: "I've watched planning teams spend weeks refining a demand forecast to squeeze out another 2% accuracy, while the commercial team was running a promotion that would make the entire forecast irrelevant. The forecasting wasn't the problem. The lack of coordination between planning and shaping was."
7. [Result-First]: "Excess inventory of Product A. Stockout of Product B. Both happened because the supply chain tried to predict demand instead of influence it. What if, instead of building more of what you think they'll buy, you redirected customers toward what you already have?"
8. [Timeline-Shock]: "6 months out: demand planning says we'll sell 50,000 units next quarter based on historical patterns and market signals. 2 weeks out: demand sensing detects that real-time POS data, web traffic, and order patterns suggest 42,000. Today: demand shaping adjusts pricing on the slower SKU and bundles it with a fast mover. Three capabilities. Three time horizons. One integrated demand picture."
9. [Comparison-Gap]: "Demand planning works in months and product families. Demand sensing works in days and individual SKUs. Demand shaping works right now and changes the equation entirely. They're not alternatives to each other. They're layers, and most companies are only running the first one."
10. [Decision-Pressure]: "Does your supply chain team influence pricing decisions? If not, you're running demand planning without demand shaping, which means you're trying to predict a future you could be influencing."
