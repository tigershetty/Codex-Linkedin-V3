## LinkedIn Caption

Single-sourcing gives you leverage through volume. Multi-sourcing gives you resilience through options.

The decision isn't about risk tolerance. It's about which risk you prefer to carry: the risk of dependency or the risk of complexity.

This is Episode 20 of Supply Chain 101. In Episode 19 we covered JIT vs. JIC and the idea that inventory strategy should be segmented by product. Today we're applying that same logic to supplier strategy, because the number of suppliers you use for a category is one of the most consequential decisions in procurement, and most companies make it by default rather than by design.

- Single-sourcing means one supplier for a category. The advantages are real: concentrated volume gives you negotiating leverage, deeper relationships, simpler quality management, and often better pricing. The risk is equally real: if that supplier fails, you have no immediate alternative.

- Dual-sourcing means two suppliers sharing volume. It's the most common risk mitigation approach because it provides a built-in backup without excessive complexity. The trade-off is that neither supplier gets your full volume, which can dilute your leverage and may mean neither prioritises you as highly.

- Multi-sourcing means three or more suppliers. The advantage is maximum optionality and redundancy. The trade-off is significant management overhead: more contracts, more quality audits, more relationship management, and diluted volume across all of them.

Here's the thing most people miss: the number of suppliers matters less than three underlying factors.
- Supply risk: how likely is disruption?
- Spend volume: is there enough to split meaningfully?
- And criticality: what happens if this component is unavailable?

A low-risk, high-volume commodity might be fine with a single source. A high-risk, critical component with limited alternatives needs at least two, and they should not be in the same geography.

For your top 5 spend categories, can you name a tested alternative?

## Hook Options — All 10 (reference)
1. [Question-Why]: "Why do some companies deliberately rely on a single supplier while others insist on three? Because single-sourcing and multi-sourcing don't solve the same problem, and most companies haven't decided which problem they're trying to solve."
2. [Question-How]: "How do you decide whether one supplier is a concentration risk or a competitive advantage? It depends on three factors that most procurement teams evaluate separately when they should be evaluating together."
3. [Stat-Lead]: "Single-sourcing gives you leverage through volume. Multi-sourcing gives you resilience through options. The decision isn't about risk tolerance. It's about which risk you prefer to carry: the risk of dependency or the risk of complexity."
4. [Contrarian]: "COVID triggered an overcorrection from single-sourcing to multi-sourcing without considering the trade-offs. Multi-sourcing a specialised component often increases cost without improving resilience if all three suppliers are in the same geography. More suppliers doesn't automatically mean less risk."
5. [Paradox]: "One supplier means all your eggs in one basket. Three suppliers means three times the management overhead, three qualification processes, and potentially three mediocre relationships instead of one strong one. Neither extreme is the answer, and the right number depends on something most companies haven't formally assessed."
6. [Personal-Reflection]: "I've managed categories where we had a single supplier that was deeply embedded in our operations and delivered consistently, and categories where we maintained three suppliers as a safety net but none of them got enough volume to prioritise us. The risk wasn't in the number of suppliers. It was in whether the strategy matched the category."
7. [Result-First]: "Your single supplier goes down. Production stops. That's the nightmare scenario for single-sourcing. But here's the one for multi-sourcing: you split volume across three suppliers, none of them treat you as a priority customer, and when capacity gets tight, you're the first one they short. Different strategy, different risk, same outcome."
8. [Timeline-Shock]: "2019: you have one supplier for a critical component. Relationship is strong, pricing is competitive, delivery is reliable. 2020: that supplier shuts down for three months. Production halts. 2021: you qualify two additional suppliers. 2023: all three are in the same region. A typhoon hits. All three are affected. The number of suppliers changed. The geographic risk didn't."
9. [Comparison-Gap]: "Single-source: one supplier, maximum volume leverage, deepest relationship, highest dependency risk. Dual-source: two suppliers, balanced volume, built-in backup, moderate complexity. Multi-source: three or more suppliers, maximum optionality, highest management overhead, diluted volume leverage. The right answer depends on the category, not the philosophy."
10. [Decision-Pressure]: "For your top 5 spend categories, can you name a tested alternative supplier? Not one you've identified. One you've qualified, trialled, and confirmed can deliver. If not, your sourcing strategy has a single point of failure whether you intended it or not."
