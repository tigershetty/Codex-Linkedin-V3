## LinkedIn Caption

Why does the team buying raw materials for your product report to the VP of Supply Chain, while the team buying laptops, cleaning services, and travel reports to... nobody in particular?

This is Episode 10 of Supply Chain 101. In Episode 9 we separated purchasing from procurement. Today we're splitting procurement itself into two categories, because the difference determines how much attention (and money) each one gets.

- Direct procurement covers everything that ends up in the finished product: raw materials, components, packaging. If you make cars, it's the steel, the electronics, the tyres. These categories get dedicated teams, supplier scorecards, and quarterly business reviews because the link between what you buy and what you sell is obvious.

- Indirect procurement covers everything the company needs to operate that doesn't touch the product: IT infrastructure, office supplies, facility management, travel, MRO (maintenance, repair, and operations), and professional services. These categories are often fragmented across departments, managed by whoever happens to need the service, and renewed on autopilot.

What I see is indirect spend is typically 15-30% of revenue, and in most organisations it has a fraction of the strategic oversight that direct spend receives. Not because the money is small, but because it's spread across dozens of categories with no single owner. And that fragmentation is exactly why it's where some of the easiest savings sit.

The contrarian view is simple: if you've already optimised your direct material costs, the next lever isn't pushing suppliers harder. It's looking at the indirect side of the ledger where contracts auto-renew and nobody benchmarks.

Who owns indirect spend in your organisation?

## Hook Options — All 10 (reference)
1. [Question-Why]: "Why does the team buying raw materials for your product report to the VP of Supply Chain, while the team buying laptops, cleaning services, and travel reports to... nobody in particular?"
2. [Question-How]: "How does a company spend 15-30% of its revenue on things that never touch the product and still not have a strategy for it? Welcome to indirect procurement. The budget nobody owns."
3. [Stat-Lead]: "Direct procurement gets a category manager, a strategy deck, and quarterly business reviews. Indirect procurement gets a shared inbox and whoever has time. The difference in attention is staggering, and it's where some of the easiest savings are hiding."
4. [Contrarian]: "Everyone focuses on negotiating better prices for raw materials. Meanwhile, indirect spend (facilities, IT, MRO, travel) is 15-30% of revenue and almost nobody is managing it strategically. The biggest savings opportunity in most companies isn't in what they make. It's in everything else."
5. [Paradox]: "Direct procurement buys what goes into your product. Indirect procurement buys everything else. One has always had strategic attention. The other is 15-30% of revenue and is often managed by whoever picks up the phone."
6. [Personal-Reflection]: "In my experience, direct spend gets all the scrutiny because it's visible in the product. But I've seen indirect spend categories where nobody had reviewed the contract in three years, and the savings sitting there were significant, simply because nobody was looking."
7. [Result-First]: "The easiest cost savings in most organisations are not in raw material negotiation. They're in indirect spend categories that haven't been reviewed, consolidated, or competitively tendered in years. Not because the savings aren't there, but because nobody owns the problem."
8. [Timeline-Shock]: "Your direct procurement team spent six months selecting a new packaging supplier. They evaluated five candidates, ran quality trials, and negotiated volume discounts. Your indirect procurement team renewed the office supplies contract on auto-pilot because it was due last Tuesday. Both are procurement decisions. Only one got the attention it deserved."
9. [Comparison-Gap]: "Direct procurement buys steel, chemicals, and packaging. Indirect procurement buys software licences, facility management, and courier services. One builds the product. The other keeps the company running. But ask most organisations who owns indirect spend strategically, and you'll get silence."
10. [Decision-Pressure]: "Who owns indirect spend in your organisation? If the answer is 'it depends on the category,' that's usually code for 'nobody owns it strategically.'"
