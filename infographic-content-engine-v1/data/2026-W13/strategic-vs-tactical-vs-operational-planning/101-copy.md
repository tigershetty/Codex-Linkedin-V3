## LinkedIn Caption

When strategic decisions get made with operational urgency, companies build warehouses they don't need. When operational decisions wait for strategic approval, customers don't get their orders.

This is Episode 4 of Supply Chain 101. Last time we separated planning from scheduling. Today we're splitting planning itself into three layers, because treating them as one is where things start going sideways.

- Strategic planning operates in years. It's about network design, market entry, and where to place capacity. The people in the room are the CEO, the VP of Supply Chain, and sometimes the board. These decisions shape everything downstream and they're expensive to reverse.

- Tactical planning operates in months to quarters. This is S&OP territory (which we'll cover in Episode 5). The question is: given the capacity we have, how do we allocate it across product lines and regions? Directors and senior managers own this layer, and the quality of their decisions depends entirely on whether the strategic layer above gave them clear guardrails.

- Operational planning operates in days to weeks. Which orders get prioritised? What do we produce on which line? This is where planners and schedulers live, and it's where most of the visible firefighting happens.

If you look a little closer, the problem isn't that companies don't plan. It's that they don't separate the layers. A five-year capacity question gets debated in a weekly operations meeting, or a daily prioritisation decision waits three weeks for a director's sign-off. Both cost money, both are avoidable.

Which level does your team spend the most time on? And is that actually where the biggest gap is?

## Hook Options — All 10 (reference)
1. [Question-Why]: "Why does your company hold one planning meeting where the CEO, the director, and the floor planner are all in the same room arguing about different things?"
2. [Question-How]: "How does a decision to open a factory in Mexico connect to which orders your planner prioritises this morning? Through three layers of planning that most companies collapse into one."
3. [Stat-Lead]: "Three levels of planning. Three time horizons. Three different people who should be making the decisions. In most companies, all three happen in the same meeting and nobody leaves satisfied."
4. [Contrarian]: "Your company does planning. Everyone does. But if the same team is deciding where to build a new warehouse and which orders ship first tomorrow, you don't have a planning process. You have a meeting."
5. [Paradox]: "Strategic planning asks: should we build a plant in Mexico? Operational planning asks: which orders do we run first today? Both are called 'planning.' Both are critical. And in most organisations, they compete for the same hour on the same calendar."
6. [Personal-Reflection]: "I've been in planning meetings where someone raises a five-year network question and someone else wants to talk about next Tuesday's production run. Both are valid. But they don't belong in the same conversation, and that confusion costs more time than people realise."
7. [Result-First]: "When strategic decisions get made with operational urgency, companies build warehouses they don't need. When operational decisions wait for strategic approval, customers don't get their orders. The fix is knowing which level you're operating at."
8. [Timeline-Shock]: "2027: decide whether to open a distribution centre in Southeast Asia. Q3 2026: allocate capacity between Product Line A and Product Line B. This afternoon: figure out if Line 4 can run an extra shift. All three are 'planning.' None of them should be done by the same person."
9. [Comparison-Gap]: "Strategic planning thinks in years and geographies. Tactical planning thinks in quarters and product families. Operational planning thinks in days and individual orders. Most companies use one word for all three and wonder why their planning process feels broken."
10. [Decision-Pressure]: "Which level of planning does your team spend the most time on? And is that the level where the biggest decisions actually need to be made?"
