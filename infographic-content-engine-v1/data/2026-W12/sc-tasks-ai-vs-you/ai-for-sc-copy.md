## LinkedIn Caption

Roughly 60% of a typical planner's week is pattern detection, report drafting, and data categorisation. That is the 60% AI compresses. The other 40% is where careers are being built.

The supply chain role you are in today will not look the same in five years. Not because the fundamentals change, but because the tools around those fundamentals are shifting faster than most teams can absorb.

• Demand planners are already using structured prompts to run bias analysis in minutes instead of hours.
• Procurement professionals are stress-testing supplier contracts with AI before they sit down to negotiate.
• Logistics coordinators are building carrier comparisons that used to take half a day in under ten minutes. This is not a future trend. It is happening now, unevenly, across the industry.

That is why I think it's important to get on that train, and take you along on that journey. I am learning and want to share with you what it looks today and what's to come.

So, here's AI for Supply Chain. A series of practical workflows, prompts and honest conversations about what AI actually changes for supply chain professionals and what it does not. No fear mongering, hype or generic "use AI to analyse your data."

It's what will separate competent from extraordinary. These are built for practitioners who already know their domain and want to compound that knowledge with speed.

This first post draws that very line. The infographic maps two columns: tasks where AI is genuinely faster than you, and tasks where your experience, relationships, and judgement cannot be replicated. The professionals who combine both sides will define the next generation of supply chain leadership.

Map your last week to those two columns. Your ratio tells you exactly where your opportunity sits.

## Hook Options — All 10 (reference)
1. [Question-Why]: "Why do the best supply chain professionals spend 60% of their week on work a free AI tool could do in minutes?"
2. [Question-How]: "How do you figure out which parts of your job to hand to AI and which parts are the reason you got hired in the first place?"
3. [Stat-Lead]: "Roughly 60% of a typical planner's week is pattern detection, report drafting, and data categorisation. That is the 60% AI compresses. The other 40% is where careers are built."
4. [Contrarian]: "AI can decompose your demand signal in 30 seconds. It cannot convince your sales director to stop over-promising to key accounts. That distinction is your career insurance."
5. [Paradox]: "The supply chain professionals most worried about AI replacing them are spending most of their time on the work AI is best at. The ones least worried are spending their time on the work AI cannot touch."
6. [Personal-Reflection]: "I looked at my own week and mapped every task to two columns: things AI handles better than me, and things I handle better than AI. The split was uncomfortable. It was also the most useful career exercise I have done in years."
7. [Result-First]: "Pattern detection, bias identification, scenario modelling, document summarisation, exception categorisation. That is the left column. Supplier negotiation, crisis prioritisation, cross-functional trade-offs, reading the shop floor when data says one thing and reality says another. That is the right column. One column AI owns. The other column is yours."
8. [Timeline-Shock]: "Five years ago, writing a weekly variance report took a planner 3 hours every Monday morning. Today it takes 8 minutes. The 2 hours and 52 minutes you get back is the real question: what do you do with it?"
9. [Comparison-Gap]: "Two planners at the same company. One spends Monday building the forecast review. The other had AI build it Sunday evening and spent Monday morning preparing the root cause narrative for the S&OP meeting. Same role. Different value."
10. [Decision-Pressure]: "Audit your last week. What percentage of your time was data processing versus decision-making? If the answer is 80/20, you are sitting on a massive opportunity to rebalance."
