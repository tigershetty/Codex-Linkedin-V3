## APPROACH 1 — Tension Paragraph

*Writing mode: Journalism. Claim before explanation. No sections. Every sentence is a visual beat.*

IKEA built its global empire on one design insight: a flat-pack bookcase fits 10 times more units into a shipping container than an assembled one. That efficiency compounded into EUR 44.6 billion in annual sales, 1,800 suppliers, and 50 countries. The same design now makes it structurally impossible to manufacture competitively in America. IKEA spent USD 70 million on a factory in North Carolina. It produces 2 million KALLAX units a year. The equivalent Chinese facility produces 20 million. A 40% effective tariff cannot close a 10-times production scale gap. The moat and the trap are the same mechanism.

## APPROACH 2 — Scene Script

*Writing mode: Visual scene description. Spatial language. Describe what to see, not what the facts are.*

---

Task: Create an infographic image for the summary below (after the rules).

Rules: Use the image attached as a reference on style, aesthetics,
colours, and illustration technique. Use a different layout for the
structure to elaborate details based on the summary. Do not use any
information or text from the attached image — only style. Use it only
for inspiration. Aspect ratio 1:1, resolution 2048x2048.

Two shipping containers side by side: the left packed densely with flat-pack furniture — 2,200 units. The right holds assembled furniture — a fraction of the space, mostly air. This density gap is IKEA's entire competitive advantage. Now place two factories beneath that comparison: left, a clean automated plant in Mocksville, North Carolina — 2 million units a year. Right, a vast manufacturing complex in China — 20 million units a year, ten times the scale. Above the factories, a tariff meter climbing: 25% in October 2025, then 40% by February 2026. Inside the US factory: EUR 1.7 billion in profit with tariff costs absorbed. On the products leaving it: a sofa marked up USD 50, a bedroom set marked up USD 100. The container that built global dominance is the same container that makes the American factory impossible to scale. One design. Two consequences.

## APPROACH 3 — Paradox Declaration + Minimal Evidence

*Writing mode: Declaration first. Four discrete facts as separate units. No connective tissue.*

---

Task: Create an infographic image for the summary below (after the rules).

Rules: Use the image attached as a reference on style, aesthetics,
colours, and illustration technique. Use a different layout for the
structure to elaborate details based on the summary. Do not use any
information or text from the attached image — only style. Use it only
for inspiration. Aspect ratio 1:1, resolution 2048x2048.

IKEA's flat-pack design is its global competitive moat and the structural reason it cannot manufacture at scale in America. The same mechanism creates both.

One 40-foot container holds 2,200 BILLY bookcases flat-packed — 10 times more product than any assembled-furniture competitor can move.

IKEA's US factory: 2 million KALLAX units per year. The equivalent Chinese plant: 20 million or more.

85% of IKEA's US products are imported — the most import-exposed of any major IKEA market. Europe: 70% domestic. Asia: 80% domestic.

40% effective tariff rate. Operating profit compressed to EUR 1.7 billion. Sofas up USD 50. Bedroom sets up USD 100. Absorption ceiling reached.

## What to evaluate in each output

- **Layout structure**: Three horizontal bands? Hub and spoke? Asymmetric? Something else entirely?
- **Visual focal point**: Is there one element that dominates the canvas — or are all elements equal weight?
- **Text density**: Are text labels legible, or is the image trying to render too many words?
- **Text garbling**: Any corrupted words or numbers?
- **Narrative coherence**: Does the image tell one story, or does it feel like three separate panels stitched together?
- **Gemini invention**: Did Gemini add any visual metaphors or diagram types not explicitly described? (These are the best signal of a working prompt.)
