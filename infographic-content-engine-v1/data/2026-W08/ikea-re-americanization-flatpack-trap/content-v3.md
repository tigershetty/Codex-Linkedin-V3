## Narrative Summary — Variant A (Paradox-Led)

IKEA built EUR 44.6 billion in global sales on one design insight: flat-pack furniture ships 10 times more product per container than assembled rivals. The same design now makes domestic manufacturing in America structurally impossible at scale. The moat and the trap are the same mechanism.

### The Flat-Pack Efficiency Moat
- **2,200 units per container**: One 40-foot container holds 2,200 BILLY bookcases flat-packed — 10 times more product than any assembled-furniture competitor can move in the same space.
- **EUR 44.6B in global sales**: This container-efficiency insight compounded into 1,800 suppliers across 50 countries and 915 million annual store visits.
- **15% US domestic sourcing**: Europe sources 70% locally. Asia sources 80% locally. IKEA's US operation is its most import-dependent major market on the planet.

### Tariff Pressure Exposed the Trap
- **40% effective tariff rate**: 25% furniture tariff (Oct 14, 2025) + 15% Section 122 global surcharge (Feb 24, 2026) = 40% effective rate on imported sofas.
- **EUR 1.7B operating profit**: Inter IKEA Group absorbed the cost in FY25 — annual results explicitly cite "higher sourcing costs including tariff costs partly absorbed."
- **USD 50 / USD 100 price increases**: The absorption ceiling arrived in October 2025. Sofas up USD 50, bedroom sets up USD 100. CEO Jesper Brodin at Davos 2025: "there is a risk that you need to pass it on to the customers."

### The Scale Gap That Cannot Close
- **10x production gap**: USD 70 million US factory (Mocksville, NC) produces 2 million KALLAX units/year. Comparable Chinese facility: 20 million or more. The gap does not close at 40% effective tariff.
- **60–80% US labor premium**: US manufacturing costs run 60–80% higher than China; labor is 39% of China's structural price advantage. The domestic break-even only exists for bulky, low-efficiency categories.
- **Kitchen cabinets vs. KALLAX**: Kitchen cabinets — bulky, low flat-pack density — are now 100% US-sourced. KALLAX shelving — 2,200 units per container — is not. The threshold is product design, not tariff rate.
