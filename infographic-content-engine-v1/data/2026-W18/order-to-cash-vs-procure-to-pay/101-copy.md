## LinkedIn Caption

Supply chain leaders who can't talk in O2C and P2P language get treated as operators, not strategists. The decisions you make every day (lead times, inventory, supplier terms) directly drive the metric your CFO actually cares about: cash conversion.

This is Episode 22 of Supply Chain 101. In Episode 21 we covered risk management. Today we're connecting supply chain to the two financial cycles that determine how cash actually flows through the business, because every decision you make about lead times, inventory, and supplier terms is shaping these cycles whether you realise it or not.

- Order-to-Cash (O2C) is the cycle from the moment a customer places an order to the moment the cash hits your bank account. It includes order management, fulfilment, invoicing, and collection. The metric that summarises it is Days Sales Outstanding (DSO): on average, how many days does it take to collect payment after a sale? Lower is better.

- Procure-to-Pay (P2P) is the mirror image. It runs from the moment you identify a need to buy something, through purchasing, receipt, invoice approval, and payment to the supplier.  The metric is Days Payable Outstanding (DPO): on average, how many days do you take to pay after receiving goods? Higher (within reason) is better for cash flow.

The gap between these two, plus the time inventory sits in between, is the Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC). DSO + Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO) - DPO = CCC. It's the metric your CFO actually watches, and it's almost entirely shaped by supply chain decisions: how much inventory you hold, how fast you fulfil orders, what payment terms you negotiate.

Supply chain leaders who frame their work in CCC terms get heard in the boardroom. The same decisions, framed as service levels or fill rates, sound operational. Framed as working capital impact, they sound strategic.

Can you calculate your company's CCC?

## Hook Options — All 10 (reference)
1. [Question-Why]: "Why does your CFO care about supply chain decisions you thought were operational? Because every sourcing choice, lead time, and inventory level shows up in two financial cycles: how fast money comes in, and how slowly it goes out."
2. [Question-How]: "How does a decision to switch suppliers become a conversation about cash flow? Through two financial cycles most supply chain teams have heard of but rarely connect to their own decisions: Order-to-Cash and Procure-to-Pay."
3. [Stat-Lead]: "Order-to-Cash is how money comes in. Procure-to-Pay is how money goes out. Supply chain sits in the middle of both. If you don't understand these cycles, you cannot explain your impact to a CFO, and that gap is why supply chain often gets treated as a cost centre rather than a value driver."
4. [Contrarian]: "Supply chain leaders who can't talk in O2C and P2P language get treated as operators, not strategists. The decisions you make every day (lead times, inventory, supplier terms) directly drive the metric your CFO actually cares about: cash conversion. But most SC teams never frame it that way."
5. [Paradox]: "You shorten supplier payment terms to be a good partner. Your DPO drops. You extend customer credit terms to win business. Your DSO rises. Both feel like good decisions in isolation. Together, they squeeze cash out of the business and nobody connected it back to supply chain."
6. [Personal-Reflection]: "I've watched supply chain teams celebrate hitting service level targets while finance was quietly raising concerns about working capital. The two conversations rarely intersected. But every safety stock decision, every supplier negotiation, every inventory policy was shaping the cash conversion cycle that was keeping the CFO up at night."
7. [Result-First]: "A 5-day reduction in your cash conversion cycle can free up millions in working capital. That's not a finance project. It's a supply chain project disguised as one, and most companies miss it because the two functions speak different languages."
8. [Timeline-Shock]: "Day 0: a customer places an order. Day 30: you ship it. Day 60: they pay you. That's your DSO. Day 0: you place a PO. Day 45: you receive goods. Day 90: you pay the supplier. That's your DPO. The gap between paying out and getting paid is the cash conversion cycle. And it's almost entirely shaped by supply chain decisions."
9. [Comparison-Gap]: "O2C measures how fast you turn customer orders into collected cash. P2P measures how efficiently you turn supplier orders into paid invoices. One is how value flows in. The other is how cost flows out. Supply chain sits in the middle, and most SC dashboards measure neither."
10. [Decision-Pressure]: "Can you calculate your company's Cash Conversion Cycle? And do you know which supply chain decisions are pushing it up or pulling it down right now?"
