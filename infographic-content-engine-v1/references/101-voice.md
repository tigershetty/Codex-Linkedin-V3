## What This Series Is

Supply Chain 101 is a weekly series of single-image infographics that break down supply chain fundamentals. Each post teaches one concept clearly. The audience includes both practitioners and non-supply-chain professionals.

**Series identity**: "Supply Chain 101" — referenced in every post
**Hashtag**: #SupplyChain101 (always included alongside #ShettysDesk)
**Sign-off**: "Follow Poornajith Shetty for more supply chain insights" + a save prompt

---

## Voice Rules — How 101 Differs from Deep Dive

| | Deep Dive | Supply Chain 101 |
|---|---|---|
| Audience assumption | VP Supply Chain, COO, Directors — they know the field | Experts AND non-practitioners — assume no prior knowledge |
| Vocabulary | Industry-grade, technical terms used freely | Plain language first, technical term second with explanation |
| Numbers | Dense, embedded mid-sentence, citation-grade | Sparingly, only when they clarify the concept |
| Tone | Peer-to-peer authority | Teacher-to-learner clarity |
| Hook style | Data-led, case-study anchored | Metaphor-led, analogy-driven, accessible |
| Caption length | 200–350 words, dense | 150–300 words, breathing room |
| Bullets | Named firms + embedded numbers | Concept explanations + simple examples |
| Opinion | "My take:" — arguable position | Lighter reframe or practical insight |
| CTA | Names "your organisation" or "your supply chain" | Invites reflection, not audit |

---

## 101 Post Structure (7 parts — adapted)

1. **Hook** (1–2 sentences) — accessible, metaphor-led, stops a non-practitioner mid-scroll
2. **Series Frame** (1–2 sentences) — positions this post in the Supply Chain 101 series, references previous post if relevant
3. **Core Explanation** (2–3 sentences) — the concept in plain language, no jargon
4. **Three Bullets** — each explains one aspect of the concept with a simple example or analogy
5. **Reframe / Opinion** (1–2 sentences) — a practical insight or a "the real point is..." moment
6. **CTA Question** — single open question, invites reflection, accessible to non-practitioners
7. **Sign-off + Hashtags**
   - "Follow Poornajith Shetty for more supply chain insights and save this for [specific reference use]."
   - #ShettysDesk #SupplyChainIntelligence #SCM #SupplyChain101 + 1 topic-specific tag

**Word count**: 150–300 words. Keep it lighter than deep dive.

## Anchor Example — W11 Post (What Is a Supply Chain?)

```
6 links. 1 product. If any single one fails, it does not arrive.
That is a supply chain.

I'm starting a series called Supply Chain 101. A simple, yet
fundamental breakdown of supply chain building blocks and concepts
made easy to understand and follow.

Both for experts and non supply chain folks!

So, what is a Supply Chain?

A supply chain is not a single department or one organisation, it
is the sequence of steps, people, and companies that turns raw
material into something sitting on a shelf with a name on it.

• The Source and the Supplier: The first 2 links determine what the
  product is made of and whether there is enough of it. Every
  shortage, substitution, and quality failure traces back here.

• The Factory and the Warehouse: Links 3 and 4 are where raw
  material becomes product and where most of the cost is locked
  in before a single order is placed.

• The Delivery and the Customer: They are the moment where 5
  previous decisions either pay off or unravel at the final
  customer and how it is brought to them.

Which of these 6 links does your organisation understand the least?
And.. is that the same one that keeps producing surprises for your
supply chain?

---

Follow Poornajith Shetty for more supply chain insights and save
this frame for the next time someone asks you to explain what a
supply chain actually does.

#ShettysDesk #SupplyChainIntelligence #SCM #SupplyChain101
#Operations
```

### What to learn from this example:
- **Hook**: Simple. One number, one consequence, one definition. No jargon.
- **Series frame**: Names the series, explains the purpose, invites both audiences.
- **Core explanation**: One sentence definition using everyday language ("sitting on a shelf with a name on it").
- **Bullets**: Named with a label ("The Source and the Supplier"), followed by a plain-language explanation with a practical consequence.
- **CTA**: Open question, reflective, accessible to anyone.
- **Sign-off**: "Follow Poornajith Shetty" + save prompt with specific use case.

## What 101 Voice is NOT

```
NOT THIS (too technical):
"Supply chain management encompasses the planning and management
of all activities involved in sourcing, procurement, conversion,
and all logistics management activities."

NOT THIS (too corporate):
"Understanding the distinction between these operational paradigms
is essential for strategic alignment across the value chain."

NOT THIS (too casual):
"So basically supply chain is like, the big picture stuff and
logistics is the moving stuff around part lol"

NOT THIS (deep-dive tone in a 101 post):
"94% of companies report revenue damage from supply chain
disruptions. Boeing's $40B outsourcing failure proves that..."
```

**The test**: Could someone outside supply chain read this post over coffee and understand the concept? If yes, the voice is right.

## Hook Taxonomy — 101 Adaptations

The same 10 hook types from the deep-dive pipeline apply, but adapted for accessibility:

| # | Type | 101 Adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Question-Why | Use an everyday question, not an industry puzzle |
| 2 | Question-How | Frame around a familiar product or experience |
| 3 | Stat-Lead | Use a simple, surprising number — not a dense ratio |
| 4 | Contrarian | Challenge a common assumption non-practitioners hold |
| 5 | Paradox | Use a relatable contradiction, not a mechanism chain |
| 6 | Personal-Reflection | "I get asked this all the time..." framing |
| 7 | Result-First | Start with a visible outcome, then explain the concept |
| 8 | Timeline-Shock | Trace a product journey as a timeline |
| 9 | Comparison-Gap | Two things people think are the same, shown as different |
| 10 | Decision-Pressure | Frame as a question the reader can ask in their own workplace |
